Class 12 Physics • Chapter 6: Electromagnetic Induction

7. MUTUAL INDUCTION

1. Basic Concept of Mutual Induction

Definition: Mutual Induction is the phenomenon in which a time-varying electric current in one coil (the primary coil) induces an electromotive force (EMF) and current in a neighboring, physically separate coil (the secondary coil).

Two Coils Setup:

  • Primary Coil ($C_2$ or $P$): Connected to a variable current source (AC generator or DC battery with key & rheostat). Generates a time-dependent magnetic field $B_2(t)$.
  • Secondary Coil ($C_1$ or $S$): Connected to a sensitive load or Galvanometer $G$. Experiences the changing magnetic flux originating from the primary coil and develops an induced EMF $\varepsilon_1$.
Figure 7.1: Mutual induction principle between Primary Coil $C_2$ and Secondary Coil $C_1$.
$$\boxed{ I_2 \text{ (Primary Current)} \longrightarrow B_2 \text{ (Primary Field)} \longrightarrow \Phi_1 \text{ (Secondary Flux)} \longrightarrow \varepsilon_1 \text{ (Induced Secondary EMF)} }$$
1. Magnetic Coupling: The coils are magnetically coupled through space or a core; no physical wire connection is required.
2. Why Steady DC Fails: A steady DC current produces a constant magnetic field ($B_2 = \text{const}$). Hence, flux through secondary is constant ($\frac{d\Phi_1}{dt} = 0$). By Faraday's law, $\varepsilon_1 = 0$. Only changing current ($\frac{dI_2}{dt} \neq 0$) induces EMF.

2. Self-Induction vs Mutual Induction

Parameter Self-Induction Mutual Induction
Definition EMF induced in a single coil due to change of current in the same coil. EMF induced in a secondary coil due to change of current in a neighboring coil.
Coils Required 1 Coil. 2 Coils (Primary & Secondary).
Governing Formula $$N\Phi = L I \implies \varepsilon = -L \frac{dI}{dt}$$ $$N_1\Phi_1 = M I_2 \implies \varepsilon_1 = -M \frac{dI_2}{dt}$$
Role Self-opposing electrical inertia of a coil. Wireless magnetic coupling & energy transfer.

Terms: $N_1$ = secondary turns, $\Phi_1$ = flux per secondary turn, $N_1\Phi_1$ = total secondary flux linkage, $M$ = mutual inductance, $I_2$ = primary current, $\varepsilon_1$ = secondary induced EMF.

3. Definition of Mutual Inductance

Since field $B_2 \propto I_2$, flux through secondary $\Phi_1 \propto B_2 \propto I_2$, giving total flux linkage:

$$N_1\Phi_1 \propto I_2 \implies \boxed{N_1\Phi_1 = M_{12}I_2}$$
$$\boxed{M_{12} = \frac{N_1\Phi_1}{I_2}}$$
Definition: Mutual Inductance ($M$) is defined as the total magnetic flux linkage with the secondary coil per unit current flowing through the primary coil (or numerically equal to the induced secondary EMF when rate of change of primary current is $1\text{ A/s}$).

Why factor $N_1$ is present: Secondary has $N_1$ turns in series. Flux $\Phi_1$ passes through each turn, so total flux linked is $N_1 \times \Phi_1$.

4. Derivation of Induced EMF Due to Mutual Induction

⚡ Step-by-Step Board Derivation

1. Start with total flux linkage: $N_1\Phi_1 = M_{12}I_2$

2. Differentiate with respect to time $t$:

$$N_1\frac{d\Phi_1}{dt} = M_{12}\frac{dI_2}{dt}$$

3. By Faraday's Law, induced EMF is $\varepsilon_1 = -N_1\frac{d\Phi_1}{dt}$:

$$\boxed{\varepsilon_1 = -M_{12}\frac{dI_2}{dt}}$$

Negative Sign (Lenz's Law):

  • $I_2 \uparrow \implies \frac{dI_2}{dt} > 0 \implies \varepsilon_1$ opposes current growth.
  • $I_2 \downarrow \implies \frac{dI_2}{dt} < 0 \implies \varepsilon_1$ aids/maintains decreasing current.
  • $I_2 = \text{const} \implies \frac{dI_2}{dt} = 0 \implies \varepsilon_1 = 0$.

5. Board Derivation: Mutual Inductance of Two Long Coaxial Solenoids

🎓 Mandatory CBSE Board Derivation

Consider two long coaxial solenoids $S_1$ (inner, radius $r_1$, turns $N_1$) and $S_2$ (outer, radius $r_2$, turns $N_2$) of equal length $l$. ($r_1 < r_2$).

Coaxial: Both solenoids share the same central axis.

Figure 7.2: Coaxial solenoids $S_1$ (inner) and $S_2$ (outer) of length $l$.

Derivation Steps:

Step 1: Field by Outer Solenoid ($S_2$) carrying current $I_2$:

$$B_2 = \mu_0 n_2 I_2 = \mu_0 \left(\frac{N_2}{l}\right) I_2$$

Step 2: Flux through one turn of Inner Solenoid ($S_1$):

$$\Phi_1 = B_2 A_1 = (\mu_0 n_2 I_2)(\pi r_1^2)$$
Exam Reason: Why $A_1 = \pi r_1^2$, NOT $\pi r_2^2$?
Turns of inner solenoid $S_1$ only enclose area $\pi r_1^2$. Magnetic flux in the region between $r_1$ and $r_2$ does not pass through the turns of $S_1$.

Step 3: Total Flux Linkage with Inner Solenoid ($S_1$):

$$N_1\Phi_1 = N_1 B_2 A_1 = N_1 \left( \frac{\mu_0 N_2 I_2}{l} \right) A_1 = \left( \frac{\mu_0 N_1 N_2 A_1}{l} \right) I_2$$

Step 4: Comparing with $N_1\Phi_1 = M I_2$:

$$\boxed{M = \frac{\mu_0 N_1 N_2 A_1}{l} = \frac{\mu_0 N_1 N_2 \pi r_1^2}{l}}$$

Alternative Form (Turn Densities $n_1 = N_1/l, n_2 = N_2/l$):

$$\boxed{M = \mu_0 n_1 n_2 A_1 l}$$

Soft Iron Core ($\mu_r$): $M = \frac{\mu_0 \mu_r N_1 N_2 A_1}{l}$.

6. Mutual Inductance Reciprocity ($M_{12} = M_{21}$)

If current $I_1$ flows in inner solenoid $S_1$, field $B_1 = \mu_0 n_1 I_1$ exists over area $A_1 = \pi r_1^2$. Flux linked with outer solenoid $S_2$ ($N_2$ turns):

$$N_2\Phi_2 = N_2 (B_1 A_1) = N_2 \left(\frac{\mu_0 N_1 I_1}{l}\right) A_1 = \left(\frac{\mu_0 N_1 N_2 A_1}{l}\right) I_1$$ $$\implies \boxed{M_{12} = M_{21} = M}$$

7. Coupling Coefficient ($K$)

$$\boxed{K = \frac{M}{\sqrt{L_1 L_2}}} \quad \left(0 \le K \le 1, \, M^2 \le L_1 L_2\right)$$
  • $K = 1$: Perfect coupling (100% flux linkage, tight winding on soft iron core).
  • $0.7 \le K \le 0.9$: Strong coupling.
  • $K \to 0$: Weak coupling (separated or perpendicular coils).

8. Special Case: Concentric Co-Planar Coils ($r_1 \ll r_2$)

Current $I_2$ in outer coil $C_2$ produces field at center $B_2 = \frac{\mu_0 N_2 I_2}{2 r_2}$. Total flux linked with inner coil $C_1$ ($A_1 = \pi r_1^2$):

$$N_1\Phi_1 = N_1 B_2 A_1 = N_1 \left(\frac{\mu_0 N_2 I_2}{2 r_2}\right) (\pi r_1^2)$$
$$\boxed{M = \frac{\mu_0 N_1 N_2 \pi r_1^2}{2 r_2}} \quad \text{(For } N_1=N_2=1 \implies \boxed{M = \frac{\mu_0 \pi r_1^2}{2 r_2}}\text{)}$$

9. Switching ON and OFF Dynamics

  • Switch ON: $I_2$ rises $0 \to I_0 \implies \frac{dI_2}{dt} > 0 \implies$ transient induced EMF $\varepsilon_1$. Once steady ($I_2 = I_0$), $\frac{dI_2}{dt} = 0 \implies \varepsilon_1 = 0$.
  • Switch OFF: $I_2$ drops $I_0 \to 0 \implies \frac{dI_2}{dt} < 0 \implies$ sharp transient induced EMF aiding current.

10. Applications of Mutual Induction

  1. Transformer: Steps up/down AC voltage. Does NOT work on DC since $\frac{dI}{dt} = 0$.
  2. Wireless Inductive Charging: Changing magnetic field from charger pad induces current in device receiver coil.
  3. Ignition Coil: Rapid primary current break produces $20\text{ kV}$ spark in secondary.

11. SI Unit of Mutual Inductance

$$\boxed{1 \text{ Henry (H)} = 1 \text{ Wb A}^{-1} = 1 \text{ V s A}^{-1} = 1 \text{ }\Omega\text{ s}}$$

1 Henry: Mutual inductance when $1\text{ A/s}$ rate of change of primary current induces $1\text{ V}$ EMF in secondary.
