Chapter 6: Electromagnetic Induction — Class Notes

1. Magnetic Flux ($\Phi_B$)

Definition: The total number of magnetic field lines passing perpendicular through a surface of area $\vec{A}$ placed in magnetic field $\vec{B}$.

Figure 6.4 & 6.5: Magnetic flux through planar surface and area elements \(d\vec{A}_i\) in magnetic field.
Formula & Units $$\Phi_B = \vec{B} \cdot \vec{A} = BA \cos\theta = \int \vec{B} \cdot d\vec{A}$$
  • $\theta$: Angle between magnetic field $\vec{B}$ and the normal/Area Vector $\vec{A}$.
  • SI Unit: $\text{Weber } (\text{Wb}) = \text{T}\cdot\text{m}^2 = \text{V}\cdot\text{s}$. CGS Unit: $\text{Maxwell } (1\text{ Wb} = 10^8\text{ Mx})$.
  • Dimensions: $[\text{M}\text{L}^2\text{T}^{-2}\text{A}^{-1}]$ (Scalar quantity).
  • Special Cases: $\Phi_{\max} = +BA$ ($\theta = 0^\circ$), $\Phi = 0$ ($\theta = 90^\circ$, field parallel to surface), $\Phi_{\min} = -BA$ ($\theta = 180^\circ$).

2. Faraday's Experiments & Laws of EMI

Figure 6.1: Magnet-coil relative motion induces electric current.
Faraday's Laws of Electromagnetic Induction

1. First Law (Qualitative): Whenever the magnetic flux linked with a closed circuit changes with time, an electromotive force (EMF) is induced. The induced EMF persists only as long as the flux change continues.

2. Second Law (Quantitative): The magnitude of induced EMF is directly proportional to the time rate of change of magnetic flux linked with the circuit:

$$\epsilon = -N \frac{d\Phi_B}{dt}$$

(Negative sign indicates direction according to Lenz's Law).

Important Exam Relations: Current & Induced Charge
  • Induced Current: $I = \frac{|\epsilon|}{R} = \frac{N}{R} \left|\frac{d\Phi_B}{dt}\right|$
  • Total Induced Charge ($\Delta q$): $$\Delta q = I \Delta t = \frac{\Delta\Phi_{\text{net}}}{R_{\text{total}}}$$ Key Board Takeaway: Induced EMF and current depend on the rate of flux change (time-dependent), whereas total induced charge $\Delta q$ is completely independent of time.

3. Lenz's Law & Conservation of Energy

Statement: The polarity of the induced EMF is always such that it produces an electric current which opposes the change in magnetic flux producing it.

Figure 6.6: Direction of induced current opposing magnet motion.
End-Face Direction Rule & Energy Conservation Proof
  • Approaching N-pole: Face becomes North Pole (Anti-Clockwise Current $\circlearrowleft$) $\implies$ Repulsion.
  • Receding N-pole: Face becomes South Pole (Clockwise Current $\circlearrowright$) $\implies$ Attraction.
  • Approaching S-pole: Face becomes South Pole (Clockwise Current $\circlearrowright$) $\implies$ Repulsion.
  • Receding S-pole: Face becomes North Pole (Anti-Clockwise Current $\circlearrowleft$) $\implies$ Attraction.

Conservation of Energy: Mechanical work done against the opposing repulsive/attractive magnetic force is converted into electrical energy, which is ultimately dissipated as Joule heat ($I^2Rt$). If Lenz's law were not true, spontaneous acceleration would generate infinite energy without work, violating the First Law of Thermodynamics.

4. Motional Electromotive Force

A. Translational Motional EMF

A straight conducting rod of length $l$ sliding with velocity $v$ perpendicular to a uniform magnetic field $\vec{B}$ into the plane ($\otimes$).

Figure 6.10: Arm PQ sliding across U-shaped rails in uniform magnetic field.
Board Derivation: Translational Motional EMF

1. Flux Rule Method: Let loop area $A = lx$. Magnetic flux $\Phi_B = Blx$.

$$\epsilon = -\frac{d\Phi_B}{dt} = -Bl\left(\frac{dx}{dt}\right) = -Bl(-v) \implies \mathbf{\epsilon = Blv}$$

2. Lorentz Force Method: Magnetic force on free charge carrier $q$ is $F_m = qvB$. Work done across length $l$ is $W = F_m \cdot l = qvBl$.

$$\epsilon = \frac{W}{q} = \mathbf{Blv}$$
Power & Dynamics in Motional EMF
  • Induced Current: $I = \frac{\epsilon}{R} = \frac{Blv}{R}$
  • Opposing Magnetic Force: $F_{\text{mag}} = I l B = \frac{B^2 l^2 v}{R}$
  • Mechanical Power Supplied: $P_{\text{mech}} = F_{\text{ext}} \cdot v = \frac{B^2 l^2 v^2}{R}$
  • Joule Heat Dissipated: $P_{\text{Joule}} = I^2 R = \frac{B^2 l^2 v^2}{R} \implies \mathbf{P_{\text{mech}} = P_{\text{Joule}}}$

B. Rotational Motional EMF

A conducting rod of length $l$ (or disc of radius $R$) rotating with angular velocity $\omega$ (frequency $\nu$) in a perpendicular magnetic field $\vec{B}$.

Figure 6.11: Rotating metallic rod hinged at center touching circular metallic ring.
Board Derivation: Rotational Motional EMF

Consider small element $dr$ at distance $r$ from the axle. Speed $v = \omega r$. Elementary EMF:

$$d\epsilon = B v dr = B \omega r dr$$

Integrating from $r = 0$ to $r = l$:

$$\epsilon = \int_0^l B\omega r dr = B\omega \left[\frac{r^2}{2}\right]_0^l = \mathbf{\frac{1}{2} B \omega l^2} = \mathbf{B A \nu} \quad (\text{where } A = \pi l^2, \omega = 2\pi\nu)$$

5. Self-Inductance & Stored Magnetic Energy

Definition: Phenomenon by which an opposing EMF (Back EMF) is induced in a coil when the current flowing through the same coil changes with time.

$$N\Phi_B = L I \implies \epsilon = -L \frac{dI}{dt}$$

Where $L$ is the Coefficient of Self-Inductance (SI Unit: Henry, $\text{H}$).

Board Derivation: Self-Inductance of a Long Solenoid

For a solenoid of length $l$, area $A$, total turns $N = nl$ carrying current $I$:

  1. Internal magnetic field: $B = \mu_0 n I = \mu_0 \left(\frac{N}{l}\right) I$
  2. Flux per single turn: $\phi = BA = \mu_0 \left(\frac{N}{l}\right) I A$
  3. Total flux linkage: $N\Phi_B = N \phi = \frac{\mu_0 N^2 A}{l} I = (\mu_0 n^2 A l) I$
$$\mathbf{L = \frac{\mu_0 N^2 A}{l} = \mu_0 n^2 A l = \mu_r \mu_0 n^2 A l}$$
Magnetic Energy Stored & Energy Density
  • Work Done / Magnetic Energy: $$dW = |\epsilon| I dt = L I dI \implies \mathbf{U_B = \frac{1}{2} L I^2}$$
  • Magnetic Energy Density ($u_B$): $$u_B = \frac{U_B}{\text{Volume}} = \mathbf{\frac{B^2}{2\mu_0}} \quad (\text{J/m}^3)$$ (Analogous to electrostatic energy density $u_E = \frac{1}{2}\epsilon_0 E^2$).

6. Mutual Induction

Definition: Phenomenon in which an EMF is induced in a secondary coil due to time-varying current in a neighboring primary coil.

$$N_1 \Phi_1 = M_{12} I_2 \implies \epsilon_1 = -M_{12} \frac{dI_2}{dt}$$
Figure 6.12: Two long co-axial solenoids \(S_1\) and \(S_2\) of length \(l\).
Board Derivation: Mutual Inductance of Two Coaxial Solenoids

Consider two co-axial solenoids of length $l$ ($r_1 < r_2$):

  • Current $I_2$ in outer solenoid $S_2$: $B_2 = \mu_0 n_2 I_2$
  • Flux linked with inner solenoid $S_1$ (area $A_1 = \pi r_1^2$): $$N_1 \Phi_1 = (n_1 l)(\pi r_1^2)(\mu_0 n_2 I_2) = (\mu_0 n_1 n_2 \pi r_1^2 l) I_2$$
$$\mathbf{M_{12} = M_{21} = M = \mu_0 n_1 n_2 \pi r_1^2 l = \frac{\mu_0 N_1 N_2 A_1}{l}}$$

Coupling Coefficient: $K = \frac{M}{\sqrt{L_1 L_2}}$ ($0 \le K \le 1$).

7. AC Generator (Alternator)

Principle: Converts mechanical energy into electrical energy using electromagnetic induction by rotating a closed coil in a uniform magnetic field.

Figure 6.13: Schematic diagram of an AC Generator mechanism.
Figure 6.14: Stages of armature coil rotation and generated alternating EMF.
Board Derivation: Alternating EMF Equation

For a coil of $N$ turns, area $A$ rotating with angular speed $\omega$ about an axis perpendicular to field $\vec{B}$:

  1. Angle at time $t$: $\theta = \omega t$
  2. Flux linkage: $\Phi_{\text{total}} = N B A \cos(\omega t)$
  3. Induced EMF: $\epsilon = -\frac{d\Phi_{\text{total}}}{dt} = -NBA \frac{d}{dt}[\cos(\omega t)]$
$$\mathbf{\epsilon = NBA\omega \sin(\omega t) = \epsilon_0 \sin(\omega t)}$$

Where Peak EMF is $\mathbf{\epsilon_0 = NBA\omega = 2\pi \nu NBA}$.

Alternating Current: $\mathbf{I = I_0 \sin(\omega t)}$ where $I_0 = \frac{\epsilon_0}{R}$.
