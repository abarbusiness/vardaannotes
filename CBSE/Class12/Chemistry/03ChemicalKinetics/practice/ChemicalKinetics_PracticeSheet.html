Type 1: Rate of Reaction & Stoichiometry

Q1. Rate Expression CBSE 2019
For the reaction $2N_2O_5(g) \rightarrow 4NO_2(g) + O_2(g)$, the rate of formation of $NO_2(g)$ is $2.8 \times 10^{-3} \text{ M s}^{-1}$. Calculate the rate of disappearance of $N_2O_5(g)$.
Solution:

From the stoichiometry of the reaction, the rate of reaction is related as:

$$ -\frac{1}{2}\frac{d[N_2O_5]}{dt} = +\frac{1}{4}\frac{d[NO_2]}{dt} $$

We are given the rate of formation of $NO_2$:

$$ \frac{d[NO_2]}{dt} = 2.8 \times 10^{-3} \text{ M s}^{-1} $$

Rate of disappearance of $N_2O_5$ ($\frac{-d[N_2O_5]}{dt}$):

$$ -\frac{d[N_2O_5]}{dt} = \frac{2}{4} \times \left(2.8 \times 10^{-3}\right) = \frac{1}{2} \times 2.8 \times 10^{-3} $$

$$ = \mathbf{1.4 \times 10^{-3} \text{ M s}^{-1}} $$

Q2. Effect of Volume CBSE 2024
For a reaction $A + B \rightarrow P$, the rate law is given by $\text{Rate} = k[A][B]^2$. How is the rate of reaction affected if the volume of the reaction vessel is suddenly reduced to half of its original volume?
Solution:

Let the initial concentrations be $[A]$ and $[B]$. Initial rate $r_1 = k[A][B]^2$.

When the volume is reduced to half ($V' = V/2$), the concentration of all gases doubles since $C = n/V$.

New concentrations: $[A]' = 2[A]$, $[B]' = 2[B]$.

New rate $r_2 = k[A]'([B]')^2 = k(2[A])(2[B])^2 = 8k[A][B]^2$.

Therefore, $r_2 = 8 r_1$. The rate of the reaction increases to 8 times its original value.

Type 2: Rate Law & Order of Reaction

Q3. Initial Rate Method CBSE 2025
From the following data for the reaction $A + B \rightarrow \text{Products}$, find the order of reaction w.r.t A and B, and write the rate law.

Exp[A] (mol/L)[B] (mol/L)Initial Rate (M/s)
10.100.10$2.0 \times 10^{-3}$
20.100.20$4.0 \times 10^{-3}$
30.200.10$8.0 \times 10^{-3}$
Solution:

Let the rate law be $\text{Rate} = k[A]^x[B]^y$.

From Exp 1 and 2: Keeping $[A]$ constant (0.10), doubling $[B]$ (from 0.10 to 0.20) doubles the rate ($2.0 \times 10^{-3}$ to $4.0 \times 10^{-3}$).

$\frac{r_2}{r_1} = \frac{k(0.10)^x(0.20)^y}{k(0.10)^x(0.10)^y} \Rightarrow 2 = 2^y \Rightarrow \mathbf{y = 1}$

From Exp 1 and 3: Keeping $[B]$ constant (0.10), doubling $[A]$ (from 0.10 to 0.20) quadruples the rate ($2.0 \times 10^{-3}$ to $8.0 \times 10^{-3}$).

$\frac{r_3}{r_1} = \frac{k(0.20)^x(0.10)^y}{k(0.10)^x(0.10)^y} \Rightarrow 4 = 2^x \Rightarrow \mathbf{x = 2}$

Rate Law: $\text{Rate} = k[A]^2[B]^1$

Q4. Units of Rate Constant CBSE 2026
Identify the reaction order from each of the following rate constants:
(i) $k = 2.3 \times 10^{-5} \text{ L mol}^{-1} \text{ s}^{-1}$
(ii) $k = 3 \times 10^{-4} \text{ s}^{-1}$
(iii) $k = 4.5 \times 10^{-2} \text{ mol L}^{-1} \text{ s}^{-1}$
Solution:

The general formula for the unit of rate constant $k$ is: $(\text{mol L}^{-1})^{1-n} \text{ s}^{-1}$, where $n$ is the order of reaction.

(i) Unit is $\text{L mol}^{-1} \text{ s}^{-1}$.
$\Rightarrow (\text{mol L}^{-1})^{-1} \text{ s}^{-1} \Rightarrow 1 - n = -1 \Rightarrow \mathbf{n = 2}$ (Second Order)

(ii) Unit is $\text{s}^{-1}$.
$\Rightarrow (\text{mol L}^{-1})^{0} \text{ s}^{-1} \Rightarrow 1 - n = 0 \Rightarrow \mathbf{n = 1}$ (First Order)

(iii) Unit is $\text{mol L}^{-1} \text{ s}^{-1}$.
$\Rightarrow (\text{mol L}^{-1})^{1} \text{ s}^{-1} \Rightarrow 1 - n = 1 \Rightarrow \mathbf{n = 0}$ (Zero Order)

Type 3: First Order Kinetics

Q5. Direct Calculation CBSE 2024
The rate constant for a first order reaction is $60 \text{ s}^{-1}$. How much time will it take to reduce the initial concentration of the reactant to its $1/16^{th}$ value?
Solution:

For a first order reaction: $t = \frac{2.303}{k} \log \frac{[R]_0}{[R]}$

Given: $k = 60 \text{ s}^{-1}$, $[R] = \frac{[R]_0}{16}$.

$$ t = \frac{2.303}{60} \log \left( \frac{[R]_0}{[R]_0 / 16} \right) = \frac{2.303}{60} \log 16 $$

$$ \log 16 = \log (2^4) = 4 \log 2 = 4 \times 0.3010 = 1.204 $$

$$ t = \frac{2.303 \times 1.204}{60} = \frac{2.772}{60} = \mathbf{4.62 \times 10^{-2} \text{ s}} $$

Alternative approach: $1/16$ means 4 half-lives ($1/2 \rightarrow 1/4 \rightarrow 1/8 \rightarrow 1/16$).
$t_{1/2} = 0.693/60 = 0.01155 \text{ s}$. Total time = $4 \times 0.01155 = 4.62 \times 10^{-2} \text{ s}$.

Q6. Multi-Step Proof CBSE 2019, 2026
Show that in a first order reaction, time required for completion of 99.9% is 10 times of half-life ($t_{1/2}$) of the reaction.
Solution:

For 99.9% completion: $[R]_0 = 100$, $[R] = 100 - 99.9 = 0.1$

$$ t_{99.9\%} = \frac{2.303}{k} \log \frac{100}{0.1} = \frac{2.303}{k} \log 1000 $$

$$ t_{99.9\%} = \frac{2.303 \times 3}{k} = \frac{6.909}{k} \quad \dots \text{(i)} $$

For half-life ($t_{1/2}$):

$$ t_{1/2} = \frac{0.693}{k} \quad \dots \text{(ii)} $$

Dividing equation (i) by (ii):

$$ \frac{t_{99.9\%}}{t_{1/2}} = \frac{6.909/k}{0.693/k} \approx 10 $$

$$ \mathbf{t_{99.9\%} = 10 \times t_{1/2}} \quad \text{(Hence Proved)} $$

Type 4: Zero Order Kinetics & Half Life

Q7. Zero Order Graph & Calc CBSE 2025
A zero order reaction is 20% complete in 10 minutes. Calculate the time taken for 80% completion. Also state what the slope represents in the plot of $[R]$ vs $t$.
Solution:

For a zero-order reaction: $k = \frac{[R]_0 - [R]}{t}$. Also, $[R] = -kt + [R]_0$, so the slope of $[R]$ vs $t$ is **$-k$**.

Case 1 (20% complete): $[R]_0 = 100, [R] = 80, t = 10 \text{ min}$

$$ k = \frac{100 - 80}{10} = \frac{20}{10} = 2 \text{ mol L}^{-1}\text{min}^{-1} $$

Case 2 (80% complete): $[R]_0 = 100, [R] = 20, t = ?$

$$ 2 = \frac{100 - 20}{t} \Rightarrow 2 = \frac{80}{t} $$

$$ t = \frac{80}{2} = \mathbf{40 \text{ minutes}} $$

Q8. Radioactive Decay NCERT
The half-life for radioactive decay of $^{14}\text{C}$ is 5730 years. An archaeological artifact containing wood had only 80% of the $^{14}\text{C}$ found in a living tree. Estimate the age of the sample. ($\log 1.25 = 0.0969$)
Solution:

Radioactive decay follows first-order kinetics.

$$ k = \frac{0.693}{t_{1/2}} = \frac{0.693}{5730} \text{ yr}^{-1} $$

Given: $[R] = \frac{80}{100}[R]_0 = 0.8[R]_0$

$$ t = \frac{2.303}{k} \log \frac{[R]_0}{[R]} = \frac{2.303 \times 5730}{0.693} \log \frac{[R]_0}{0.8[R]_0} $$

$$ t = \frac{13196}{0.693} \log(1.25) $$

$$ t = 19041.8 \times 0.0969 = \mathbf{1845 \text{ years}} $$

Type 5: Arrhenius Equation & Activation Energy

Q9. Temperature Coefficient CBSE 2026
The rate constant of a reaction increases by 5% when its temperature is raised from 27°C to 28°C. Calculate the activation energy of the reaction. ($R = 8.314 \text{ J K}^{-1}\text{mol}^{-1}$)
Solution:

Given: $T_1 = 27^\circ\text{C} = 300\text{K}$, $T_2 = 28^\circ\text{C} = 301\text{K}$.

Rate constant increases by 5%, so $k_2 = k_1 + 0.05k_1 = 1.05k_1$. Therefore, $\frac{k_2}{k_1} = 1.05$.

$$ \log \frac{k_2}{k_1} = \frac{E_a}{2.303 R} \left[ \frac{T_2 - T_1}{T_1 T_2} \right] $$

$$ \log(1.05) = \frac{E_a}{2.303 \times 8.314} \left[ \frac{301 - 300}{300 \times 301} \right] $$

$$ 0.0212 = \frac{E_a}{19.147} \left[ \frac{1}{90300} \right] $$

$$ E_a = 0.0212 \times 19.147 \times 90300 = 36654.5 \text{ J mol}^{-1} $$

$$ \mathbf{E_a = 36.65 \text{ kJ mol}^{-1}} $$

Q10. Fraction of Molecules CBSE 2024
The activation energy for the reaction $2HI(g) \rightarrow H_2(g) + I_2(g)$ is $209.5 \text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$ at 581 K. Calculate the fraction of molecules of reactants having energy equal to or greater than activation energy. ($R = 8.314 \text{ J K}^{-1}\text{mol}^{-1}$)
Solution:

The fraction of molecules having energy equal to or greater than $E_a$ is given by $x = e^{-E_a / RT}$.

Taking natural logarithm: $\ln x = -\frac{E_a}{RT}$

Converting to base 10: $\log x = -\frac{E_a}{2.303 RT}$

Given $E_a = 209.5 \text{ kJ mol}^{-1} = 209500 \text{ J mol}^{-1}$, $T = 581 \text{ K}$.

$$ \log x = -\frac{209500}{2.303 \times 8.314 \times 581} = -\frac{209500}{11124.7} = -18.832 $$

$$ x = \text{Antilog} (-18.832) = \text{Antilog} (\overline{19}.168) $$

$$ \mathbf{x = 1.47 \times 10^{-19}} $$

Type 6: Pseudo First Order & Pressure Based

Q11. Ester Hydrolysis Concept CBSE 2024
Why is the hydrolysis of ethyl acetate in acidic medium a pseudo-first order reaction? State its rate law and what happens to the rate constant if the concentration of water is increased?
Solution:

The chemical reaction is:

$$ \text{CH}_3\text{COOC}_2\text{H}_5 + \text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow{\text{H}^+} \text{CH}_3\text{COOH} + \text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH} $$

The true rate law is: $\text{Rate} = k'[\text{CH}_3\text{COOC}_2\text{H}_5][\text{H}_2\text{O}]$.

Because water is present in such a large excess, its concentration remains almost constant throughout the reaction. Therefore, the term $k'[\text{H}_2\text{O}]$ is taken as a new constant $k$.

New Rate Law: $\text{Rate} = k[\text{CH}_3\text{COOC}_2\text{H}_5]$, behaving as first-order.

If the concentration of water is changed (increased), the observed rate constant $k$ will slightly change because $k = k'[\text{H}_2\text{O}]$, however, in practical aqueous solutions, water is always considered constant.

Q12. Pressure Based Kinetics CBSE 2025
For the first order thermal decomposition $SO_2Cl_2(g) \rightarrow SO_2(g) + Cl_2(g)$ at constant volume:
At $t = 0$, $P_{total} = 0.5 \text{ atm}$.
At $t = 100 \text{ s}$, $P_{total} = 0.6 \text{ atm}$.
Calculate the rate constant of the reaction. ($\log(1.25) = 0.0969$)
Solution:

Initial pressure of $SO_2Cl_2$ ($P_i$) = 0.5 atm.

At time $t$, pressure of $SO_2Cl_2$ = $P_i - p$
Pressure of products = $p$ and $p$.
Total pressure $P_t = (P_i - p) + p + p = P_i + p$
$\Rightarrow p = P_t - P_i$

Therefore, partial pressure of $SO_2Cl_2$ at time $t$, $P_A = P_i - p = P_i - (P_t - P_i) = 2P_i - P_t$

At $t = 100 \text{ s}$, $P_t = 0.6 \text{ atm}$:
$P_A = 2(0.5) - 0.6 = 0.4 \text{ atm}$

$$ k = \frac{2.303}{t} \log \frac{P_i}{P_A} = \frac{2.303}{100} \log \frac{0.5}{0.4} $$

$$ k = \frac{2.303}{100} \log(1.25) = \frac{2.303 \times 0.0969}{100} $$

$$ \mathbf{k = 2.23 \times 10^{-3} \text{ s}^{-1}} $$
