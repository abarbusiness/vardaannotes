Chapter 4: The d- and f-Block Elements (Class 12 Revision Notes)

1. Introduction & IUPAC Definition of Transition Elements

d-Block: Groups 3 to 12 where penultimate $(n-1)d$ subshells are progressively filled across $3d, 4d, 5d, 6d$ series. f-Block: Antepenultimate $(n-2)f$ subshells filled ($4f$ Lanthanoids, $5f$ Actinoids).

Position of d-Block (Groups 3-12) and f-Block in Periodic Table
IUPAC Definition & Group 12 Exception - Transition Metal Definition: An element having an incompletely (partially) filled d-subshell ($d^{1-9}$) in its ground state or any of its common oxidation states.
- Why Zn, Cd, Hg, Cn are NOT true transition metals? Zinc ($Zn$), Cadmium ($Cd$), Mercury ($Hg$), and Copernicium ($Cn$) have completely filled $d^{10}$ configurations in both ground state ($d^{10}s^2$) and common $+2$ state ($d^{10}$). They are non-typical transition elements.

2. Electronic Configurations & Anomalies

General outer configuration: $(n-1)d^{1-10} ns^{1-2}$.

Key Configurations & Exchange Energy - Anomalous Configurations:
  • Chromium ($Cr, Z=24$): $[Ar] 3d^5 4s^1$ (Half-filled $d^5$ stability)
  • Copper ($Cu, Z=29$): $[Ar] 3d^{10} 4s^1$ (Fully-filled $d^{10}$ stability)
  • Palladium ($Pd, Z=46$): $[Kr] 4d^{10} 5s^0$ *(Only element with $5s^0$!)*
- Exchange Energy ($K = \frac{n(n-1)}{2}$): Electrons with parallel spins exchange positions, releasing stabilization energy. Exchange pairs are maximum at half-filled ($3d^5 \rightarrow 10 \text{ pairs}$) and fully-filled ($3d^{10}$) states.
- 4s Orbital Rule: $4s$ is filled BEFORE $3d$ (Aufbau rule in neutral atom), but $4s$ electrons are lost FIRST during ionization ($Fe \rightarrow Fe^{2+} + 2e^-$ removes $4s^2$).
Exchange Energy Pairs: 3d5 (10 Pairs) vs 3d4 (6 Pairs)

3. Physical Properties & Periodic Trends

3.1 Melting Points & Enthalpies of Atomisation

Transition metals have very high melting points and atomisation enthalpies due to metallic bonding plus covalent-like d-d orbital overlaps.

NCERT Fig 4.1 & 4.2: Melting Points & Enthalpies of Atomisation Trends
Melting Point Anomalies - Peak at $d^5$ (Middle of Series): $Cr, Mo, W$ show highest melting points due to maximum unpaired d-electrons.
- Anomalous Dip at Mn ($3d^5$) & Tc ($4d^5$): Exceptionally stable $d^5$ configuration binds electrons tightly to individual nuclei $\rightarrow$ less effective participation in metallic lattice bonding $\rightarrow$ lower melting points.
- Lowest Atomisation Enthalpy (Zinc, $126 \text{ kJ/mol}$): Fully filled $3d^{10}$ subshell has no unpaired d-electrons for interatomic bonding.

3.2 Atomic Radii Trends & Lanthanoid Contraction

NCERT Fig 4.3: Atomic Radii Trends across 3d, 4d, and 5d Series
The Tug-of-War: Atomic Radius along a 3d Period 1. Sc to Cr (Decreases): Nuclear charge ($Z_{eff}$) increases faster than weak d-electron shielding.
2. Mn to Ni (Nearly Constant): Increasing d-electron screening balances increasing nuclear charge.
3. Cu to Zn (Slight Increase): Paired $d^{10}$ electron-electron repulsions overcome nuclear attraction.
Shielding Effectiveness: s > p > d > f (f-orbitals shield poorest)
Lanthanoid Contraction & Identical 4d/5d Radii - Cause: Before $5d$ series, 14 lanthanoid elements ($4f^{14}$) are filled. Highly diffused $4f$ electrons exert extremely poor shielding $\rightarrow Z_{eff}$ increases sharply by $+14 \rightarrow$ outer electrons pulled strongly inward.
- Consequence: $4d$ and $5d$ element pairs in the same group have nearly IDENTICAL atomic radii!
Examples: $Zr (4d) = 160 \text{ pm}$ vs $Hf (5d) = 159 \text{ pm}$; $Nb (4d) = 146 \text{ pm}$ vs $Ta (5d) = 146 \text{ pm}$.

3.3 Ionisation Enthalpy Anomalies

  • Second IE ($\Delta_i H_2^\circ$): High for $Cr$ ($3d^5 \rightarrow 3d^4$) and $Cu$ ($3d^{10} \rightarrow 3d^9$) because removing 2nd electron disrupts stable half/fully-filled d-orbitals.
  • Third IE ($\Delta_i H_3^\circ$): High for $Mn^{2+}$ ($3d^5 \rightarrow 3d^4$) and $Zn^{2+}$ ($3d^{10} \rightarrow 3d^9$). Low for $Fe^{2+}$ ($3d^6 \rightarrow 3d^5$ stable).

4. Oxidation States & Electrode Potentials ($E^\circ$)

NCERT Key Oxidation State Rules 1. Variability: States differ by unity (+1) in d-block (e.g. $V^{II}, V^{III}, V^{IV}, V^{V}$) vs two units (+2) in p-block due to inert pair effect.
2. Scandium: Shows ONLY $+3$ state ($Sc^{3+}: 3d^0$). Manganese: Maximum $+7$ state ($MnO_4^-$).
3. Heavier d-Block Members Favor HIGHER States: $Mo(VI)$ and $W(VI)$ are stable, whereas $Cr(VI)$ in $Cr_2O_7^{2-}$ is strongly oxidising.

4.1 Standard Electrode Potentials ($E^\circ$) & Copper Anomaly

$$\mathbf{\Delta H_{total} = \Delta_a H^\circ \text{ (Sublimation)} + IE_1 + IE_2 + \Delta_{hyd}H^\circ \text{ (Hydration)}}$$

NCERT Fig 4.4: Standard Electrode Potential E°(M2+/M) for 3d Series
Important E° Anomalies to Remember - Copper Anomaly ($E^\circ = +0.34 \text{ V}$): Copper is the ONLY 3d metal with a positive $E^\circ(M^{2+}/M)$ value $\rightarrow$ cannot liberate $H_2$ gas from dilute acids! High $\Delta_a H^\circ + IE_1+IE_2$ is NOT balanced by hydration enthalpy.
- $Cr^{2+}$ Reducing vs $Mn^{3+}$ Oxidising ($d^4$ Config):
  • $Cr^{2+} \rightarrow Cr^{3+} + e^-$ ($d^4 \rightarrow d^3$): In water, $d^3$ represents a half-filled, extra stable $t_{2g}^3$ crystal field state.
  • $Mn^{3+} + e^- \rightarrow Mn^{2+}$ ($d^4 \rightarrow d^5$): Reverts to extra stable half-filled $d^5$ subshell.
Halides & Oxides Anomalies - Copper Iodide Anomaly: $Cu^{2+}$ oxidises $I^-$ to $I_2$: $\mathbf{2Cu^{2+} + 4I^- \rightarrow Cu_2I_2(s) + I_2}$. Hence $CuI_2$ does NOT exist.
- Disproportionation of $Cu^+$ in Water: $\mathbf{2Cu^+(aq) \rightarrow Cu^{2+}(aq) + Cu(s)}$. Hydration enthalpy of $Cu^{2+}$ is far more negative than $Cu^+$, compensating for $IE_2$.

5. Important Characteristics & Mechanisms

Magnetic Moment & d-d Transition Color - Spin-Only Magnetic Formula: $\mathbf{\mu = \sqrt{n(n+2)} \text{ BM}}$ ($n$ = unpaired $e^-$).
Values: $n=1 \rightarrow 1.73 \text{ BM}$; $n=2 \rightarrow 2.84 \text{ BM}$; $n=3 \rightarrow 3.87 \text{ BM}$; $n=4 \rightarrow 4.90 \text{ BM}$; $n=5 \rightarrow 5.92 \text{ BM}$.
- Color Mechanism: Unpaired d-electrons undergo d-d transition ($t_{2g} \rightarrow e_g$) by absorbing visible light. Ions with $d^0$ ($Sc^{3+}, Ti^{4+}$) or $d^{10}$ ($Cu^+, Zn^{2+}$) are colorless. $MnO_4^-$ purple color is due to LMCT (Charge Transfer).
Crystal Field Splitting & d-d Transition Mechanism
Catalysts, Interstitial Compounds & Alloys - Catalysts: $V_2O_5$ (Contact process for $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$), Finely divided $Fe$ (Haber process for $NH_3$), $Ni$ (Hydrogenation), $TiCl_4+Al(CH_3)_3$ (Ziegler-Natta catalyst).
- Interstitial Compounds: Small non-metal atoms ($H, C, N$) trapped in lattice voids ($TiC, Fe_3H$). High melting points, extremely hard, retain metallic conductivity, chemically inert.
- Alloys: Atomic radii within 15% allow easy substitution in lattice.
