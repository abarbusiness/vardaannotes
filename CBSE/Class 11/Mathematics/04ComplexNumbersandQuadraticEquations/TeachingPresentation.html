"Why does $\sqrt{-1}$ exist? Because equations demanded it."

Vardaan Learning Institute Topic 1 — The Imaginary Unit 04
Concept Why Do We Need Complex Numbers?
The Problem

In $\mathbb{R}$: $x^2 = -1$ has no real solution because for all $x \in \mathbb{R}$:

$$x^2 \geq 0$$

So the equation $x^2 + 1 = 0$ cannot be solved in $\mathbb{R}$.

The Solution — Introducing $i$

We define a new symbol $i$ (iota) such that:

$$\boxed{i = \sqrt{-1} \qquad \Longleftrightarrow \qquad i^2 = -1}$$
Consequence

For any $a > 0$:   $\sqrt{-a} = \sqrt{a \times (-1)} = \sqrt{a} \cdot \sqrt{-1} = i\sqrt{a}$

Examples:   $\sqrt{-4} = 2i$    $\sqrt{-9} = 3i$    $\sqrt{-7} = i\sqrt{7}$

Notes / Examples
Vardaan Learning Institute Topic 1 — Important Caution 05
Caution Critical Rule: Multiplication of Negative Square Roots
⚠ NCERT Special Note — Very Frequently Tested!

$\sqrt{a} \times \sqrt{b} = \sqrt{ab}$ holds ONLY when both $a \geq 0$ and $b \geq 0$

When $a < 0$ or $b < 0$, you must FIRST convert to $i$ form.

❌ WRONG Approach
$$\sqrt{-2} \times \sqrt{-3} = \sqrt{(-2)(-3)} = \sqrt{6}$$

This is INCORRECT! ❌

✔ CORRECT Approach
$$\sqrt{-2} \times \sqrt{-3} = (i\sqrt{2})(i\sqrt{3}) = i^2\sqrt{6} = -\sqrt{6}$$

This is CORRECT! ✔

Vardaan Learning Institute Topic 2 — Standard Form 09
Concept Complex Number: Standard Form $z = a + ib$
Definition (NCERT)

A number $z = a + ib$ where $a, b \in \mathbb{R}$ and $i = \sqrt{-1}$ is called a Complex Number.

  • Real part: $\text{Re}(z) = a$
  • Imaginary part: $\text{Im}(z) = b$  (NOT $ib$!)
ConditionTypeExample
$b = 0$Purely Real$5 + 0i = 5$
$a=0,\ b\neq0$Purely Imaginary$3i$
$a=0,\ b=0$Zero$0$
$a\neq0,\ b\neq0$Proper Complex$3+4i$
Equality Rule

$a+ib = c+id \iff a=c$ AND $b=d$

Complex numbers CANNOT be ordered ($z_1 > z_2$ has no meaning)

Number System Hierarchy

$$\mathbb{N} \subset \mathbb{Z} \subset \mathbb{Q} \subset \mathbb{R} \subset \mathbb{C}$$

Algebra of Complex Numbers

Addition • Subtraction • Multiplication • Division

Vardaan Learning Institute Topic 3 — Algebra of Complex Numbers 14
Concept Addition, Subtraction & Multiplication
Addition
$$(a+ib)+(c+id) = (a+c)+i(b+d)$$

Properties: Closure, Commutative, Associative, Identity ($0+0i$), Inverse ($-a-ib$)

Subtraction
$$(a+ib)-(c+id) = (a-c)+i(b-d)$$
Multiplication
$$(a+ib)(c+id) = (ac-bd)+i(ad+bc)$$

Expand like FOIL, then replace $i^2 = -1$

Properties: Closure, Commutative, Associative, Distributive, Identity ($1+0i$)

Worked Examples
Vardaan Learning Institute Topic 3 — Division 15
Concept Division of Complex Numbers — Rationalise the Denominator
Method: Multiply by Conjugate of Denominator
$$\frac{a+ib}{c+id} = \frac{(a+ib)(c-id)}{(c+id)(c-id)} = \frac{(ac+bd)+i(bc-ad)}{c^2+d^2}$$

The denominator $(c+id)(c-id) = c^2+d^2$ is always a real positive number.

Multiplicative Inverse (Reciprocal)
$$z^{-1} = \frac{1}{z} = \frac{\bar{z}}{|z|^2} = \frac{a-ib}{a^2+b^2}$$

Vardaan Learning Institute Topic 4 — Powers of $i$ 21
Concept Powers of $i$ — The Cyclic Pattern (Period = 4)
PowerValuePowerValue
$i^1$$i$$i^{-1}$$-i$
$i^2$$-1$$i^{-2}$$-1$
$i^3$$-i$$i^{-3}$$i$
$i^4$$1$$i^{-4}$$1$
$i^5$$i$$i^{4k}$$1$

General Rule: Find $n \bmod 4$, then:

$r=0\Rightarrow i^n=1$   $r=1\Rightarrow i^n=i$   $r=2\Rightarrow i^n=-1$   $r=3\Rightarrow i^n=-i$

Key Facts

$\dfrac{1}{i} = -i$     $i^{4k}=1$ for any integer $k$

Sum of any 4 consecutive powers of $i$ = $0$

of a Complex Number

Vardaan Learning Institute Topic 5 — Modulus 25
Concept Modulus of a Complex Number $|z|$
$$|z| = \sqrt{a^2+b^2} \geq 0 \quad \text{for } z = a+ib$$
Key Properties of Modulus
  • $|z|=0 \iff z=0$
  • $z\bar{z} = |z|^2$   (Most important!)
  • $|z_1 z_2| = |z_1|\cdot|z_2|$
  • $\left|\dfrac{z_1}{z_2}\right|=\dfrac{|z_1|}{|z_2|}$
  • $|\text{Re}(z)|\leq|z|$   and   $|\text{Im}(z)|\leq|z|$
Triangle Inequalities

$|z_1+z_2| \leq |z_1|+|z_2|$   (Triangle Inequality)

$\big||z_1|-|z_2|\big| \leq |z_1-z_2|$   (Reverse)

$|z| = \text{distance from Origin } O \text{ to } P(a, b) = \sqrt{a^2+b^2}$
Notes
Vardaan Learning Institute Topic 5 — Conjugate 26
Concept Conjugate of a Complex Number $\bar{z}$
$$\text{If } z = a+ib, \quad \text{then} \quad \bar{z} = a-ib$$
Key Properties of Conjugate
  • $\overline{(\bar{z})} = z$
  • $z+\bar{z} = 2\,\text{Re}(z)$   (purely real)
  • $z-\bar{z} = 2i\,\text{Im}(z)$   (purely imaginary)
  • $z\bar{z} = |z|^2$   ★ Most used identity!
  • $\overline{z_1+z_2} = \bar{z}_1+\bar{z}_2$    $\overline{z_1 z_2} = \bar{z}_1\bar{z}_2$
  • $z$ is real $\iff z = \bar{z}$
  • $z$ is purely imaginary $\iff z+\bar{z}=0$
$$z^{-1} = \frac{\bar{z}}{|z|^2} \qquad \text{Re}(z)=\frac{z+\bar{z}}{2} \qquad \text{Im}(z)=\frac{z-\bar{z}}{2i}$$
$Q(a,-b) = \bar{z}$ is the mirror image of $P(a,b) = z$ in the Real Axis
Geometric meaning & Examples

Vardaan Learning Institute Topic 6 — Argand Plane 31
Concept The Argand Plane (Complex Plane)
Definition
  • $z=a+ib \longleftrightarrow$ point $(a,b)$
  • Real Axis: X-axis (purely real numbers)
  • Imaginary Axis: Y-axis (purely imaginary numbers)
  • This plane is the Argand Plane or Complex Plane
Geometric Interpretations
  • Addition: Parallelogram Law
  • $|z|$: Length of $\overrightarrow{OP}$ from origin
  • $|z_1-z_2|$: Distance between $P_1$ and $P_2$
  • $\bar{z}$: Reflection in Real Axis
  • $-z$: Reflection through Origin
  • $iz$: Rotation by $90°$ anticlockwise
Signs of $(\text{Re}(z), \text{Im}(z))$ uniquely locate $z$ in the 4 Quadrants

Vardaan Learning Institute Topic 7 — Polar Form 35
Concept Polar Form of a Complex Number
$$z = r(\cos\theta + i\sin\theta)$$

where $r = |z| = \sqrt{a^2+b^2}$ and $\theta = \arg(z)$

Relations

$a = r\cos\theta$    $b = r\sin\theta$    $\tan\theta = \dfrac{b}{a}$ (careful about quadrant!)

Principal Argument $\theta \in (-\pi, \pi]$
Quadrant$\alpha = \tan^{-1}|b/a|$$\theta$
I ($a>0, b>0$)$\alpha$$\alpha$
II ($a<0, b>0$)$\alpha$$\pi-\alpha$
III ($a<0, b<0$)$\alpha$$\alpha-\pi$
IV ($a>0, b<0$)$\alpha$$-\alpha$
Argument Properties

$\arg(z_1z_2) = \arg z_1+\arg z_2$    $\arg\!\left(\dfrac{z_1}{z_2}\right)=\arg z_1-\arg z_2$

$z = r(\cos\theta + i\sin\theta)$ with $r=|z|$ and $\theta=\arg(z)$

Finding $\sqrt{a+ib}$ using the $x+iy$ method

Vardaan Learning Institute Topic 8 — Square Roots 39
Concept Square Root of a Complex Number $\sqrt{a+ib}$
Method: Let $\sqrt{a+ib} = x+iy$

Squaring: $(x+iy)^2 = a+ib$

Gives two equations:

(i)   $x^2 - y^2 = a$     (ii)   $2xy = b$

Taking moduli:   $x^2+y^2 = \sqrt{a^2+b^2}$   (iii)

$$x^2 = \frac{\sqrt{a^2+b^2}+a}{2} \qquad y^2 = \frac{\sqrt{a^2+b^2}-a}{2}$$

Sign of $y$ = Sign of $b$ (since $2xy=b$)

Special Cases

$\sqrt{i} = \pm\dfrac{1+i}{\sqrt{2}}$      $\sqrt{-i} = \pm\dfrac{1-i}{\sqrt{2}}$

Example: $\sqrt{-5+12i}$

with Complex Roots

When $D = b^2-4ac < 0$, roots are complex conjugates

Vardaan Learning Institute Topic 9 — Quadratic Equations 42
Concept Quadratic Equations — Nature of Roots & Formula
Discriminant $D = b^2 - 4ac$
  • $D > 0$ → Two distinct real roots
  • $D = 0$ → Two equal real roots (repeated)
  • $D < 0$ → Two complex conjugate roots
$$x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}$$

When $D < 0$: $\sqrt{D} = i\sqrt{|D|}$, so $x = \dfrac{-b \pm i\sqrt{|D|}}{2a}$

Vieta's Formulas (Sum & Product of Roots)

If $\alpha, \beta$ are roots of $ax^2+bx+c=0$:

$$\alpha+\beta = -\frac{b}{a} \qquad \alpha\beta = \frac{c}{a}$$

$1 + \omega + \omega^2 = 0$  —  One of the most beautiful results in mathematics

Vardaan Learning Institute Topic 10 — Cube Roots of Unity 49
Concept Cube Roots of Unity — The Magic of $\omega$
Three Cube Roots of $x^3=1$
$$1, \quad \omega = \frac{-1+i\sqrt{3}}{2}, \quad \omega^2 = \frac{-1-i\sqrt{3}}{2}$$

$\omega$ is a root of $x^2+x+1=0$   (since $x^3-1=(x-1)(x^2+x+1)$)

★ Must-Memorize Properties:

$$\omega^3 = 1 \qquad 1+\omega+\omega^2 = 0 \qquad \bar{\omega} = \omega^2$$
Further Properties
  • $|\omega|=|\omega^2|=1$
  • $1+\omega^n+\omega^{2n}=3$ if $3|n$; else $=0$
  • $(1+\omega)=-\omega^2$    $(1+\omega^2)=-\omega$
  • $\omega^{3k}=1$, $\omega^{3k+1}=\omega$, $\omega^{3k+2}=\omega^2$
