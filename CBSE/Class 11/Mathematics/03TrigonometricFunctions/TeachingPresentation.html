Practice Q1 Degree to Radian Conversion
Question 01 NCERT CBSE Core

Convert $-47^\circ 30'$ into radian measure.

Vardaan Learning Institute Topic 01 — Solution 06
Solution Q1 Degree to Radian Conversion
Step-by-Step Conversion

Step 1: Convert minutes into fractional degrees:

     $30' = \left(\dfrac{30}{60}\right)^\circ = \dfrac{1}{2}^\circ$.

Step 2: Express total magnitude in fractional degrees:

     $-47^\circ 30' = -\left(47 + \dfrac{1}{2}\right)^\circ = -\left(\dfrac{95}{2}\right)^\circ$.

Step 3: Multiply by the conversion factor $\dfrac{\pi}{180}$:

     $\text{Radians} = -\dfrac{95}{2} \times \dfrac{\pi}{180} = -\dfrac{19\pi}{2 \times 36} = \mathbf{-\dfrac{19\pi}{72}\text{ radians}}$.

Result:   $-47^\circ 30' = -\dfrac{19\pi}{72}\text{ radians}$

Practice Q2 Revolutions & Angular Velocity
Question 02 RD Sharma NCERT

A wheel makes $360$ revolutions in one minute. Through how many radians does it turn in one second?

Vardaan Learning Institute Topic 01 — Solution 08
Solution Q2 Angular Velocity & Total Radians
Calculation

Step 1: Calculate revolutions per second:

     Number of revolutions in $60\text{ seconds} = 360$.

     Number of revolutions in $1\text{ second} = \dfrac{360}{60} = \mathbf{6\text{ revolutions}}$.

Step 2: Calculate radians turned:

     In $1$ complete revolution, a wheel turns through an angle of $2\pi\text{ radians}$.

     In $6$ revolutions, total angle turned in $1\text{ second} = 6 \times 2\pi = \mathbf{12\pi\text{ radians}}$.

Result:   $\theta = 12\pi\text{ radians in one second}$

Practice Q3 Central Angle from Arc & Radius
Question 03 NCERT RS Aggarwal

Find the degree measure of the angle subtended at the centre of a circle of radius $100\text{ cm}$ by an arc of length $22\text{ cm}$ (Use $\pi = \dfrac{22}{7}$).

Vardaan Learning Institute Topic 01 — Solution 10
Solution Q3 Central Angle Calculation
Arc Formula & Degree Conversion

Step 1: Compute angle in radians:

     Given $r = 100\text{ cm}$, $l = 22\text{ cm}$.

     $\theta = \dfrac{l}{r} = \dfrac{22}{100} = \dfrac{11}{50}\text{ radians}$.

Step 2: Convert radians to degrees:

     $\theta = \dfrac{11}{50} \times \dfrac{180}{\pi} = \dfrac{11}{50} \times \dfrac{180 \times 7}{22} = \dfrac{126}{10}^\circ = 12.6^\circ$.

Step 3: Convert fractional degrees to minutes:

     $0.6^\circ = (0.6 \times 60)' = 36'$.   Thus: $\mathbf{\theta = 12^\circ 36'}$.

Result:   $\theta = 12^\circ 36'$

Vardaan Learning Institute Topic 02 — Concept 12
Concept The Unit Circle Definition & ASTC Quadrant Rule
Point on Unit Circle $x^2 + y^2 = 1$

• Abscissa (x): $\cos t = x$.   Ordinate (y): $\sin t = y$.

• Fundamental Identity: $\cos^2 t + \sin^2 t = x^2 + y^2 = 1$.

• $\tan t = \dfrac{y}{x}$, $\cot t = \dfrac{x}{y}$, $\sec t = \dfrac{1}{x}$, $\csc t = \dfrac{1}{y}$.

ASTC Rule ("All Sugar To Coffee")

• Quadrant I ($0$ to $\pi/2$): All trig functions are positive (A).

• Quadrant II ($\pi/2$ to $\pi$): $\sin$ and $\csc$ are positive (S).

• Quadrant III ($\pi$ to $3\pi/2$): $\tan$ and $\cot$ are positive (T).

• Quadrant IV ($3\pi/2$ to $2\pi$): $\cos$ and $\sec$ are positive (C).

Practice Q4 Finding Other Five Trig Ratios
Question 04 NCERT CBSE 2022

If $\cos x = -\dfrac{3}{5}$, and $x$ lies in the third quadrant, find the values of other five trigonometric functions.

Vardaan Learning Institute Topic 02 — Solution 15
Solution Q4 Trig Ratios in Quadrant III
Evaluation using Pythagorean Identities

Step 1: Check quadrant signs:

     In Quadrant III, $\tan x > 0$ and $\cot x > 0$, while $\sin x, \cos x, \sec x, \csc x < 0$.

Step 2: Find $\sin x$:

     $\sin^2 x = 1 - \cos^2 x = 1 - \left(-\dfrac{3}{5}\right)^2 = \dfrac{16}{25} \implies \sin x = -\sqrt{\dfrac{16}{25}} = \mathbf{-\dfrac{4}{5}}$.

Step 3: Find $\tan x$ and $\cot x$:

     $\tan x = \dfrac{-4/5}{-3/5} = \mathbf{\dfrac{4}{3}}$,   $\cot x = \dfrac{1}{\tan x} = \mathbf{\dfrac{3}{4}}$.

Step 4: Find reciprocals:

     $\sec x = \mathbf{-\dfrac{5}{3}}$,   $\csc x = \mathbf{-\dfrac{5}{4}}$.

$\sin x = -\frac{4}{5}$,   $\tan x = \frac{4}{3}$,   $\cot x = \frac{3}{4}$,   $\sec x = -\frac{5}{3}$,   $\csc x = -\frac{5}{4}$

Practice Q5 Trig Values of Large Negative Angles
Question 05 NCERT RD Sharma

Find the exact value of:   (i) $\sin\left(-\dfrac{11\pi}{3}\right)$   (ii) $\cot\left(-\dfrac{15\pi}{4}\right)$.

Vardaan Learning Institute Topic 02 — Solution 17
Solution Q5 Periodicity & Negative Angle Identities
Simplification

(i) $\sin\left(-\dfrac{11\pi}{3}\right)$ :

     Use $\sin(-\theta) = -\sin\theta$:

     $\sin\left(-\dfrac{11\pi}{3}\right) = -\sin\left(\dfrac{11\pi}{3}\right) = -\sin\left(4\pi - \dfrac{\pi}{3}\right)$.

     Since $4\pi - \dfrac{\pi}{3}$ is in Quadrant IV where sine is negative:

     $-\left(-\sin\dfrac{\pi}{3}\right) = \sin\dfrac{\pi}{3} = \mathbf{\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}}$.

(ii) $\cot\left(-\dfrac{15\pi}{4}\right)$ :

     Use $\cot(-\theta) = -\cot\theta$:

     $\cot\left(-\dfrac{15\pi}{4}\right) = -\cot\left(\dfrac{15\pi}{4}\right) = -\cot\left(4\pi - \dfrac{\pi}{4}\right) = -\left(-\cot\dfrac{\pi}{4}\right) = \cot\dfrac{\pi}{4} = \mathbf{1}$.

Answers:   (i) $\mathbf{\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}}$   (ii) $\mathbf{1}$

Practice Q6 Allied Angles Simplification Proof
Question 06 NCERT CBSE 2021

Prove that:   $\dfrac{\cos(\pi+\theta)\cos(-\theta)}{\sin(\pi-\theta)\cos\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2}+\theta\right)} = \cot^2\theta$.

Vardaan Learning Institute Topic 03 — Solution 21
Solution Q6 Allied Angles Simplification
Reduction of Individual Terms

Step 1: Simplify each term using reduction rules:

• $\cos(\pi + \theta) = -\cos\theta$   [Quadrant III: cosine is negative, $n=2$ even $\implies \cos$ remains $\cos$].

• $\cos(-\theta) = \cos\theta$   [Quadrant IV: cosine is positive].

• $\sin(\pi - \theta) = \sin\theta$   [Quadrant II: sine is positive, $n=2$ even $\implies \sin$ remains $\sin$].

• $\cos\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2} + \theta\right) = -\sin\theta$   [Quadrant II: cosine is negative, $n=1$ odd $\implies \cos \to \sin$].

Step 2: Substitute into LHS:

     $\text{LHS} = \dfrac{(-\cos\theta)(\cos\theta)}{(\sin\theta)(-\sin\theta)} = \dfrac{-\cos^2\theta}{-\sin^2\theta} = \dfrac{\cos^2\theta}{\sin^2\theta} = \mathbf{\cot^2\theta} = \text{RHS}$.   $\blacksquare$

Verified:   $\dfrac{(-\cos\theta)(\cos\theta)}{(\sin\theta)(-\sin\theta)} = \cot^2\theta$

Question 07 RD Sharma RS Aggarwal

Find the exact value of:   (i) $\tan 15^\circ$   (ii) $\cos 105^\circ$.

Vardaan Learning Institute Topic 03 — Solution 23
Solution Q7 Exact Trigonometric Evaluations
Derivations

(i) $\tan 15^\circ$ :

     Write $15^\circ = 45^\circ - 30^\circ$:

     $\tan 15^\circ = \dfrac{\tan 45^\circ - \tan 30^\circ}{1 + \tan 45^\circ\tan 30^\circ} = \dfrac{1 - 1/\sqrt{3}}{1 + 1/\sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}-1}{\sqrt{3}+1} = \mathbf{2 - \sqrt{3}}$.

(ii) $\cos 105^\circ$ :

     $\cos 105^\circ = \cos(90^\circ + 15^\circ) = -\sin 15^\circ$.

     Since $\sin 15^\circ = \dfrac{\sqrt{6}-\sqrt{2}}{4}$:

     $\cos 105^\circ = -\left(\dfrac{\sqrt{6}-\sqrt{2}}{4}\right) = \mathbf{\dfrac{\sqrt{2}-\sqrt{6}}{4}}$.

Answers:   (i) $\mathbf{2 - \sqrt{3}}$   (ii) $\mathbf{\dfrac{\sqrt{2}-\sqrt{6}}{4}}$

Practice Q8 Allied Angles Identity Proof
Question 08 CBSE 2023 RD Sharma

Prove that:   $\sin(180^\circ - \theta)\cos(270^\circ + \theta) + \cos(180^\circ + \theta)\sin(270^\circ - \theta) = 1$.

Vardaan Learning Institute Topic 03 — Solution 25
Solution Q8 Allied Angles Identity
Step-by-Step Reduction

Step 1: Simplify each factor on LHS:

• $\sin(180^\circ - \theta) = \sin\theta$   [Quadrant II: sine is positive].

• $\cos(270^\circ + \theta) = \sin\theta$   [Quadrant IV: cosine is positive, odd multiple $\implies \cos \to \sin$].

• $\cos(180^\circ + \theta) = -\cos\theta$   [Quadrant III: cosine is negative].

• $\sin(270^\circ - \theta) = -\cos\theta$   [Quadrant III: sine is negative, odd multiple $\implies \sin \to \cos$].

Step 2: Substitute into LHS:

     $\text{LHS} = (\sin\theta)(\sin\theta) + (-\cos\theta)(-\cos\theta) = \sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta$.

Step 3: Apply Pythagorean Identity:

     $\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = \mathbf{1} = \text{RHS}$.   $\blacksquare$

Verified:   $\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1$

Practice Q9 Tangent Product Identity
Question 09 NCERT CBSE 2022

Prove that:   $\tan 3x \tan 2x \tan x = \tan 3x - \tan 2x - \tan x$.

Vardaan Learning Institute Topic 04 — Solution 29
Solution Q9 Tangent Decomposition Method
Proof

Step 1: Write $3x$ as sum of $2x$ and $x$:

     $3x = 2x + x \implies \tan 3x = \tan(2x + x)$.

Step 2: Apply the compound tangent addition formula:

     $\tan 3x = \dfrac{\tan 2x + \tan x}{1 - \tan 2x \tan x}$.

Step 3: Cross-multiply:

     $\tan 3x (1 - \tan 2x \tan x) = \tan 2x + \tan x$.

     $\tan 3x - \tan 3x \tan 2x \tan x = \tan 2x + \tan x$.

Step 4: Rearrange terms:

     $\mathbf{\tan 3x \tan 2x \tan x = \tan 3x - \tan 2x - \tan x}$.   Hence Proved. $\blacksquare$

Key Trick:   Start with $\tan(A+B)$ where $A=2x, B=x$, then cross-multiply.

Practice Q10 Algebraic Cotangent Relation
Question 10 RD Sharma HOTS

If $\tan A - \tan B = x$ and $\cot B - \cot A = y$, prove that $\cot(A - B) = \dfrac{1}{x} + \dfrac{1}{y}$.

Vardaan Learning Institute Topic 04 — Solution 31
Solution Q10 Cotangent Subtraction Proof
Proof

Step 1: Simplify the second given equation:

     $y = \cot B - \cot A = \dfrac{1}{\tan B} - \dfrac{1}{\tan A} = \dfrac{\tan A - \tan B}{\tan A \tan B}$.

Step 2: Substitute $\tan A - \tan B = x$:

     $y = \dfrac{x}{\tan A \tan B} \implies \tan A \tan B = \dfrac{x}{y}$.

Step 3: Expand $\cot(A - B)$:

     $\cot(A - B) = \dfrac{1}{\tan(A - B)} = \dfrac{1 + \tan A \tan B}{\tan A - \tan B} = \dfrac{1 + x/y}{x} = \dfrac{(y+x)/y}{x} = \dfrac{x + y}{xy} = \mathbf{\dfrac{1}{x} + \dfrac{1}{y}}$.

     Hence Proved. $\blacksquare$

Verified:   $\cot(A - B) = \dfrac{1 + \tan A \tan B}{\tan A - \tan B} = \dfrac{1}{x} + \dfrac{1}{y}$

Practice Q11 Cosine Sum Evaluation
Question 11 NCERT CBSE 2023

Prove that:   $\cos\left(\dfrac{\pi}{4}+x\right) + \cos\left(\dfrac{\pi}{4}-x\right) = \sqrt{2}\cos x$.

Vardaan Learning Institute Topic 04 — Solution 33
Solution Q11 Cosine Expansion & Evaluation
Method 1: Direct Expansion

Step 1: Expand both cosine terms:

• $\cos\left(\dfrac{\pi}{4} + x\right) = \cos\dfrac{\pi}{4}\cos x - \sin\dfrac{\pi}{4}\sin x = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\cos x - \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sin x$.

• $\cos\left(\dfrac{\pi}{4} - x\right) = \cos\dfrac{\pi}{4}\cos x + \sin\dfrac{\pi}{4}\sin x = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\cos x + \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sin x$.

Step 2: Add the two equations:

     $\text{LHS} = \left(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\cos x - \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sin x\right) + \left(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\cos x + \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sin x\right) = \dfrac{2}{\sqrt{2}}\cos x = \mathbf{\sqrt{2}\cos x} = \text{RHS}$.   $\blacksquare$

Verified:   $\cos(\pi/4 + x) + \cos(\pi/4 - x) = 2\cos(\pi/4)\cos x = \sqrt{2}\cos x$

Practice Q12 Ratio of Sums Proof
Question 12 NCERT CBSE 2023

Prove that:   $\dfrac{\sin 5x + \sin 3x}{\cos 5x + \cos 3x} = \tan 4x$.

Vardaan Learning Institute Topic 05 — Solution 37
Solution Q12 C-D Transformation Method
Proof

Step 1: Apply C-D formula to the Numerator:

     $\sin 5x + \sin 3x = 2\sin\left(\dfrac{5x+3x}{2}\right)\cos\left(\dfrac{5x-3x}{2}\right) = 2\sin 4x \cos x$.

Step 2: Apply C-D formula to the Denominator:

     $\cos 5x + \cos 3x = 2\cos\left(\dfrac{5x+3x}{2}\right)\cos\left(\dfrac{5x-3x}{2}\right) = 2\cos 4x \cos x$.

Step 3: Divide numerator by denominator:

     $\text{LHS} = \dfrac{2\sin 4x \cos x}{2\cos 4x \cos x} = \dfrac{\sin 4x}{\cos 4x} = \mathbf{\tan 4x} = \text{RHS}$.   $\blacksquare$

Verified:   $\dfrac{\sin 5x + \sin 3x}{\cos 5x + \cos 3x} = \tan 4x$

Practice Q13 Cosine Product Identity (Classic)
Question 13 RD Sharma CBSE 2022

Prove that:   $\cos 20^\circ \cos 40^\circ \cos 60^\circ \cos 80^\circ = \dfrac{1}{16}$.

Vardaan Learning Institute Topic 05 — Solution 39
Solution Q13 Product Transformation Proof
Proof

Step 1: Substitute $\cos 60^\circ = \dfrac{1}{2}$:

     $\text{LHS} = \dfrac{1}{2} (\cos 20^\circ \cos 40^\circ \cos 80^\circ) = \dfrac{1}{4} [2\cos 40^\circ \cos 20^\circ] \cos 80^\circ$.

Step 2: Use $2\cos A\cos B = \cos(A+B) + \cos(A-B)$:

     $= \dfrac{1}{4} [\cos 60^\circ + \cos 20^\circ] \cos 80^\circ = \dfrac{1}{4} \left[\dfrac{1}{2} + \cos 20^\circ\right] \cos 80^\circ$.

Step 3: Distribute $\cos 80^\circ$:

     $= \dfrac{1}{8}\cos 80^\circ + \dfrac{1}{4}\cos 80^\circ\cos 20^\circ = \dfrac{1}{8}\cos 80^\circ + \dfrac{1}{8}[2\cos 80^\circ\cos 20^\circ]$.

     $= \dfrac{1}{8}\cos 80^\circ + \dfrac{1}{8}[\cos 100^\circ + \cos 60^\circ]$.

Step 4: Note $\cos 100^\circ = -\cos 80^\circ$:

     $= \dfrac{1}{8}\cos 80^\circ - \dfrac{1}{8}\cos 80^\circ + \dfrac{1}{8}\cos 60^\circ = \dfrac{1}{8}\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right) = \mathbf{\dfrac{1}{16}} = \text{RHS}$.   $\blacksquare$

Verified:   $\cos 20^\circ\cos 40^\circ\cos 60^\circ\cos 80^\circ = \dfrac{1}{16}$

Practice Q14 Four-Term Transformation Identity
Question 14 NCERT Misc HOTS

Prove that:   $\dfrac{\sin 7x + \sin 5x + \sin 3x + \sin x}{\cos 7x + \cos 5x + \cos 3x + \cos x} = \tan 4x$.

Vardaan Learning Institute Topic 05 — Solution 41
Solution Q14 Strategic Pairing Transformation
Proof

Step 1: Pair terms symmetrically to create common angle $4x$:

     Numerator $= (\sin 7x + \sin x) + (\sin 5x + \sin 3x)$

     $= 2\sin\left(\dfrac{7x+x}{2}\right)\cos\left(\dfrac{7x-x}{2}\right) + 2\sin\left(\dfrac{5x+3x}{2}\right)\cos\left(\dfrac{5x-3x}{2}\right)$

     $= 2\sin 4x \cos 3x + 2\sin 4x \cos x = 2\sin 4x (\cos 3x + \cos x)$.

Step 2: Pair terms in the denominator similarly:

     Denominator $= (\cos 7x + \cos x) + (\cos 5x + \cos 3x)$

     $= 2\cos 4x \cos 3x + 2\cos 4x \cos x = 2\cos 4x (\cos 3x + \cos x)$.

Step 3: Divide Numerator by Denominator:

     $\text{LHS} = \dfrac{2\sin 4x (\cos 3x + \cos x)}{2\cos 4x (\cos 3x + \cos x)} = \dfrac{\sin 4x}{\cos 4x} = \mathbf{\tan 4x} = \text{RHS}$.   $\blacksquare$

Strategic Tip:   Pair $(7x, x)$ and $(5x, 3x)$ so that $(C+D)/2 = 4x$ factors out directly!

Triple Angle$\sin 3x = 3\sin x - 4\sin^3 x, \quad \cos 3x = 4\cos^3 x - 3\cos x, \quad \tan 3x = \dfrac{3\tan x - \tan^3 x}{1 - 3\tan^2 x}$
Transformation$2\sin A\cos B = \sin(A+B)+\sin(A-B), \quad \sin C+\sin D = 2\sin\frac{C+D}{2}\cos\frac{C-D}{2}$
General Soln$\sin x = \sin\alpha \implies x = n\pi+(-1)^n\alpha, \quad \cos x = \cos\alpha \implies x = 2n\pi\pm\alpha$
Triangle Laws$\dfrac{a}{\sin A} = \dfrac{b}{\sin B} = \dfrac{c}{\sin C} = 2R, \quad \cos A = \dfrac{b^2+c^2-a^2}{2bc}$

Revision Common Mistakes & How to Avoid Them
❌ Common Mistake✔ Correct Mathematical Approach
Using $l = r\theta$ with $\theta$ in degrees$\theta$ MUST ALWAYS be converted to radians first: $\theta_{\text{rad}} = \theta^\circ \times \dfrac{\pi}{180}$
$\sin(A+B) = \sin A + \sin B$Trig functions do NOT distribute over addition! $\sin(A+B) = \sin A\cos B + \cos A\sin B$
$\cos(-x) = -\cos x$Cosine is an EVEN function: $\cos(-x) = +\cos x$, $\sec(-x) = +\sec x$
Cancelling $\sin x$ across an equation ($2\sin^2 x = \sin x \implies 2\sin x = 1$)Never divide by variable factors! You lose root $\sin x = 0$. Factor: $\sin x(2\sin x - 1) = 0$
Missing quadrant sign when taking square roots in half-anglesAlways determine the quadrant of $\dfrac{x}{2}$ explicitly before choosing $+$ or $-$ sign!
Forgetting $(-1)^n$ in $\sin x = \sin\alpha$The general solution is $x = n\pi + (-1)^n\alpha$, NOT $n\pi + \alpha$ (which is for $\tan$)
