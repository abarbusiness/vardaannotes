Similar Figures
& Similarity of Polygons

"Same shape, different size — the foundation of all triangle geometry."

Vardaan Learning InstituteTopic 1 — Similarity04
ConceptSimilar Figures — Definition & Conditions
Definition

Two figures having the same shape but not necessarily same size are called Similar Figures.

  • Congruent figures are always similar (same shape AND same size)
  • Similar figures are not necessarily congruent
  • All circles are similar; All squares are similar; All equilateral triangles are similar
Condition for Similar Polygons

Two polygons of the same number of sides are similar if:

(a) All corresponding angles are equal, AND

(b) All corresponding sides are proportional

⚠ Both conditions must hold simultaneously!

⚠ Common Mistake: Square vs Rhombus

A square and rhombus of same side are NOT similar — sides are proportional but angles differ (90° ≠ 60°)

Practice Q1Similarity Concepts & Polygon Conditions
Question 1 NCERT Ex 6.1 Board PYQ

(i) Fill in the blanks: All circles are ________, all squares are ________, all equilateral triangles are ________.

(ii) State whether a square of side 3 cm and a rhombus of side 3 cm with acute angle 60° are similar. Give a valid mathematical reason.

(iii) Two polygons are similar if their corresponding angles are ________ and their corresponding sides are ________.

Square vs Rhombus (3 cm): Angles Unequal ⇒ Not Similar
Vardaan Learning InstituteTopic 1 — Solution06
Solution Q1Similarity Concepts — Full Solutions
✔ Detailed Solution

(i) All circles are similar — all equilateral triangles are similar — all squares are similar.

(ii) Square vs Rhombus: Ratio of corresponding sides $= \frac{3}{3} = 1$ (sides are proportional).

But in the square, all angles $= 90°$; in the rhombus, angles are $60°$ and $120°$.

Since corresponding angles are NOT equal $\Rightarrow$ Square and Rhombus are NOT similar.

(iii) Two polygons are similar if their corresponding angles are equal and their corresponding sides are proportional.

⚠ Key Exam Tip

For similarity, BOTH conditions are mandatory. Equal sides alone (like square and rhombus) do NOT guarantee similarity!

Basic Proportionality Theorem
(BPT / Thales Theorem)

"A line parallel to one side of a triangle divides the other two sides proportionally."

Vardaan Learning InstituteTopic 2 — BPT08
Theorem 6.1Basic Proportionality Theorem (BPT) — Statement & Proof
Statement

If a line is drawn parallel to one side of a triangle to intersect the other two sides in distinct points, the other two sides are divided in the same ratio.

In $\Delta ABC$, if $DE \parallel BC$ intersects $AB$ at $D$ and $AC$ at $E$, then:

$$\frac{AD}{DB} = \frac{AE}{EC}$$
Key Corollaries

$\dfrac{AD}{AB} = \dfrac{AE}{AC}$    $\dfrac{DB}{AB} = \dfrac{EC}{AC}$    $\dfrac{AD}{AB} = \dfrac{DE}{BC}$

Proof Outline

Join BE and CD. Draw $EN \perp AB$ and $DM \perp AC$.

$\dfrac{\text{ar}(\Delta ADE)}{\text{ar}(\Delta BDE)} = \dfrac{AD}{DB}$   and   $\dfrac{\text{ar}(\Delta ADE)}{\text{ar}(\Delta DEC)} = \dfrac{AE}{EC}$

Since $\Delta BDE$ and $\Delta DEC$ have same base $DE$ and same parallels: $\text{ar}(\Delta BDE) = \text{ar}(\Delta DEC)$  $\Rightarrow$  $\dfrac{AD}{DB} = \dfrac{AE}{EC}$  $\blacksquare$

Question 2 NCERT Ex 6.2 Q1 Board PYQ

In $\Delta ABC$, $DE \parallel BC$. Find: (i) $EC$ when $AD = 1.5, DB = 3, AE = 1$ cm  •  (ii) $EC$ when $AD = 1, DB = 3, AE = 1.5$ cm  •  (iii) $x$ when $AD = x, DB = x - 2, AE = x + 2, EC = x - 1$.

Fig 6.2 Reference: (a) BPT Numerical Dimensions | (b) Double Parallel Lines DE ∥ BC & FE ∥ DC
Vardaan Learning InstituteTopic 2 — Solution10
Solution Q2BPT Numerical Solutions
✔ Solution

(i) By BPT: $\dfrac{AD}{DB} = \dfrac{AE}{EC} \Rightarrow \dfrac{1.5}{3} = \dfrac{1}{EC}$

$EC = \dfrac{1 \times 3}{1.5} = \mathbf{2 \text{ cm}}$

✔ Solution (ii)

$\dfrac{AD}{DB} = \dfrac{AE}{EC} \Rightarrow \dfrac{1}{3} = \dfrac{1.5}{EC}$

$EC = 1.5 \times 3 = \mathbf{4.5 \text{ cm}}$

✔ Solution (iii) — Variable

$DE \parallel BC \iff \dfrac{AD}{DB} = \dfrac{AE}{EC}$

$\Rightarrow \dfrac{x}{x-2} = \dfrac{x+2}{x-1}$

$x(x-1) = (x-2)(x+2)$

$x^2 - x = x^2 - 4$

$-x = -4 \Rightarrow \mathbf{x = 4}$

Key Formula

$DE \parallel BC \Rightarrow \dfrac{AD}{DB} = \dfrac{AE}{EC}$  (BPT)

Converse of BPT
(Theorem 6.2)

"If the sides are divided proportionally, the dividing line must be parallel."

Vardaan Learning InstituteTopic 3 — Converse BPT12
Theorem 6.2Converse of BPT — Statement & Proof
Statement (Converse BPT)

If a line divides any two sides of a triangle in the same ratio, then the line is parallel to the third side.

$$\frac{AD}{DB} = \frac{AE}{EC} \implies DE \parallel BC$$
Proof by Contradiction
  1. Suppose $DE$ is NOT parallel to $BC$. Draw $DE' \parallel BC$ meeting $AC$ at $E'$.
  2. By BPT: $\dfrac{AD}{DB} = \dfrac{AE'}{E'C}$
  3. Given: $\dfrac{AD}{DB} = \dfrac{AE}{EC} \Rightarrow \dfrac{AE'}{E'C} = \dfrac{AE}{EC}$
  4. $\Rightarrow \dfrac{AC}{E'C} = \dfrac{AC}{EC} \Rightarrow E'C = EC \Rightarrow E' \equiv E$
  5. $\therefore DE \parallel BC$.    $\blacksquare$

Trapeziums & Diagonal
Proportionality (BPT Applications)

"Diagonals of a trapezium divide each other proportionally."

Vardaan Learning InstituteTopic 4 — Trapezium16
TheoremTrapezium Diagonal Proportionality
Trapezium Core Properties
  • Diagonals Property: In trapezium $ABCD$ with $AB \parallel DC$, diagonals $AC$ and $BD$ intersect at $O$: $$\frac{AO}{BO} = \frac{CO}{DO}$$
  • Converse: If $\dfrac{AO}{CO} = \dfrac{BO}{DO}$, then $ABCD$ is a trapezium.
  • Midpoint Line: Line $EF \parallel AB \parallel DC$ through $O$  $\Rightarrow$  $OE = OF$
Proof Key Steps
  1. Draw $OE \parallel AB \parallel DC$ meeting $AD$ at $E$.
  2. In $\Delta ADC$: $OE \parallel DC \Rightarrow \dfrac{AE}{ED} = \dfrac{AO}{OC}$ (BPT)
  3. In $\Delta DAB$: $OE \parallel AB \Rightarrow \dfrac{AE}{ED} = \dfrac{BO}{OD}$
  4. $\Rightarrow \dfrac{AO}{OC} = \dfrac{BO}{OD} \Rightarrow \dfrac{AO}{BO} = \dfrac{CO}{DO}$  $\blacksquare$

Practice Q4Trapezium Diagonals — Variable & Proof
Question 4 CBSE 2022 RD Sharma NCERT Ex 6.2 Q10

(i) In trapezium $ABCD$, $AB \parallel DC$. Diagonals intersect at $O$. If $AO = 3x-1$, $OC = 5x-3$, $BO = 2x+1$, $OD = 6x-5$, find $x$.

(ii) The diagonals of quadrilateral $ABCD$ intersect at $O$ such that $\dfrac{AO}{BO} = \dfrac{CO}{DO}$. Show that $ABCD$ is a trapezium.

Trapezium Diagonals: AO/OC = BO/OD (x = 2)
Vardaan Learning InstituteTopic 4 — Solution18
Solution Q4Trapezium Solutions
✔ Part (i) — Finding x

$\dfrac{AO}{OC} = \dfrac{BO}{OD} \Rightarrow \dfrac{3x-1}{5x-3} = \dfrac{2x+1}{6x-5}$

$(3x-1)(6x-5) = (2x+1)(5x-3)$

$18x^2 - 21x + 5 = 10x^2 - x - 3$

$8x^2 - 20x + 8 = 0 \Rightarrow 2x^2 - 5x + 2 = 0$

$(2x-1)(x-2) = 0 \Rightarrow x = \frac{1}{2}$ (rejected, $OD < 0$) or $\mathbf{x = 2}$

✔ Part (ii) — Proving Trapezium

Given: $\dfrac{AO}{CO} = \dfrac{BO}{DO}$. Draw $OE \parallel AB$ meeting $AD$ at $E$.

In $\Delta DAB$: $OE \parallel AB \Rightarrow \dfrac{AE}{ED} = \dfrac{BO}{DO}$ (BPT)

$= \dfrac{AO}{CO}$ (given)

In $\Delta ADC$: $\dfrac{AE}{ED} = \dfrac{AO}{CO} \Rightarrow OE \parallel DC$ (Converse BPT)

Since $OE \parallel AB$ and $OE \parallel DC \Rightarrow \mathbf{AB \parallel DC}$

$\therefore ABCD$ is a trapezium. $\blacksquare$

Criteria for Similarity
AA • SSS • SAS

"Three powerful tests to establish that two triangles are similar."

Vardaan Learning InstituteTopic 5 — Similarity Criteria20
Theorems 6.3–6.5AA, SSS, SAS Similarity Criteria
AA Similarity (Theorem 6.3)

If two angles of one triangle are respectively equal to two angles of another triangle, the triangles are similar.

$\angle A = \angle D$ and $\angle B = \angle E \Rightarrow \Delta ABC \sim \Delta DEF$

SSS Similarity (Theorem 6.4)

If all three corresponding sides are in proportion:

$\dfrac{AB}{DE} = \dfrac{BC}{EF} = \dfrac{CA}{FD} \Rightarrow \Delta ABC \sim \Delta DEF$

SAS Similarity (Theorem 6.5)

If two sides are proportional and the included angle is equal:

$\dfrac{AB}{DE} = \dfrac{AC}{DF}$ and $\angle A = \angle D \Rightarrow \Delta ABC \sim \Delta DEF$

⚠ SAS Rule — Critical!

The equal angle MUST be between the two proportional sides. $\angle B = \angle E$ with $\frac{AB}{DE} = \frac{AC}{DF}$ does NOT qualify for SAS!

Practice Q5Similarity Criteria — Proofs & Applications
Question 5 CBSE 2020 NCERT Ex 6.3 Q11, Q3, Q13 RD Sharma

(i) In figure, $E$ is a point on side $CB$ produced of an isosceles triangle $ABC$ with $AB = AC$. If $AD \perp BC$ and $EF \perp AC$, prove that $\Delta ABD \sim \Delta ECF$.

(ii) Diagonals of a trapezium $ABCD$ with $AB \parallel DC$ intersect at $O$. Using a similarity criterion for two triangles, show that $\dfrac{OA}{OC} = \dfrac{OB}{OD}$.

(iii) In $\Delta ABC$, $D$ is a point on side $BC$ such that $\angle ADC = \angle BAC$. Prove that $CA^2 = CB \times CD$.

Isosceles ΔABC: AD ⊥ BC and EF ⊥ AC ⇒ ΔABD ∼ ΔECF
Vardaan Learning InstituteTopic 5 — Solution22
Solution Q5Similarity Criteria Solutions
✔ Part (i) — Isosceles Triangle AA Similarity

In $\Delta ABC$, since $AB = AC$ (angles opposite to equal sides are equal):

$\implies \angle B = \angle C \implies \angle ABD = \angle ECF$

Also, $AD \perp BC \implies \angle ADB = 90°$ and $EF \perp AC \implies \angle EFC = 90°$

$\implies \angle ADB = \angle EFC = 90°$

Therefore, by AA similarity criterion:

$\mathbf{\Delta ABD \sim \Delta ECF}$  $\blacksquare$

✔ Part (ii) — Trapezium Diagonals via Similarity

In trapezium $ABCD$, $AB \parallel DC$ and diagonals intersect at $O$.

In $\Delta OAB$ and $\Delta OCD$:

$\bullet$ $\angle OAB = \angle OCD$ (Alternate interior angles, $AB \parallel DC$)

$\bullet$ $\angle OBA = \angle ODC$ (Alternate interior angles, $AB \parallel DC$)

$\bullet$ $\angle AOB = \angle COD$ (Vertically opposite angles)

$\therefore \Delta OAB \sim \Delta OCD$ (by AA similarity)

$\implies \dfrac{OA}{OC} = \dfrac{OB}{OD} = \dfrac{AB}{CD} \implies \mathbf{\dfrac{OA}{OC} = \dfrac{OB}{OD}}$  $\blacksquare$

✔ Part (iii) — $CA^2 = CB \times CD$

In $\Delta ABC$ and $\Delta DAC$:

$\bullet$ $\angle BAC = \angle ADC$ (Given)

$\bullet$ $\angle C = \angle C$ (Common angle to both triangles)

$\therefore \Delta ABC \sim \Delta DAC$ (by AA similarity criterion)

Since corresponding sides of similar triangles are in proportion:

$\dfrac{CA}{CD} = \dfrac{CB}{CA}$

Cross-multiplying: $\mathbf{CA^2 = CB \times CD}$  $\blacksquare$

Exam Pro-Tip: AA Similarity

In Class 10 Board exams, over 80% of similarity proof questions use the AA criterion. Identifying just two pairs of equal angles (such as a common angle + a right angle, or alternate interior angles) completes the proof!

Practice Q6Real-World Applications — Shadows & Mirrors
Question 6 NCERT Ex 6.3 Q15 CBSE 2019 NCERT Exemplar

(i) A 6 m pole casts a 4 m shadow. Find the height of a tower casting a 28 m shadow at the same time.  •  (ii) A girl of height 90 cm walks away from a 3.6 m lamp-post at 1.2 m/s. Find her shadow after 4 s.  •  (iii) A person (1.8 m) stands 2.4 m from a ground mirror to view a building top 12 m from mirror. Find building height.

Real-World Figures from Notes: (a) Sun Shadow Method ($h=42$ m) | (b) Girl & Lamp-Post Shadow ($x=1.6$ m)
Vardaan Learning InstituteTopic 6 — Solution25
Solution Q6Real-World Application Solutions
✔ Part (i) — Sun Shadow

Same sun angle $\Rightarrow \Delta ABC \sim \Delta PQR$ (AA Similarity)

$\dfrac{\text{Pole}}{\text{Tower}} = \dfrac{\text{Shadow}_P}{\text{Shadow}_T} \Rightarrow \dfrac{6}{h} = \dfrac{4}{28}$

$h = \dfrac{6 \times 28}{4} = \mathbf{42 \text{ metres}}$

✔ Part (iii) — Mirror Method

Law of Reflection: angle of incidence = angle of reflection

AA Similarity: $\dfrac{h}{1.8} = \dfrac{12}{2.4} = 5$

$h = \mathbf{9.0 \text{ metres}}$

✔ Part (ii) — Lamp-Post Shadow

Distance walked $BD = 1.2 \times 4 = 4.8$ m. Let shadow $= x$ cm.

$\Delta ABE \sim \Delta CDE$ (AA Similarity)

$\dfrac{360}{90} = \dfrac{480 + x}{x} \Rightarrow 4x = 480 + x$

$3x = 480 \Rightarrow \mathbf{x = 160 \text{ cm} = 1.6 \text{ m}}$

Practice Q7HOTS — Square Inscribed in Triangle & Classic 5-Markers
Question 7 5 Marks NCERT Exemplar RD Sharma CBSE 2023

(i) Square $DEFG$ is inscribed in right $\Delta ABC$ ($\angle A=90°$) with $DE$ on hypotenuse $BC$. Prove: (a) $\Delta AGF \sim \Delta DBG$, (b) $\Delta AGF \sim \Delta EFC$, (c) $DE^2 = BD \times EC$.  •  (ii) If $AB \perp BF, CD \perp BF, EF \perp BF$ with lengths $x, z, y$, prove $\dfrac{1}{x} + \dfrac{1}{y} = \dfrac{1}{z}$.

HOTS Figures from Notes: (a) Inscribed Square $DE^2 = BD \times EC$ | (b) Three Perpendiculars $\dfrac{1}{x} + \dfrac{1}{y} = \dfrac{1}{z}$
Vardaan Learning InstituteTopic 7 — Solution28
Solution Q7HOTS Solutions — 5-Mark Proofs
✔ Square in Triangle — Parts (i) to (iii)

(a) $DEFG$ is a square $\Rightarrow GD \perp BC, FE \perp BC, GF \parallel BC$

$\angle BGD = 90° - \angle B = \angle C = \angle AFG \Rightarrow \mathbf{\Delta AGF \sim \Delta DBG}$ (AA)

(b) $\angle CFE = 90° - \angle C = \angle B = \angle AGF$

$\Rightarrow \mathbf{\Delta AGF \sim \Delta EFC}$ (AA)

(c) From (a) and (b): $\Delta DBG \sim \Delta EFC$

$\Rightarrow \dfrac{BD}{EF} = \dfrac{GD}{EC}$; Since $GD = EF = DE$ (square sides)

$\dfrac{BD}{DE} = \dfrac{DE}{EC} \Rightarrow \mathbf{DE^2 = BD \times EC}$ $\blacksquare$

✔ Three Perpendiculars: $1/x + 1/y = 1/z$

In $\Delta ABF$, $CD \parallel AB \Rightarrow \dfrac{z}{x} = \dfrac{DF}{BF}$   ...(I)

In $\Delta EBF$, $CD \parallel EF \Rightarrow \dfrac{z}{y} = \dfrac{BD}{BF}$   ...(II)

Adding (I) and (II):

$\dfrac{z}{x} + \dfrac{z}{y} = \dfrac{DF + BD}{BF} = \dfrac{BF}{BF} = 1$

$\Rightarrow z \left(\dfrac{1}{x} + \dfrac{1}{y}\right) = 1 \Rightarrow \mathbf{\dfrac{1}{x} + \dfrac{1}{y} = \dfrac{1}{z}}$ $\blacksquare$

Question 8 Competence-Based CBSE 2024

Case Study: A survey team measures river width using indirect triangulation. Points $A$ (tree on opposite bank) and $B$ (near bank) form perpendicular line $AB$. Line $BCD$ is staked along bank with $BC = 40$ m, $CD = 20$ m. Line $DE \perp BD$ is walked away from river to $E = 30$ m such that $A, C, E$ are collinear.

(i) Prove that $\Delta ABC \sim \Delta EDC$.  •  (ii) Calculate width $w = AB$.  •  (iii) Find ratio Perimeter($\Delta ABC$) : Perimeter($\Delta EDC$).

Triangulation: Indirect River Width Measurement
Vardaan Learning InstituteCompetence-Based — Solution30
Solution Q8Case Study Solutions
✔ Part (i) — Proving ΔABC ∼ ΔEDC

In $\Delta ABC$ and $\Delta EDC$:

1. $\angle B = \angle D = 90°$ (perpendicular to base line)

2. $\angle ACB = \angle ECD$ (vertically opposite angles)

$\therefore \mathbf{\Delta ABC \sim \Delta EDC}$ (by AA similarity criterion)  $\blacksquare$

✔ Part (ii) — River Width w

Since $\Delta ABC \sim \Delta EDC$, corresponding sides are proportional:

$\dfrac{AB}{ED} = \dfrac{BC}{CD} \implies \dfrac{w}{30} = \dfrac{40}{20} = 2$

$\implies \mathbf{w = 30 \times 2 = 60 \text{ metres}}$  $\blacksquare$

✔ Part (iii) — Perimeter Ratio

Ratio of perimeters of similar triangles equals ratio of their corresponding sides:

$\dfrac{\text{Perimeter}(\Delta ABC)}{\text{Perimeter}(\Delta EDC)} = \dfrac{BC}{CD} = \dfrac{40}{20} = \mathbf{2 : 1}$  $\blacksquare$

Key Takeaway: Triangulation enables measuring inaccessible distances (rivers, canyons) purely through angle sightlines and proportional lengths.
