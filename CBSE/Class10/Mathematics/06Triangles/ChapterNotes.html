Topic 1: Geometric Similarity vs Congruence & Similarity of Polygons

Core Definitions & Similarity Concept
  • Similar Figures ($\sim$): Two geometric figures having the exact same shape, but not necessarily the same size.
  • Congruent Figures ($\cong$): Two geometric figures having both the exact same shape and the exact same size (special case of similarity with scale factor $k = 1$).
  • Similarity of Polygons: Two polygons of the same number of sides are similar if and only if both conditions hold:
    1. All corresponding angles are equal, AND
    2. All corresponding sides are in the same ratio (proportional).
  • Universal Truths: All circles are similar, all squares are similar, and all equilateral triangles are similar.
Parameter Congruent Triangles ($\Delta ABC \cong \Delta DEF$) Similar Triangles ($\Delta ABC \sim \Delta DEF$)
Shape Identical Identical
Size Exactly Equal May be Different (Scaled)
Corresponding Angles Equal ($\angle A = \angle D, \angle B = \angle E, \angle C = \angle F$) Equal ($\angle A = \angle D, \angle B = \angle E, \angle C = \angle F$)
Corresponding Sides Equal ($AB = DE, BC = EF, AC = DF$) In the Same Ratio ($\frac{AB}{DE} = \frac{BC}{EF} = \frac{AC}{DF} = k$)
Fig 6.1: Congruent Triangles ($\Delta ABC \cong \Delta DEF$) vs Similar Triangles ($\Delta PQR \sim \Delta XYZ$)
Teacher's Exam Secret — 1-Mark MCQ Rules
All congruent figures are similar, but similar figures need NOT be congruent. For two polygons to be similar, equality of corresponding angles ALONE or proportionality of sides ALONE is insufficient (except for triangles where either implies the other).
Topic 1: School Exam Practice Problem Kit
Problem 1.1 (Polygon Similarity Condition) 1 MARK

State whether a square of side $3\text{ cm}$ and a rhombus of side $3\text{ cm}$ are similar. Justify your answer.

Step-by-Step Solution:
NO, they are not similar. Although their corresponding sides are proportional ($\frac{3}{3} = 1$), their corresponding angles are not equal (square angles are all $90^\circ$, whereas a rhombus has non-right angles).
Problem 1.2 (Equilateral Triangles Similarity) 1 MARK

Why are any two equilateral triangles always similar, regardless of their side lengths?

Step-by-Step Solution:
In every equilateral triangle, each interior angle is exactly $60^\circ$. Hence, by the AAA Similarity Criterion, all corresponding angles are equal, ensuring they are always similar.

Topic 2: Basic Proportionality Theorem (Thales Theorem - Theorem 6.1)

Theorem 6.1: Basic Proportionality Theorem (BPT / Thales Theorem)

Statement: If a line is drawn parallel to one side of a triangle to intersect the other two sides in distinct points, the other two sides are divided in the same ratio.

$$\mathbf{\frac{AD}{DB} = \frac{AE}{EC}}$$
Fig 6.2: Proof Diagram for Theorem 6.1 (BPT) with Altitudes $EN \perp AB$, $DM \perp AC$ and Segments $BE$, $CD$

Given: In $\Delta ABC$, line $DE \parallel BC$ intersects $AB$ at $D$ and $AC$ at $E$.

To Prove: $\frac{AD}{DB} = \frac{AE}{EC}$

Construction: Join $B$ to $E$ and $C$ to $D$. Draw $DM \perp AC$ and $EN \perp AB$.

Proof:

  1. $\text{Area of a triangle} = \frac{1}{2} \times \text{base} \times \text{height}$.
  2. $\text{ar}(\Delta ADE) = \frac{1}{2} \times AD \times EN$ and $\text{ar}(\Delta BDE) = \frac{1}{2} \times DB \times EN$. $$\implies \frac{\text{ar}(\Delta ADE)}{\text{ar}(\Delta BDE)} = \frac{\frac{1}{2} \times AD \times EN}{\frac{1}{2} \times DB \times EN} = \frac{AD}{DB} \quad \text{--- (1)}$$
  3. Similarly, $\text{ar}(\Delta ADE) = \frac{1}{2} \times AE \times DM$ and $\text{ar}(\Delta DEC) = \frac{1}{2} \times EC \times DM$. $$\implies \frac{\text{ar}(\Delta ADE)}{\text{ar}(\Delta DEC)} = \frac{\frac{1}{2} \times AE \times DM}{\frac{1}{2} \times EC \times DM} = \frac{AE}{EC} \quad \text{--- (2)}$$
  4. $\Delta BDE$ and $\Delta DEC$ are on the same base $DE$ and between the same parallel lines $DE$ and $BC$. $$\implies \text{ar}(\Delta BDE) = \text{ar}(\Delta DEC) \quad \text{--- (3)}$$
  5. From (1), (2), and (3), we get: $$\mathbf{\frac{AD}{DB} = \frac{AE}{EC}}$$
■
Corollaries of BPT:
$\mathbf{\frac{AB}{AD} = \frac{AC}{AE}}$   or   $\mathbf{\frac{AD}{AB} = \frac{AE}{AC}}$
$\mathbf{\frac{AB}{DB} = \frac{AC}{EC}}$   or   $\mathbf{\frac{DB}{AB} = \frac{EC}{AC}}$
Topic 2: School Exam Practice Problem Kit
Problem 2.1 (Quadratic BPT Equation) NCERT / 3 MARKS

In $\Delta ABC$, $DE \parallel BC$. If $AD = x$, $DB = x - 2$, $AE = x + 2$, and $EC = x - 1$, find the value of $x$.

Step-by-Step Solution:
By BPT, $\frac{AD}{DB} = \frac{AE}{EC} \implies \frac{x}{x-2} = \frac{x+2}{x-1}$. $$x(x-1) = (x-2)(x+2) \implies x^2 - x = x^2 - 4 \implies \mathbf{x = 4}$$

Problem 2.2 (Linear Proportional Calculation) 2 MARKS

In $\Delta ABC$, $DE \parallel BC$. If $AD = 2.4\text{ cm}$, $DB = 3.6\text{ cm}$, and $AC = 7.5\text{ cm}$, find the length of $AE$.

Step-by-Step Solution:
$AB = AD + DB = 2.4 + 3.6 = 6.0\text{ cm}$. $$\frac{AD}{AB} = \frac{AE}{AC} \implies \frac{2.4}{6.0} = \frac{AE}{7.5} \implies AE = \frac{2 \times 7.5}{5} = \mathbf{3.0\text{ cm}}$$

Topic 3: Converse of Basic Proportionality Theorem (Theorem 6.2)

Theorem 6.2: Converse of BPT

Statement: If a line divides any two sides of a triangle in the same ratio, then the line is parallel to the third side.

$$\text{If } \frac{AD}{DB} = \frac{AE}{EC} \implies \mathbf{DE \parallel BC}$$
Fig 6.3: Proof by Contradiction — Supposing $DE' \parallel BC$ shows that $E'$ and $E$ Coincide

Proof Logic (Contradiction): Assume $DE$ is not parallel to $BC$. Then draw $DE' \parallel BC$. By BPT, $\frac{AD}{DB} = \frac{AE'}{E'C}$. But given $\frac{AD}{DB} = \frac{AE}{EC} \implies \frac{AE'}{E'C} = \frac{AE}{EC}$. Adding $1$ to both sides gives $\frac{AC}{E'C} = \frac{AC}{EC} \implies E'C = EC$, proving $E'$ and $E$ coincide. Hence $\mathbf{DE \parallel BC}$. ■

Topic 3: School Exam Practice Problem Kit
Problem 3.1 (Testing Parallelism) NCERT EX 6.2

In $\Delta PQR$, $E$ and $F$ are points on $PQ$ and $PR$ such that $PE = 4\text{ cm}, QE = 4.5\text{ cm}, PF = 8\text{ cm}, RF = 9\text{ cm}$. State whether $EF \parallel QR$.

Step-by-Step Solution:
$$\frac{PE}{EQ} = \frac{4}{4.5} = \frac{8}{9}, \qquad \frac{PF}{FR} = \frac{8}{9}$$ Since $\frac{PE}{EQ} = \frac{PF}{FR} = \frac{8}{9}$, by Converse of BPT, $\mathbf{EF \parallel QR}$.
Problem 3.2 (Converse BPT in Polygon/Triangles) 3 MARKS

In $\Delta PQR$, $A, B, C$ lie on $OP, OQ, OR$ such that $AB \parallel PQ$ and $AC \parallel PR$. Show that $BC \parallel QR$.

Step-by-Step Solution:
In $\Delta OPQ$, $AB \parallel PQ \implies \frac{OA}{AP} = \frac{OB}{BQ}$.
In $\Delta OPR$, $AC \parallel PR \implies \frac{OA}{AP} = \frac{OC}{CR}$.
Equating: $\frac{OB}{BQ} = \frac{OC}{CR} \implies \mathbf{BC \parallel QR}$ (by Converse of BPT in $\Delta OQR$). ■

Topic 4: Trapezium Diagonals & Parallel Segments Theorems

Trapezium Core Properties
  • Trapezium Diagonals Property: The diagonals of a trapezium with $AB \parallel DC$ intersect at $O$ such that: $$\mathbf{\frac{AO}{BO} = \frac{CO}{DO} \iff \frac{AO}{CO} = \frac{BO}{DO}}$$
  • Converse Property: If the diagonals of a quadrilateral divide each other proportionally, then it is a trapezium.
Theorem: Trapezium Diagonals Proportionality

Statement: In trapezium $ABCD$ with $AB \parallel DC$, diagonals $AC$ and $BD$ intersect at $O$. Prove that $\frac{AO}{BO} = \frac{CO}{DO}$.

Fig 6.4: Trapezium $ABCD$ with Diagonals Intersecting at $O$ and Construction $OE \parallel AB \parallel DC$

Proof: Draw $OE \parallel AB \parallel DC$ meeting $AD$ at $E$.

  1. In $\Delta ADC$, $OE \parallel DC \implies \frac{AE}{ED} = \frac{AO}{OC}$ (by BPT).
  2. In $\Delta DAB$, $OE \parallel AB \implies \frac{DE}{EA} = \frac{DO}{OB} \implies \frac{AE}{ED} = \frac{BO}{OD}$.
  3. Equating both: $\frac{AO}{OC} = \frac{BO}{OD} \implies \mathbf{\frac{AO}{BO} = \frac{CO}{DO}}$. ■
Topic 4: School Exam Practice Problem Kit
Problem 4.1 (Nested Parallel Segments Proof) NCERT EX 6.2 Q4

In $\Delta ABC$, $DE \parallel AC$ and $DF \parallel AE$. Prove that: $$\frac{BF}{FE} = \frac{BE}{EC}$$

Step-by-Step Solution:
In $\Delta BAC$, $DE \parallel AC \implies \frac{BD}{DA} = \frac{BE}{EC}$ (by BPT).
In $\Delta BAE$, $DF \parallel AE \implies \frac{BD}{DA} = \frac{BF}{FE}$ (by BPT).
Equating RHS: $\mathbf{\frac{BF}{FE} = \frac{BE}{EC}}$. ■
Problem 4.2 (Trapezium Diagonal Variable) 3 MARKS

In trapezium $ABCD$, $AB \parallel DC$. If $AO = 3x - 1, OC = 5x - 3, BO = 2x + 1, OD = 6x - 5$, find $x$.

Step-by-Step Solution:
$$\frac{AO}{OC} = \frac{BO}{OD} \implies \frac{3x-1}{5x-3} = \frac{2x+1}{6x-5}$$ $$(3x-1)(6x-5) = (2x+1)(5x-3) \implies 18x^2 - 21x + 5 = 10x^2 - x - 3$$ $$8x^2 - 20x + 8 = 0 \implies 2x^2 - 5x + 2 = 0 \implies (2x-1)(x-2) = 0$$ Since $x = 1/2$ makes $OD = -2 < 0$ (rejected), we get $\mathbf{x = 2}$.

Topic 5: Criteria for Similarity of Triangles (AA, SSS, SAS)

Theorem 6.3: AA (Angle-Angle) Similarity Criterion

Statement: If two angles of one triangle are respectively equal to two angles of another triangle, then the two triangles are similar.

$$\text{If } \angle A = \angle D \text{ and } \angle B = \angle E \implies \mathbf{\Delta ABC \sim \Delta DEF}$$
Fig 6.5: AA Similarity — Two Equal Angles Guarantee Equiangular Triangles
SSS & SAS Similarity Criteria
  • SSS Similarity (Theorem 6.4): If $\frac{AB}{DE} = \frac{BC}{EF} = \frac{AC}{DF} \implies \Delta ABC \sim \Delta DEF$.
  • SAS Similarity (Theorem 6.5): If $\frac{AB}{DE} = \frac{AC}{DF}$ and the included angle $\angle A = \angle D \implies \Delta ABC \sim \Delta DEF$.
Teacher's Exam Warning on SAS Similarity
The equal angle MUST be enclosed between the two proportional sides. If $\frac{AB}{DE} = \frac{AC}{DF}$, but $\angle B = \angle E$ is given, SAS similarity CANNOT be applied!
Topic 5: School Exam Practice Problem Kit
Problem 5.1 (NCERT Classic Ratio & Angle) NCERT EX 6.3 Q4

In figure, $\frac{QR}{QS} = \frac{QT}{PR}$ and $\angle 1 = \angle 2$. Show that $\Delta PQS \sim \Delta TQR$.

Step-by-Step Solution:
In $\Delta PQR$, $\angle 1 = \angle 2 \implies PQ = PR$.
Substitute $PR = PQ$ in given ratio: $\frac{QR}{QS} = \frac{QT}{PQ} \implies \frac{PQ}{QT} = \frac{QS}{QR}$.
In $\Delta PQS$ and $\Delta TQR$, $\frac{PQ}{QT} = \frac{QS}{QR}$ and $\angle Q = \angle Q$ (common).
Therefore, by SAS Similarity, $\mathbf{\Delta PQS \sim \Delta TQR}$. ■
Problem 5.2 (Side-Square Similarity Proof) 3 MARKS / CBSE 2023

$D$ is a point on side $BC$ of $\Delta ABC$ such that $\angle ADC = \angle BAC$. Prove that $CA^2 = CB \times CD$.

Step-by-Step Solution:
In $\Delta ABC$ and $\Delta DAC$: $\angle BAC = \angle ADC$ (given) and $\angle C = \angle C$ (common).
By AA similarity, $\Delta ABC \sim \Delta DAC$. $$\frac{CA}{CD} = \frac{CB}{CA} \implies \mathbf{CA^2 = CB \times CD} \quad \blacksquare$$

Topic 6: Real-World Similarity Applications (Shadow & Mirror Methods)

Example 6.1: The Sun Shadow Method NCERT EX 6.3 Q15

A vertical pole of length $6\text{ m}$ casts a shadow $4\text{ m}$ long on the ground and at the same time a tower casts a shadow $28\text{ m}$ long. Find the height of the tower.

Fig 6.6: Similar Right Triangles Formed by Sun Rays at the Same Instant

Solution: Since sun elevation angle is equal at the same instant, $\Delta ABC \sim \Delta PQR$ (AA Similarity).

$$\frac{\text{Height of Pole}}{\text{Height of Tower}} = \frac{\text{Shadow of Pole}}{\text{Shadow of Tower}} \implies \frac{6}{h} = \frac{4}{28} \implies h = 6 \times 7 = \mathbf{42\text{ metres}}$$
Topic 6: School Exam Practice Problem Kit
Problem 6.1 (Moving Person Lamp-Post Shadow) 3 MARKS / CBSE 2023

A girl of height $90\text{ cm}$ walks away from the base of a lamp-post of height $3.6\text{ m}$ at a speed of $1.2\text{ m/s}$. Find the length of her shadow after 4 seconds.

Step-by-Step Solution:
Distance walked $BD = 1.2 \times 4 = 4.8\text{ m} = 480\text{ cm}$. Let shadow $DE = x\text{ cm}$. $$\Delta ABE \sim \Delta CDE \implies \frac{360}{90} = \frac{480 + x}{x} \implies 4x = 480 + x \implies 3x = 480 \implies \mathbf{x = 160\text{ cm} = 1.6\text{ m}}$$

Topic 7: Medians, Altitudes & Advanced Proportionality

Theorem: Median Proportionality in Similar Triangles

Statement: Sides $AB, BC$ and median $AD$ of $\Delta ABC$ are proportional to sides $PQ, QR$ and median $PM$ of $\Delta PQR$. Prove that $\Delta ABC \sim \Delta PQR$.

Proof:

  1. Given $\frac{AB}{PQ} = \frac{BC}{QR} = \frac{AD}{PM}$. Since $AD$ and $PM$ are medians, $BD = BC/2$ and $QM = QR/2$.
  2. $\implies \frac{AB}{PQ} = \frac{BD}{QM} = \frac{AD}{PM} \implies \Delta ABD \sim \Delta PQM$ (by SSS Similarity).
  3. $\implies \angle B = \angle Q$ (corresponding angles).
  4. In $\Delta ABC$ and $\Delta PQR$, $\frac{AB}{PQ} = \frac{BC}{QR}$ and $\angle B = \angle Q \implies \mathbf{\Delta ABC \sim \Delta PQR}$ (by SAS Similarity). ■

Topic 8: Comprehensive Exam HOTS & Case Study Kit

Case Study 1: Ground Mirror Reflection Surveying CASE STUDY

A surveyor $1.8\text{ m}$ tall wants to determine the height of a flagpole. He places a mirror on the ground $12\text{ m}$ from the flagpole and walks backward $2.4\text{ m}$ until the top of the flagpole is visible in the mirror. Find the height of the flagpole.

Solution:

By Law of Reflection, $\text{Angle of Incidence} = \text{Angle of Reflection}$. Hence $\Delta ABM \sim \Delta CDM$ (AA Similarity).

$$\frac{h}{1.8} = \frac{12}{2.4} = 5 \implies h = 5 \times 1.8 = \mathbf{9.0\text{ metres}}$$
HOTS 2: Parallelogram Extension Proof 5 MARKS

Through the midpoint $M$ of side $CD$ of parallelogram $ABCD$, line $BM$ is drawn intersecting $AC$ in $L$ and $AD$ produced in $E$. Prove that $EL = 2BL$.

Solution:

$\Delta BMC \cong \Delta EMD \implies BC = ED \implies AE = AD + DE = 2BC$.

$\Delta AEL \sim \Delta CBL \implies \frac{EL}{BL} = \frac{AE}{BC} = \frac{2BC}{BC} = 2 \implies \mathbf{EL = 2BL}$. ■

Topic 9: 100/100 School & Board Examination Master Checklist

Master Exam Checklist
  • Basic Proportionality Theorem (BPT): $DE \parallel BC \implies \frac{AD}{DB} = \frac{AE}{EC}$ and $\frac{AD}{AB} = \frac{AE}{AC}$.
  • Converse of BPT: $\frac{AD}{DB} = \frac{AE}{EC} \implies DE \parallel BC$.
  • AA Similarity: 2 pairs of equal corresponding angles $\implies$ Triangles are similar.
  • SSS Similarity: $\frac{AB}{DE} = \frac{BC}{EF} = \frac{AC}{DF} \implies \Delta ABC \sim \Delta DEF$.
  • SAS Similarity: 2 proportional sides and the included angle equal $\implies \Delta ABC \sim \Delta DEF$.
  • Trapezium Diagonals: If $AB \parallel DC \implies \frac{AO}{CO} = \frac{BO}{DO}$.
  • Linear Ratios: $\frac{\text{Perimeter}_1}{\text{Perimeter}_2} = \frac{\text{Side}_1}{\text{Side}_2} = \frac{\text{Altitude}_1}{\text{Altitude}_2} = \frac{\text{Median}_1}{\text{Median}_2} = \frac{\text{Angle Bisector}_1}{\text{Angle Bisector}_2}$.
