Theorem 10.1

"A tangent to a circle is perpendicular to the radius through the point of contact." — The fundamental right-angle cornerstone of circle geometry.

Vardaan Learning Institute Topic 01 — Foundations 04
Concept 1.1 Circle & Line Relationships: Non-Intersecting, Secant & Tangent
Three Possible Geometric Cases
  • Case 1 (Non-Intersecting Line): The straight line has $0$ points in common with the circle ($d > r$).
  • Case 2 (Secant Line): The line cuts through the circle at exactly two distinct points $A$ and $B$ ($d < r$).
  • Case 3 (Tangent Line): The line touches the circle at only one single point $P$. Point $P$ is called the Point of Contact ($d = r$).
Key Axiom for Board Exams

A tangent is a limiting case of a secant when the two end points of its corresponding chord coincide into a single point.

At any single point on a circle, there exists ONE and only ONE tangent.
Vardaan Learning Institute Topic 01 — Theorem 10.1 05
Theorem 10.1 The Tangent-Radius Perpendicularity Theorem
Formal NCERT Theorem Statement

"The tangent at any point of a circle is perpendicular to the radius through the point of contact."

$$\mathbf{OP \perp XY \implies \angle OPX = \angle OPY = 90^\circ}$$
Core Proof Idea (Shortest Distance Principle)
  1. Take any point $Q$ on $XY$ other than $P$. $Q$ must lie outside the circle.
  2. Therefore, $OQ > \text{radius } OP \implies OP$ is the shortest distance from center $O$ to line $XY$.
  3. The shortest distance from a point to a straight line is always the perpendicular distance. Hence, $OP \perp XY$. [Hence Proved]
Radius $OP$ is the unique shortest segment from $O$ to line $XY$.

Vardaan Learning Institute Topic 02 — Number of Tangents 09
Concept 2.1 Number of Tangents Determined by Point Location
Case A: Point $P$ Inside the Circle ($OP < r$)

Any straight line passing through an interior point cuts the circle twice. Hence, 0 tangents can be drawn from inside.

Case B: Point $P$ On the Circle ($OP = r$)

There is exactly 1 unique tangent touching at point $P$, perpendicular to radius $OP$.

Case C: Point $P$ Outside the Circle ($OP > r$)

There are exactly 2 tangents ($PT_1$ and $PT_2$) drawn from point $P$ to the circle.

Point location strictly dictates whether 0, 1, or 2 tangents exist.
Vardaan Learning Institute Topic 02 — Theorem 10.2 10
Theorem 10.2 Equal Tangent Segments Theorem ($AP = AQ$) • Complete Proof
Theorem Statement (CBSE Board Classic)

"The lengths of tangents drawn from an external point to a circle are equal."

Formal Proof via RHS Congruence
  1. Given: Circle with center $O$; $P$ and $Q$ points of contact of tangents from external point $A$.
  2. To Prove: $AP = AQ$.
  3. Construction: Join $OP, OQ$, and $OA$.
  4. In right triangles $\triangle OPA$ and $\triangle OQA$:
    • $\angle OPA = \angle OQA = 90^\circ$ (Theorem 10.1)
    • Hypotenuse $OA = OA$ (Common side)
    • $OP = OQ$ (Radii of same circle)
  5. $\therefore \triangle OPA \cong \triangle OQA$ by RHS Congruence Criterion.
  6. $\implies \mathbf{AP = AQ}$ (by CPCTC). [Hence Proved]
$\triangle OPA \cong \triangle OQA \implies AP = AQ$
Vardaan Learning Institute Topic 02 — Corollaries 11
Corollaries The Twin Symmetries of External Tangents
Corollary 1: Equal Central Subtended Angles

The two tangents subtend equal angles at the center of the circle:

$$\mathbf{\angle AOP = \angle AOQ}$$

Direct consequence of $\triangle OPA \cong \triangle OQA$ via CPCTC.

Corollary 2: Angle Bisector of Tangents

The segment joining the center to the external point bisects the angle between the two tangents:

$$\mathbf{\angle OAP = \angle OAQ = \frac{1}{2}\angle PAQ}$$
Why This Matters in Numerical Questions

Whenever an angle between tangents $\angle PAQ$ is given (e.g., $60^\circ$ or $80^\circ$):
• $\triangle OPA$ is instantly resolved as a $30^\circ-60^\circ-90^\circ$ right triangle.
• Tangent lengths and chords can be determined instantly using simple trigonometric ratios!

Vardaan Learning Institute Topic 03 — Supplementary Angles 15
Concept 3.1 Supplementary Center Angle Theorem ($\angle APB + \angle AOB = 180^\circ$)
Theorem Statement (NCERT Ex 10.2 Q4)

"Prove that the angle between the two tangents drawn from an external point to a circle is supplementary to the angle subtended by the line-segment joining the points of contact at the center."

Simple 3-Line Proof

1. In quadrilateral $PAOB$, sum of interior angles is $360^\circ$: $$\angle APB + \angle OAP + \angle AOB + \angle OBP = 360^\circ$$ 2. Since radius $\perp$ tangent: $\angle OAP = 90^\circ$ and $\angle OBP = 90^\circ$. $$\angle APB + 90^\circ + \angle AOB + 90^\circ = 360^\circ$$ $$\mathbf{\angle APB + \angle AOB = 180^\circ} \quad \text{[Hence Proved]}$$

Master Shortcut Identity
$$\mathbf{\angle AOB = 180^\circ - \angle APB}$$
Instant MCQ Example

If $\angle APB = 70^\circ$, then: $$\angle AOB = 180^\circ - 70^\circ = \mathbf{110^\circ}$$ If $\angle AOB = 125^\circ$, then: $$\angle APB = 180^\circ - 125^\circ = \mathbf{55^\circ}$$

Vardaan Learning Institute Topic 03 — Concentric Circles 16
Concept 3.2 Concentric Circles: Chord of Larger Circle Touching Inner Circle
The Bisection Property

Let two concentric circles have radii $R$ and $r$ ($R > r$) with common center $O$. Let chord $AB$ of the outer circle be a tangent to the inner circle at point $P$:

  • By Theorem 10.1, radius $OP \perp AB$.
  • From Class 9 geometry, the perpendicular drawn from the center of a circle to a chord bisects the chord!
  • Therefore: $\mathbf{AP = PB = \frac{1}{2}AB}$.
$$\mathbf{AB = 2\sqrt{R^2 - r^2}}$$
In right $\triangle OPA$: $AP = \sqrt{R^2 - r^2} \implies AB = 2AP$

Vardaan Learning Institute Topic 04 — Circumscribed Quadrilateral 20
Board Theorem Quadrilateral Circumscribing a Circle: $\mathbf{AB + CD = AD + BC}$
Formal Board Proof (Theorem 10.2 Application)
  1. Let quadrilateral $ABCD$ circumscribe a circle touching sides at $P, Q, R, S$.
  2. By Theorem 10.2, tangents from each external vertex are equal:
    • From $A$: $AP = AS \quad \text{--- (1)}$
    • From $B$: $BP = BQ \quad \text{--- (2)}$
    • From $C$: $CR = CQ \quad \text{--- (3)}$
    • From $D$: $DR = DS \quad \text{--- (4)}$
  3. Adding column-wise: $(AP + BP) + (CR + DR) = (AS + DS) + (BQ + CQ)$
  4. Since $AP+BP = AB$, $CR+DR = CD$, $AS+DS = AD$, and $BQ+CQ = BC$: $$\mathbf{AB + CD = AD + BC} \quad \text{[Hence Proved]}$$
True Incircle touching all 4 sides at $P, Q, R, S$ with zero gap.
Vardaan Learning Institute Topic 04 — Parallelogram is Rhombus 21
Deduction 4.1 Parallelogram Circumscribing a Circle is a Rhombus
NCERT Ex 10.2 Q11 • 3-Mark Board Step-by-Step
  1. Let $ABCD$ be a parallelogram circumscribing a circle. Opposite sides of a parallelogram are equal: $$AB = CD \quad \text{and} \quad AD = BC$$
  2. By the Circumscribed Quadrilateral Theorem proved on Slide 20: $$AB + CD = AD + BC$$
  3. Substitute $CD = AB$ and $BC = AD$: $$AB + AB = AD + AD \implies 2AB = 2AD \implies \mathbf{AB = AD}$$
  4. Since adjacent sides are equal in a parallelogram ($AB = AD$), all 4 sides are equal: $$AB = BC = CD = DA$$ $$\mathbf{\therefore ABCD \text{ is a Rhombus.}} \quad \text{[Hence Proved]}$$
Parallelogram + Incircle
$\Downarrow$
RHOMBUS
Board Marking Tip

In board exams, do NOT just state $AB + CD = AD + BC$. First derive it in 3 steps using $AP=AS, BP=BQ, CR=CQ, DR=DS$ to secure all 3 marks!

Vardaan Learning Institute Topic 04 — Central Angles 22
Theorem 4.2 Opposite Sides Subtend Supplementary Angles at Center
NCERT Ex 10.2 Q13 (CBSE 2017, 2023)

Opposite sides of a quadrilateral circumscribing a circle subtend supplementary angles at the center of the circle:

$$\mathbf{\angle AOB + \angle COD = 180^\circ} \quad \text{and} \quad \mathbf{\angle BOC + \angle AOD = 180^\circ}$$
Proof Outline (Pairing 8 Central Angles)

By SSS congruence of tangent triangles at each vertex: $\angle 1 = \angle 8$, $\angle 2 = \angle 3$, $\angle 4 = \angle 5$, $\angle 6 = \angle 7$.
Sum around $O$ is $360^\circ \implies 2(\angle 1+\angle 2) + 2(\angle 5+\angle 6) = 360^\circ$.
$\implies \angle AOB + \angle COD = 180^\circ$. [Hence Proved]

8 central angles in pairs $\implies \angle AOB + \angle COD = 180^\circ$
Vardaan Learning Institute Topic 04 — Parallel Tangents Intercept 23
NCERT Q9 Parallel Tangents with Intercept Tangent ($\mathbf{\angle AOB = 90^\circ}$)
NCERT Ex 10.2 Q9 Problem Statement

$XY \parallel X'Y'$ are parallel tangents to a circle with center $O$. Another tangent $AB$ with point of contact $C$ intersects $XY$ at $A$ and $X'Y'$ at $B$. Prove that $\mathbf{\angle AOB = 90^\circ}$.

Elegant Proof by Angle Bisectors

1. Join $OC$. $\triangle OPA \cong \triangle OCA \implies \angle POA = \angle COA = x$.
2. Similarly, $\triangle OQB \cong \triangle OCB \implies \angle QOB = \angle COB = y$.
3. Since $POQ$ is a straight diameter line: $$2x + 2y = 180^\circ \implies x + y = 90^\circ$$ $$\mathbf{\angle AOB = \angle COA + \angle COB = 90^\circ} \quad \text{[Hence Proved]}$$

Angle bisector summation $\implies \angle AOB = 90^\circ$

Vardaan Learning Institute Topic 05 — Angle Doubling Theorem 26
Theorem 5.1 The Angle Doubling Theorem: $\mathbf{\angle PTQ = 2\angle OPQ}$
NCERT Example 2 • Step-by-Step Proof
  1. Let $\angle PTQ = \theta$.
  2. In $\triangle TPQ$, $TP = TQ$ (Theorem 10.2). Therefore, $\triangle TPQ$ is an isosceles triangle: $$\angle TPQ = \angle TQP = \frac{180^\circ - \theta}{2} = 90^\circ - \frac{\theta}{2}$$
  3. By Theorem 10.1, radius $OP \perp TP \implies \angle OPT = 90^\circ$.
  4. From the diagram, $\angle OPQ = \angle OPT - \angle TPQ$: $$\angle OPQ = 90^\circ - \left(90^\circ - \frac{\theta}{2}\right) = \frac{\theta}{2}$$
  5. Multiplying both sides by $2$: $$\mathbf{\theta = 2\angle OPQ \implies \angle PTQ = 2\angle OPQ} \quad \text{[Hence Proved]}$$
$\angle PTQ = \theta \implies \angle OPQ = \frac{\theta}{2} \implies \angle PTQ = 2\angle OPQ$
Vardaan Learning Institute Topic 05 — Landmark HOTS 5-Marker 27
NCERT Q12 Triangle Circumscribing an Incircle ($r = 4\text{ cm}$) • Setup
NCERT Ex 10.2 • Q12 NCERT 5-MARKER

A triangle $ABC$ is drawn to circumscribe a circle of radius $r = 4\text{ cm}$ such that the segments $BD$ and $DC$ into which $BC$ is divided by the point of contact $D$ are of lengths $8\text{ cm}$ and $6\text{ cm}$ respectively. Find sides $AB$ and $AC$.

Vertex Tangent Symmetry (Theorem 10.2)
  • From vertex $C$: $CE = CD = 6\text{ cm}$.
  • From vertex $B$: $BF = BD = 8\text{ cm}$.
  • From vertex $A$: Let $AF = AE = x\text{ cm}$.
  • Sides: $a = 14\text{ cm}$, $b = x + 6\text{ cm}$, $c = x + 8\text{ cm}$.
True Incircle touching sides $BC, AC, AB$ at $D, E, F$.
Vardaan Learning Institute Topic 05 — Dual-Area Method 28
Dual-Area Proof Equating Inradius Area with Heron's Formula Area
Method A: Sum of 3 Sub-Triangles ($\text{Area} = r \times s$)

Semi-perimeter: $s = \frac{14 + (x+6) + (x+8)}{2} = \frac{28 + 2x}{2} = 14 + x$.
$\text{Area}(\triangle ABC) = \text{Area}(\triangle OBC) + \text{Area}(\triangle OCA) + \text{Area}(\triangle OAB)$ $$= \frac{1}{2}r(a + b + c) = r \cdot s = 4(14 + x) = \mathbf{56 + 4x}$$

Method B: Heron's Formula

$s - a = (14+x) - 14 = x$,   $s - b = (14+x) - (x+6) = 8$,   $s - c = 6$. $$\text{Area} = \sqrt{s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)} = \sqrt{(14+x) \cdot x \cdot 8 \cdot 6} = \mathbf{\sqrt{48x(14+x)}}$$

Equating & Solving for $x$

$$4(14 + x) = \sqrt{48x(14 + x)}$$ Squaring both sides: $$16(14 + x)^2 = 48x(14 + x)$$ Divide both sides by $16(14 + x)$ (since $14 + x \ne 0$): $$14 + x = 3x \implies 2x = 14 \implies \mathbf{x = 7\text{ cm}}$$

$$\mathbf{AB = x + 8 = 15\text{ cm}} \quad \text{and} \quad \mathbf{AC = x + 6 = 13\text{ cm}}$$
Vardaan Learning Institute Topic 05 — Right Triangle Incircle 29
High-Speed Formula Right-Angled Triangle Inradius Formula: $\mathbf{r = \frac{a + b - c}{2}}$
Derivation via Corner Square at Right Angle

Let a circle of radius $r$ be inscribed in a right triangle with legs $a, b$ and hypotenuse $c$:

  • At the right-angle vertex, the two contact points and center form a square of side $r$.
  • Tangent segments from the two acute vertices are $(a - r)$ and $(b - r)$.
  • Hypotenuse $c = (a - r) + (b - r) = a + b - 2r$.
  • Solving for $r$: $\mathbf{2r = a + b - c \implies r = \frac{a + b - c}{2}}$.
$$\mathbf{r = \frac{\text{Base} + \text{Perpendicular} - \text{Hypotenuse}}{2}}$$
Instant Mental Math Examples

1. Sides 6, 8, 10: $$r = \frac{6 + 8 - 10}{2} = \frac{4}{2} = \mathbf{2\text{ cm}}$$ 2. Sides 5, 12, 13: $$r = \frac{5 + 12 - 13}{2} = \frac{4}{2} = \mathbf{2\text{ cm}}$$ 3. Sides 8, 15, 17: $$r = \frac{8 + 15 - 17}{2} = \frac{6}{2} = \mathbf{3\text{ cm}}$$

Vardaan Learning Institute Exam Traps 31
Caution Common Board Exam Mistakes & How to Avoid Them
⚠ Warning: The Top 3 Red-Flag Errors in CBSE Class 10 Circles

Over 35% of lost marks in Chapter 10 come from misinterpreting which angles are supplementary or forgetting Theorem 10.1 perpendicularity.

❌ WRONG Understandings
  • Writing that opposite angles of a circumscribed quadrilateral are supplementary ($\angle A + \angle C = 180^\circ$). FALSE! Only the angles subtended at the center are supplementary!
  • Assuming any line touching a circle is tangent without verifying radius $\perp$ line.
  • Using $AP=AQ$ when $P$ and $Q$ are NOT points of contact on the circle.
✔ CORRECT Approaches
  • $\angle AOB + \angle COD = 180^\circ$ (central angles from opposite sides).
  • Always state "Theorem 10.1: radius $\perp$ tangent at point of contact" in step 1 of any calculation.
  • For circumscribed polygons, add equations column-wise ensuring matching adjacent segments align into whole sides ($AP+BP=AB$).
