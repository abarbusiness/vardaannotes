Chapter 6: Democracy

NCERT Solutions

Questions and Activities

1. Read the passage and answer the questions that follow:

A school cabinet was responsible for planning a Republic Day celebration, and members had different opinions on which activities to include. After a discussion, they decided to vote, and the activities supported by the majority were selected.

a. Which democratic values are reflected in this situation?
Answer
The values reflected in this situation include consultation, shared discussion, freedom of expression, collective decision-making, and respecting the opinion of the majority.
b. How does voting help resolve differences of opinion?
Answer
Voting provides a fair, peaceful, and equal method (one person, one vote) to make a collective decision when people disagree. It ensures that the idea supported by the largest number of people is adopted, avoiding conflict.
c. Why is the majority decision important in a democracy?
Answer
The majority decision is important because it reflects the will of the largest number of people, which is the core principle of popular sovereignty—that the ultimate source of power lies with the people.
d. Mention responsibilities of elected representatives after a decision is made.
Answer
After a decision is made, elected representatives have the responsibility to respect the outcome, implement the decision efficiently, ensure accountability, and maintain transparency in the execution of the selected activities.
2. How would democracy be affected if citizens stopped following laws and civic responsibilities? Explain with an example.
Answer
Democracy relies heavily on the active participation and civic responsibility of its citizens. If citizens stop following laws (violating the Rule of Law) and ignore civic duties (like voting, respecting others' rights, or staying informed), the democratic system weakens and can lead to chaos or authoritarianism.

Example: If citizens stop paying taxes or start damaging public property, the government will not have the funds or resources to maintain infrastructure, schools, and hospitals, leading to public suffering and instability. Similarly, if citizens irresponsibly spread fake news on social media, it can create confusion and conflict in society.
3. Which situation best reflects the idea of popular sovereignty and why?
  1. A law is passed without public discussion
  2. Citizens vote to choose their representatives
  3. A court gives a final judgment
  4. A minister takes an independent decision
Answer
(b) Citizens vote to choose their representatives

Reason: Popular sovereignty means that the ultimate source of power lies with the people, and the state derives its authority from them. When citizens vote, they are actively exercising this power to delegate authority to their chosen representatives to govern on their behalf.
4. The Rule of Law is violated when:
  1. Laws are applied equally to all
  2. Courts review government actions
  3. Powerful individuals are treated above the law
  4. Citizens challenge laws through legal means
Answer
(c) Powerful individuals are treated above the law
5. Why is the Rule of Law essential for protecting democracy?
Answer
The Rule of Law is essential because it guarantees equality before the law and equal protection of the law. It ensures that no individual, regardless of their power or status, is above the law. It protects citizens' fundamental rights from arbitrary state action and ensures that disputes are resolved fairly through established procedures, thereby promoting justice, accountability, and public trust in the democratic system.
6. Is voting alone enough to make a country democratic? Support your answer with examples from the chapter.
Answer
No, voting alone is not enough to make a country fully democratic. While holding free and fair elections (Universal Adult Franchise) is a fundamental requirement, a true democracy must also include:
  • A Constitution: To guarantee Fundamental Rights and duties.
  • Rule of Law: Ensuring everyone is equal under the law.
  • Separation of Powers: Checks and balances between the Legislature, Executive, and Judiciary.
  • Accountability & Transparency: Tools like the Right to Information (RTI) Act empower citizens beyond elections.
  • Grassroots Participation: As seen in the Jethipura Gram Panchayat example, where Special Gram Sabhas ensured active participation in development.
7. Social media allows people to express opinions freely.
a. How can this strengthen democracy?
Answer
Social media strengthens democracy by providing a fast and accessible platform for citizens to raise public issues, engage in debate, hold the government accountable, and rapidly spread awareness. It acts as an extension of the "fourth pillar of democracy."
b. How can it weaken democracy if used irresponsibly?
Answer
If used irresponsibly, social media can severely weaken democracy by spreading misinformation and fake news. This can manipulate public opinion, create societal confusion, and incite conflicts based on religion, caste, or region, thereby damaging the social fabric.
8. Write a paragraph on what democracy means to you as a young citizen.
Answer
As a young citizen, democracy means having the freedom to express my ideas, ask questions, and eventually use my vote to shape the future of my country. It means living in a society where every person, regardless of their background, is treated equally under the Rule of Law and has their Fundamental Rights protected. It also reminds me of my civic responsibilities—to stay informed, respect diverse opinions, participate in community service, and responsibly consume media. Democracy makes me feel that my voice and my actions genuinely matter in building our nation.
9. Conduct a model parliament session or Gram Sabha in class on local issues—cleanliness, responsibilities towards public property, or school safety.
Activity Guide
(This is a practical classroom activity. Here is a guide on how to conduct it):
  1. Assign Roles: Elect a Sarpanch/Speaker, a few Panches/Ministers, and let the rest of the class act as active citizens/opposition.
  2. Set the Agenda: Pick a topic, for example, "Improving School Cleanliness".
  3. Debate & Discuss: Allow citizens to raise concerns (e.g., "The playground is littered after recess"). Let the ministers propose solutions.
  4. Voting: Call for a vote on proposed solutions (e.g., forming a weekly student cleaning committee) using a show of hands.
  5. Record Decisions: Note down the final decisions passed by the majority, demonstrating democratic decision-making in action.
10. Choose any one democratic institution in India (such as Parliament, Election Commission, Judiciary, or Panchayat). Prepare a short note explaining: a. Its role in democracy b. How it ensures accountability and participation.
Answer (Example: Election Commission of India)
Institution Chosen: The Election Commission of India (ECI)

a. Its role in democracy: The ECI is an independent body responsible for conducting free, fair, and periodic elections across the country. Since India is a representative democracy, the ECI's role is foundational in allowing citizens to choose their government.

b. How it ensures accountability and participation: It ensures participation by constantly updating voter rolls (enabling Universal Adult Franchise), setting up over a million polling booths (even for single voters in remote areas), and providing election symbols so that citizens who cannot read can vote independently. It ensures accountability by strictly enforcing the Model Code of Conduct, ensuring that political parties do not misuse power and compete fairly during elections.
11. What role does the Constitution play in strengthening democracy in India?
Answer
The Constitution acts as the supreme rulebook that limits the power of the government and protects the citizens. It strengthens democracy by:
  • Guaranteeing Fundamental Rights, preventing discrimination based on religion, race, caste, sex, or place of birth.
  • Establishing the Rule of Law.
  • Setting up the Separation of Powers among the Legislature, Executive, and Judiciary to maintain checks and balances.
  • Providing for Universal Adult Franchise, ensuring every citizen aged 18 and above has the right to vote.
12. What challenges do you think democracy in India faces today?
Answer
Today, democracy in India faces several challenges:
  • Socio-economic Barriers: Issues like poverty, illiteracy, gender inequality, and regionalism act as barriers to equal participation.
  • Misinformation: The rapid spread of fake news, especially through social media and digital platforms, creates confusion and can lead to conflict.
  • Implementation Gaps: Inadequate or ineffective implementation of laws and policies can reduce public trust in democratic institutions.
These challenges require constant care, civic awareness, and active participation from citizens to overcome.
