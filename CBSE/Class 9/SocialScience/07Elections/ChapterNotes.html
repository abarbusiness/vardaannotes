Elections

1. Why do Elections Matter?

Elections are the core process for exercising democratic rights. They ensure that a government does not continue beyond its prescribed term without a fresh mandate from the people.

  • Direct Elections: Citizens vote directly to choose their representatives (e.g., Lok Sabha, Vidhan Sabha, Panchayats, Municipal Corporations).
  • Indirect Elections: Citizens vote for representatives who then choose/elect leaders on their behalf (e.g., President, Vice President, Rajya Sabha).
Psephology

Derived from the Greek words psephos (meaning 'pebble') and logy (meaning 'systematic study'). In ancient Greece, pebbles were used for voting. Today, it refers to the scientific study of elections.

2. The Electoral System

The first step in conducting elections is deciding how votes are converted into seats. In India, we use two main systems:

First-Past-The-Post (FPTP) System

Also known as the plurality system. Used for electing members to the Lok Sabha and the Vidhan Sabha. The candidate who receives the most votes in a constituency wins, even if they secure less than 50% of the total votes.

Proportional Representation (PR)

Used for elections to the Rajya Sabha, the President, and the Vice President. Seats are allotted in proportion to the total votes received.

Six states in India (Andhra Pradesh, Bihar, Karnataka, Maharashtra, Telangana, Uttar Pradesh) have a bicameral legislature with an Upper House (Vidhan Parishad or Legislative Council). Elections to the Vidhan Parishad are held using the PR system by means of a Single Transferable Vote.

3. Inside India's Election Machinery: The Laws

For elections to be free and fair, a robust machinery and clear laws are required. The major legislations include:

  • Representation of the People Act, 1950: Deals with the allocation of seats, delimitation of constituencies, and preparation of electoral rolls. Ensures every citizen above 18 has the right to vote.
  • Representation of the People Act, 1951: Deals with nomination of candidates, election campaigns, voting procedures, and resolving disputes. It lists electoral offences (e.g., bribing, appealing for votes based on religion/caste, using government personnel).
  • The Presidential and Vice-Presidential Act, 1952.
Let's Explore Delimitation Commission:

The boundaries of parliamentary and assembly constituencies change over time depending on population shifts. Delimitation is the process of fixing the boundaries to ensure the ratio of seats to population is equal across constituencies. (Article 82 of the Constitution mandates this).

4. Election Commission of India (ECI)

The ECI is an autonomous, permanent constitutional body established on 25 January 1950 (celebrated as National Voters' Day). Article 324 vests the superintendence, direction, and control of elections in the ECI.

At the state level, election work is supervised by the Chief Electoral Officer (CEO), and at the district level by District Magistrates acting as District Election Officers.

Functions of the ECI:

  1. Creates the Electoral Roll: Collects data on eligible voters (>18 years). Conducts Special Intensive Revision (SIR) to update, verify, and correct rolls (adding new 18-year-olds, deleting deceased/shifted voters).
  2. Decides Schedule and Date: Considers weather, agricultural cycles, exams, and festivals before declaring the election timeline.
  3. Registers Political Parties & Allocates Symbols: Classifies parties as national, state/regional, or registered-unrecognised. Allocates election symbols so even those who cannot read can vote independently. Ensures inner-party democracy and acts as a quasi-judicial body in disputes.

5. Ensuring Free and Fair Elections through Technology

If fairness is compromised (e.g., by bribery or inaccurate counting), public confidence weakens. The ECI has introduced several digital initiatives to make elections transparent and inclusive:

Figure 7.6: Diagram of Digital Ecosystem for electoral services
  • ETPBS (Electronically Transmitted Postal Ballot System): Enables service voters (like armed forces) to receive and cast postal ballots electronically from anywhere.
  • cVIGIL App: Allows voters to instantly report Model Code of Conduct violations. Flying squads navigate to the spot using auto-location.
  • Saksham App: Eases voter registration and helps find polling stations for Persons with Disabilities (PwDs).
  • Suvidha: An app for candidates to handle nominations and apply for campaign permissions.
  • Voter Helpline App: For citizens to register, verify details, and get election news.
  • Voting from Home: Introduced in the 2024 General Elections for senior citizens (>85 years) and PwDs (with >40% benchmark disability).

Don't Miss Out IEVP

The International Election Visitors' Programme (IEVP) is a flagship programme of the ECI for international cooperation. The ECI has signed MoUs with 28 Election Management Bodies (EMBs) and international organisations like IFES, International IDEA, and the United Nations.

6. Political Parties

Political parties play a fundamental role by presenting diverse viewpoints through their programmes, policies, and manifestos. They help voters make informed choices and ensure that democracy functions effectively by providing governance, representation, and accountability.

The Multi-Party System & Defection

India has a multi-party system reflecting its rich diversity. However, sometimes elected representatives switch parties. Defection refers to abandoning or switching from the political party under whose banner a candidate was elected. It is often considered political opportunism.

Let's Explore Anti-Defection Law (1985):

Passed through the 52nd Constitutional Amendment Act, it aims to prevent defection and provide stability to the elected government. If a member voluntarily gives up party membership or votes against the party's whip, they can be disqualified by the Speaker/Chairman of the House.

Recognition of Political Parties

The ECI registers and recognises political parties based on specific criteria:

  • National Party: Needs either 6% of valid votes in 4+ states (Lok Sabha/Assembly) + 4 Lok Sabha seats, OR 2% of Lok Sabha seats from at least 3 states, OR recognition as a State Party in at least 4 states.
  • State Party: Needs either 6% valid votes in Assembly + 2 Assembly seats, OR 6% valid votes in Lok Sabha + 1 Lok Sabha seat, OR 3% of Assembly seats (or 3 seats, whichever is more), OR 1 Lok Sabha seat for every 25 allotted to the state.
  • Registered Unrecognised Political Parties (RUPP): Parties that have not secured enough votes or have never contested elections since registration.

7. Challenges to Free and Fair Elections

Conducting elections for over 96.8 crore voters (as of 2024) across thousands of polling stations is a mammoth task. Elections face significant challenges such as:

  • Misinformation and Fake News
  • Intimidation of voters
  • Socio-economic realities that may hinder equal participation

The ECI addresses these challenges through the stringent Model Code of Conduct, using secure EVMs and VVPAT (Voter Verifiable Paper Audit Trail), conducting voter awareness campaigns, and encouraging active citizen participation.
