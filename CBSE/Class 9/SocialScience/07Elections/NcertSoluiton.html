Chapter 7: Elections

NCERT Solutions
Q1. Why do we need elections in a democracy? State any three reasons.
Answer

Elections are considered essential for any representative democracy for the following reasons:

  • Selection of Representatives: In large countries, it is impossible for all citizens to sit together every day and take all the decisions. Therefore, elections allow people to choose representatives who will make laws and govern on their behalf.
  • Accountability: Elections ensure that a government does not continue beyond its prescribed term without a fresh mandate. If the voters are unhappy with the performance of the ruling party, they can vote them out in the next election.
  • Policy Choice: Through elections, voters get a chance to choose the party whose policies will guide the government and law-making.
Q2. What is the difference between direct and indirect elections? Give one example of each in India.
Answer

Direct Elections:

Citizens vote directly to choose their representatives. The candidate who gets the most votes is elected directly by the people.

  • Example: Elections for the Members of Parliament (MPs) in the Lok Sabha, or Members of Legislative Assembly (MLAs) in the Vidhan Sabha.

Indirect Elections:

Citizens do not vote directly. Instead, they vote for representatives (like MPs and MLAs), and these elected representatives then vote to elect the leaders on behalf of the citizens.

  • Example: The election of the President of India or the members of the Rajya Sabha.
Q3. Differentiate between the First-Past-The-Post (FPTP) system and Proportional Representation (PR).
Answer
Feature First-Past-The-Post (FPTP) Proportional Representation (PR)
Winning Criteria The candidate with the most votes in a constituency wins, even if they secure less than 50% of the total votes. Seats are allotted to parties in proportion to the total percentage of votes they receive.
Focus of Vote Voters usually vote for a specific candidate. Voters usually vote for a political party.
Usage in India Used in Lok Sabha, Vidhan Sabha, and Panchayat elections. Used for electing the President, Vice President, Rajya Sabha, and Vidhan Parishad.
Q4. What is the Election Commission of India (ECI)? Mention any three of its main functions.
Answer

The Election Commission of India (ECI) is an autonomous, permanent constitutional body established on 25 January 1950. Article 324 of the Constitution vests it with the power of superintendence, direction, and control of elections in India.

Functions of the ECI:

  • Creates and Updates Electoral Rolls: It prepares the voters' list, ensuring every eligible citizen above 18 is registered and deceased/shifted voters are removed.
  • Decides Election Schedule: It plans the timeline of elections considering factors like weather, festivals, and school exams to ensure maximum participation.
  • Registers Political Parties: It grants recognition to national and state parties, allocates election symbols, and implements the Model Code of Conduct to ensure free and fair campaigning.
Q5. What is meant by the Delimitation Commission? Why is delimitation necessary?
Answer

The Delimitation Commission is a high-power body appointed by the President of India whose orders have the force of law. Its main task is to redraw the boundaries of parliamentary and assembly constituencies.

Why is it necessary?

Populations do not remain static; they shift over time due to birth rates and migration. Delimitation is the process of adjusting constituency boundaries to ensure that each constituency has roughly the same population. This ensures the principle of "One Person, One Vote, One Value", maintaining equal representation across all areas.

Q6. What role does technology play in ensuring free, fair, and transparent elections in India today? Give examples of apps/systems used by the ECI.
Answer

Technology has revolutionized the electoral process in India, making it faster, more inclusive, and less prone to rigging.

  • EVMs and VVPAT: Electronic Voting Machines ensure quick and secure recording of votes, preventing invalid votes. The Voter Verifiable Paper Audit Trail (VVPAT) allows voters to verify that their vote went to the intended candidate, ensuring transparency.
  • cVIGIL App: Empowers citizens to report violations of the Model Code of Conduct (like bribing voters) instantly with photos/videos, which are immediately acted upon using auto-location.
  • Saksham App: Designed to ease the voting process for Persons with Disabilities (PwDs), allowing them to request wheelchair assistance or find accessible polling stations.
  • ETPBS: Electronically Transmitted Postal Ballot System allows service personnel (like armed forces) to receive and cast their votes from remote borders.
Q7. What is a Political Party? What are the criteria for a party to be recognized as a National Party?
Answer

A Political Party is a group of people who come together to contest elections and hold power in the government. They agree on some policies and programmes for the society with a view to promote the collective good.

Criteria for a National Party:

According to the ECI, a political party is recognized as a National Party if it fulfills any one of the following conditions:

  • It secures at least 6% of the valid votes polled in four or more states (in Lok Sabha or State Assembly elections) AND wins at least 4 seats in the Lok Sabha.
  • It wins at least 2% of the total seats in the Lok Sabha, with candidates elected from at least 3 different states.
  • It is already recognized as a State Party in at least four states.
Q8. What is 'Defection'? How does the Anti-Defection Law aim to curb it?
Answer

Defection in politics means changing party allegiance from the party on whose ticket a person got elected (to a legislative body) to a different party. It is often driven by the lure of ministerial positions or money, leading to political instability.

The Anti-Defection Law (1985):

Passed via the 52nd Amendment to the Constitution, this law aims to prevent such political opportunism.

  • If an elected member voluntarily gives up the membership of their original political party, they can be disqualified.
  • If they vote (or abstain from voting) in the legislature contrary to the directions (whip) issued by their party, they stand disqualified by the Speaker or Chairman of the House.
  • This brings stability to elected governments by ensuring legislators remain loyal to the mandate given by the voters.
Q9. Despite a robust system, what are the major challenges to free and fair elections in India today?
Answer

Conducting elections for nearly a billion voters is a mammoth task. Major challenges include:

  • Money Power: Candidates and parties with immense wealth often enjoy an unfair advantage over smaller parties or independent candidates, heavily influencing voters through extravagant campaigns.
  • Misinformation and Fake News: The rapid spread of fake news and deepfakes via social media can manipulate voter behavior and create communal tension during elections.
  • Intimidation: In some regions, voters (especially from weaker sections) may face physical intimidation or coercion to vote for a particular candidate.
  • Criminalization of Politics: Candidates with serious criminal records sometimes secure party tickets, pushing out honest individuals from the electoral race.
