Topic 1: Circle Definitions, Locus & Key Components

Definition of Circle & Locus
  • Circle: The locus (set) of all points in a two-dimensional plane that are at a fixed constant distance from a fixed point.
  • Centre ($O$): The fixed point in the plane.
  • Radius ($r$): The fixed distance from the centre $O$ to any point on the boundary of the circle.
  • Chord ($BC$): A straight line segment joining any two points $B$ and $C$ on the circle.
  • Diameter ($d$): A chord passing through the centre $O$. It is the longest chord of a circle ($d = 2r$).

Immediate Solved Application

Example 1.1: Longest Chord Calculation NCERT

Question: Find the length of the longest chord in a circle of radius $r = 7.5\text{ cm}$.

Solution:

The longest chord of a circle is its diameter ($d = 2r$).

$$\text{Diameter } d = 2 \times 7.5\text{ cm} = \mathbf{15\text{ cm}}$$

Topic 2: Symmetries of a Circle & Number of Circles Through Points

Symmetry & Point Rules
  • Rotational Symmetry: Infinite / continuous rotational symmetry around centre $O$ through any angle $\theta$.
  • Reflection Symmetry: Every line containing a diameter is a line of reflection symmetry (infinitely many lines of symmetry).
  • Circles Through 1 Point: Infinitely many circles can pass through a single point $A$.
  • Circles Through 2 Points ($A, B$): Infinitely many circles can pass through $A$ and $B$. All their centres lie on the perpendicular bisector of line segment $AB$.
    Smallest Circle: The circle with $AB$ as diameter (radius $= \frac{1}{2}AB$).
  • Circles Through 3 Collinear Points: ZERO circles! (Because perpendicular bisectors of collinear segments are parallel and never intersect).
Fig 5.2: Infinitely Many Circles Pass Through Two Points $A$ and $B$ (Centres Lie on Perpendicular Bisector of $AB$)
Theorem 1: Unique Circle Through 3 Non-Collinear Points

Statement: There is one and only one circle passing through three non-collinear points $A, B, C$.

Given: Three non-collinear points $A, B, C$.

To Prove: A unique circle passes through $A, B, C$.

Proof:

  1. Join $AB$ and $BC$. Draw perpendicular bisector $L_1$ of $AB$ and $L_2$ of $BC$.
  2. Since $A, B, C$ are non-collinear, lines $AB$ and $BC$ are not parallel. Hence their perpendicular bisectors $L_1$ and $L_2$ must intersect at a unique point, say $O$.
  3. Since $O$ lies on $L_1$ (bisector of $AB$), $OA = OB$.
  4. Since $O$ lies on $L_2$ (bisector of $BC$), $OB = OC$.
  5. Therefore, $OA = OB = OC = R$. Taking $O$ as centre and radius $R = OA$, the circle passes through $A, B, C$. Since two non-parallel lines intersect at exactly ONE point, $O$ is unique. Thus, the circle is UNIQUE. $\blacksquare$

Topic 3: Chords & Angles Subtended at the Centre

Theorem 2: Equal Chords Subtend Equal Angles at Centre

Statement: Equal chords of a circle subtend equal angles at the centre.

Given: A circle with centre $C$. Chords $AB = DE$.

To Prove: $\angle ACB = \angle DCE$.

Proof:

In $\Delta CAB$ and $\Delta CDE$:

  • $CA = CD = r$ (Radii of same circle)
  • $CB = CE = r$ (Radii of same circle)
  • $AB = DE$ (Given equal chords)

By SSS Congruence Rule, $\Delta CAB \cong \Delta CDE$.

$$\implies \mathbf{\angle ACB = \angle DCE} \quad (\text{by CPCTC}) \quad \blacksquare$$
Fig 5.7: Proof Diagram for Theorem 2 & 3 — Equal Chords $AB = DE \iff \angle ACB = \angle DCE$
Theorem 3 (Converse): Subtended Angles Equal $\implies$ Chords Equal

Statement: If the angles subtended by two chords at the centre are equal, then the chords are equal in length.

Given: $\angle ACB = \angle DCE$ in circle with centre $C$.

To Prove: $AB = DE$.

Proof: In $\Delta CAB$ and $\Delta CDE$, $CA = CD = r$, $\angle ACB = \angle DCE$, $CB = CE = r$. By SAS Congruence Rule, $\Delta CAB \cong \Delta CDE \implies \mathbf{AB = DE}$ (CPCTC). $\blacksquare$

Immediate Solved Application

Example 3.1: Central Angle Calculation NCERT

Question: In a circle of radius $12\text{ cm}$, a central angle $\angle AOB = 60^\circ$. Find the length of chord $AB$.

Solution:

In $\Delta AOB$, $OA = OB = 12\text{ cm}$ (radii). Thus, $\Delta AOB$ is isosceles with $\angle OAB = \angle OBA$.

$$\angle OAB + \angle OBA = 180^\circ - 60^\circ = 120^\circ \implies \angle OAB = \angle OBA = 60^\circ$$

Since all three angles are $60^\circ$, $\Delta AOB$ is an equilateral triangle!

$$\mathbf{AB = OA = 12\text{ cm}}$$

Topic 4: Midpoints & Perpendicular Bisectors of Chords

Theorem 4: Line Joining Centre to Midpoint is Perpendicular

Statement: The line joining the centre of a circle to the midpoint of a chord is perpendicular to the chord.

Given: $M$ is midpoint of chord $AB$ ($AM = BM$) in circle with centre $C$.

To Prove: $CM \perp AB$ ($\angle CMA = \angle CMB = 90^\circ$).

Proof:

In $\Delta CMA$ and $\Delta CMB$:

  • $CA = CB = r$ (Radii)
  • $CM = CM$ (Common side)
  • $AM = BM$ (Given $M$ is midpoint)

By SSS congruence, $\Delta CMA \cong \Delta CMB \implies \angle CMA = \angle CMB$ (CPCTC).

Since $\angle CMA + \angle CMB = 180^\circ$ (linear pair) $\implies \mathbf{\angle CMA = \angle CMB = 90^\circ \implies CM \perp AB}$. $\blacksquare$

Fig 5.8: Proof Diagram for Theorem 4 & 5 — Perpendicular $CM \perp AB \iff AM = BM = L/2$
Theorem 5 (Converse): Perpendicular from Centre Bisects Chord

Statement: The perpendicular from the centre of a circle to a chord bisects the chord.

Proof: Right $\Delta CMA \cong \text{Right } \Delta CMB$ by RHS congruence ($CA = CB = r, CM = CM, \angle CMA = \angle CMB = 90^\circ$). $\implies \mathbf{AM = BM}$ (CPCTC). $\blacksquare$

$$\mathbf{\text{Baudhāyana-Pythagoras Chord Distance Relation:}}$$ $$\mathbf{r^2 = d^2 + \left(\frac{L}{2}\right)^2} \implies \mathbf{L = 2\sqrt{r^2 - d^2}}$$ where $r = \text{radius}$, $d = \text{perpendicular distance } CM$, $L = \text{chord length } AB$.

Immediate Solved Application

Example 4.1: Distance of Chord Calculation NCERT

Question: A chord of length $24\text{ cm}$ is drawn in a circle of diameter $26\text{ cm}$. Find the perpendicular distance of the chord from the centre.

Solution:

Radius $r = 26/2 = 13\text{ cm}$. Half chord $AM = 24/2 = 12\text{ cm}$.

$$r^2 = d^2 + AM^2 \implies 13^2 = d^2 + 12^2 \implies 169 = d^2 + 144 \implies d^2 = 25 \implies \mathbf{d = 5\text{ cm}}$$

Topic 5: Distance of Chords & Comparison of Unequal Chords

Theorem 6: Equal Chords are Equidistant from Centre

Statement: Chords of a circle having equal length are at equal distance from the centre.

Proof: Draw $CE \perp AB$ and $CH \perp FG$. By Theorem 5, $AE = AB/2$ and $FH = FG/2$. Since $AB = FG \implies AE = FH$. Right $\Delta CEA \cong \text{Right } \Delta CHF$ by RHS ($CA = CF = r, AE = FH, \angle CEA = \angle CHF = 90^\circ$). $\implies \mathbf{CE = CH}$. $\blacksquare$

Fig 5.9: Proof Diagram for Theorem 6 & 7 — Equal Chords $AB = FG \iff CE = CH$
Theorem 7 (Converse): Equidistant Chords are Equal

Statement: Chords that lie at the same distance from the centre are equal in length ($CE = CH \implies AB = FG$).

Theorem 8: Comparison of Unequal Chords

Statement: Among two unequal chords of a circle, the longer chord is closer to the centre.

Proof: $r^2 = CF^2 + AF^2 = CG^2 + GD^2$. If $AB > DE \implies AF > GD \implies AF^2 > GD^2 \implies CF^2 < CG^2 \implies \mathbf{CF < CG}$. $\blacksquare$

Fig 5.10: Proof Diagram for Theorem 8 — Longer Chord $AB > DE \implies \text{Distance } CF < CG$

Topic 6: Angles Subtended by an Arc & Cyclic Theorems

Theorem 9: Central Angle is Double Circumference Angle

Statement: The angle subtended by an arc at the centre of a circle is double the angle subtended by it at any point on the remaining part of the circle.

$$\mathbf{\angle AOB = 2 \angle ACB}$$

Proof:

Join $CO$ and extend it to point $E$. In $\Delta AOC$, $OA = OC = r \implies \angle OAC = \angle OCA$.

Exterior angle $\angle AOE = \angle OAC + \angle OCA = 2 \angle OCA$.

Similarly in $\Delta BOC$, exterior angle $\angle BOE = 2 \angle OCB$.

$$\angle AOB = \angle AOE + \angle BOE = 2 \angle OCA + 2 \angle OCB = 2(\angle OCA + \angle OCB) = \mathbf{2 \angle ACB} \quad \blacksquare$$
Fig 5.11: Proof Diagram for Theorem 9 — Central Angle $\angle AOB = 2 \angle ACB$ with Ray $COE$
$$\mathbf{\text{Corollary 1: Angles in the Same Segment are Equal } (\angle ADB = \angle AEB)}$$ $$\mathbf{\text{Corollary 2: Angle in a Semicircle is a Right Angle } (90^\circ)}$$
Fig 5.12-5.13: Corollary Diagrams — Angle in a Semicircle ($90^\circ$) & Angles in the Same Segment ($\angle ADB = \angle AEB$)

Immediate Solved Application

Example 6.1: Angle Subtended by Arc NCERT

Question: An arc subtends $70^\circ$ at the centre of a circle. Find the measure of the angle subtended by this arc at any point on the remaining part of the circle.

Solution:

By Theorem 9, $\text{Angle at circumference} = \frac{1}{2} \times \text{Central Angle}$.

$$\text{Angle} = \frac{70^\circ}{2} = \mathbf{35^\circ}$$

Topic 7: Concyclicity of Points & Cyclic Quadrilaterals

Concyclicity Rules
  • Concyclic Points: Four or more points lying on the same circle.
  • Theorem 10: If a line segment joining two points subtends equal angles at two other points on the same side, the 4 points are concyclic.
  • Cyclic Quadrilateral: A quadrilateral whose all 4 vertices lie on a circle.
Theorem 11: Sum of Opposite Angles of Cyclic Quad is 180°

Statement: The sum of either pair of opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral is $180^\circ$.

$$\mathbf{\angle A + \angle C = 180^\circ} \qquad \mathbf{\angle B + \angle D = 180^\circ}$$

Proof:

Let $ABCD$ be a cyclic quadrilateral in circle with centre $O$.

By Theorem 9, $\angle BAD = \frac{1}{2}(\text{Reflex } \angle BOD)$ and $\angle BCD = \frac{1}{2}(\text{Angle } \angle BOD)$.

$$\angle BAD + \angle BCD = \frac{1}{2}(\text{Reflex } \angle BOD + \text{Angle } \angle BOD) = \frac{1}{2}(360^\circ) = \mathbf{180^\circ} \quad \blacksquare$$
Fig 5.14: Proof Diagram for Theorem 11 — Cyclic Quadrilateral $ABCD$ with Central Angles $\angle BOD$
$$\mathbf{\text{Exterior Angle Property: } \text{Exterior Angle} = \text{Interior Opposite Angle}}$$ $$\mathbf{\text{Brahmagupta's Cyclic Quadrilateral Area Formula: } \text{Area} = \sqrt{(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)(s-d)}}$$

Immediate Solved Application

Example 7.1: Opposite Angles of Cyclic Quad NCERT Ex 5.6

Question: In cyclic quadrilateral $ABCD$, $\angle A = 75^\circ$ and $\angle B = 110^\circ$. Find $\angle C$ and $\angle D$.

Solution:

$\angle A + \angle C = 180^\circ \implies 75^\circ + \angle C = 180^\circ \implies \mathbf{\angle C = 105^\circ}$.

$\angle B + \angle D = 180^\circ \implies 110^\circ + \angle D = 180^\circ \implies \mathbf{\angle D = 70^\circ}$.

Topic 8: Case Studies & Exam HOTS Problems

Case Study 1: Parallel Chords Geometry CASE STUDY

Two parallel chords of lengths $10\text{ cm}$ and $24\text{ cm}$ are on the same side of the centre of a circle. The distance between the chords is $7\text{ cm}$. Find the radius $r$ of the circle.

Solution:

Let perpendicular distance of $24\text{ cm}$ chord from centre be $x$. Distance of $10\text{ cm}$ chord $= x + 7$.

Half chords are $12\text{ cm}$ and $5\text{ cm}$.

$$r^2 = x^2 + 12^2 = x^2 + 144$$ $$r^2 = (x + 7)^2 + 5^2 = x^2 + 14x + 49 + 25 = x^2 + 14x + 74$$

Equating: $x^2 + 144 = x^2 + 14x + 74 \implies 14x = 70 \implies \mathbf{x = 5\text{ cm}}$.

$$r^2 = 5^2 + 144 = 25 + 144 = 169 \implies \mathbf{r = 13\text{ cm}}$$
Fig 5.15: Case Study 1 — Parallel Chords $24\text{ cm}$ and $10\text{ cm}$ on Same Side of Centre ($r = 13\text{ cm}$)
HOTS 1: Area of Cyclic Quadrilateral with Sides 5, 5, 12, 12 HOTS / Brahmagupta

Question: A cyclic quadrilateral has sides $a=5, b=5, c=12, d=12\text{ units}$. Calculate its area using Brahmagupta's formula.

Solution:

Semi-perimeter $s = \frac{5 + 5 + 12 + 12}{2} = \mathbf{17}$.

$$\text{Area} = \sqrt{(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)(s-d)} = \sqrt{(17-5)(17-5)(17-12)(17-12)}$$ $$= \sqrt{12 \times 12 \times 5 \times 5} = 12 \times 5 = \mathbf{60\text{ sq units}}$$

9. 100/100 Quick Revision Checklist

Master Checklist
  • Unique Circle: 1 unique circle passes through 3 non-collinear points. Zero through 3 collinear points.
  • Equal Chords: Equal chords subtend equal central angles and are equidistant from centre.
  • Perpendicular Bisector: Perpendicular from centre bisects chord ($r^2 = d^2 + (L/2)^2$).
  • Angle at Centre: $\angle AOB = 2 \angle ACB$. Angle in semicircle $= 90^\circ$.
  • Cyclic Quad: Opposite angles add up to $180^\circ$. Exterior angle $=$ Interior opposite angle.
