Civics Chapter 4: Elections (Class Notes)

1. Meaning of Elections

Concept

An election is a formal democratic process by which citizens select their representatives to govern and hold public office.

  • Universal Adult Franchise (Article 326): Every citizen of India aged 18 years or above has the right to vote equally without discrimination on grounds of religion, race, caste, sex, or place of birth. (Voting age lowered from 21 to 18 by the 61st Amendment Act, 1988).
  • One Person, One Vote, One Value: Every registered voter casts one vote with identical democratic weight.
  • Secret Ballot: Votes are marked inside a private voting compartment to safeguard voter anonymity and prevent intimidation or bribery.

2. Election Commission of India (ECI)

Composition (Article 324)
  • Structure: Multi-member body with 1 Chief Election Commissioner (CEC) and 2 Election Commissioners (ECs), appointed by the President of India.
  • Tenure: 6 years or until the age of 65 years, whichever is earlier.
  • Status: Enjoy status, salary, and perks equal to a Supreme Court Judge; decisions are taken by majority.

A. Safeguards Ensuring Independence

Constitutional Autonomy
  • Security of Tenure: The CEC cannot be removed at the President's pleasure. Removal requires the same rigorous procedure as a Supreme Court Judge — an address by both Houses of Parliament passed with a two-thirds majority on grounds of proved misbehaviour or incapacity.
  • Protection of ECs: Other Election Commissioners cannot be removed except on the recommendation of the CEC.
  • Non-votable Service Conditions: Salaries and allowances are charged on the Consolidated Fund of India and cannot be varied to their disadvantage after appointment.

B. Key Functions of the Election Commission

Category Core Functions (Syllabus Highlights)
Administrative
  • Prepares, revises, and publishes Electoral Rolls; issues Voter ID cards (EPIC).
  • Notifies dates and schedules; scrutinizes candidate nomination papers.
  • Accredits political parties and allocates reserved election symbols.
  • Enforces the Model Code of Conduct (MCC) to ensure clean, bribe-free campaigns.
  • Orders repoll or countermands elections in case of booth-capturing, violence, or irregularities.
Advisory & Quasi-Judicial
  • Advises the President on disqualification of MPs, and Governors on MLAs.
  • Acts as a tribunal to settle disputes regarding splits/mergers and allotment of party symbols.

3. Direct and Indirect Elections

Basis Direct Election Indirect Election
Definition Citizens cast votes directly to choose their representatives. Citizens elect an intermediary body (Electoral College), which then elects the representative.
Electorate All eligible adult citizens (mass voting). Elected legislators (MPs and MLAs).
Public Participation Direct and widespread; stimulates civic awareness. Indirect; general public does not vote directly.
Examples Lok Sabha (MPs), State Vidhan Sabha (MLAs), Gram Panchayats. President of India, Vice-President, Rajya Sabha members.

4. Types of Elections: General, Mid-Term, and By-Election

Type of Election When is it Held? Scope / Area Tenure of Elected Members
General Election Held at the normal expiry of the House term (every 5 years). All constituencies across the country (Lok Sabha) or State (Assembly). Full term of 5 years for the new House.
Mid-Term Election Held when the House is dissolved prematurely before 5 years (e.g. government loses majority). All constituencies of the dissolved House. Full fresh term of 5 years for the newly elected House.
By-Election Held to fill a casual vacancy caused by death, resignation, or disqualification of an individual sitting member. Only the single vacant constituency. Only the remaining unexpired term of the existing House.

5. High-Yield Board Questions (Quick Revision)

2 Marks
Q1. State two differences between a Mid-Term Election and a By-Election.
1. Scope: A mid-term election is held for the entire House, whereas a by-election is held only for one vacant constituency.
2. Tenure: In a mid-term election, members get a full fresh term of 5 years; in a by-election, the member serves only for the remaining unexpired period of the House.
3 Marks
Q2. State three constitutional safeguards that ensure the independence of the ECI.
  1. Security of Tenure: The CEC can only be removed like a Supreme Court Judge via parliamentary impeachment with a 2/3rd majority on proved misbehaviour or incapacity.
  2. Protection of ECs: Other Election Commissioners cannot be removed except on the recommendation of the CEC.
  3. Protected Service Conditions: Salaries are charged on the Consolidated Fund of India and cannot be altered to their disadvantage during their tenure.
3 Marks
Q3. Give three administrative functions of the Election Commission of India.
  1. Preparation and periodic revision of the Electoral Rolls (Voter List).
  2. Recognition of political parties and allotment of election symbols.
  3. Enforcement of the Model Code of Conduct (MCC) to ensure free and fair elections.
