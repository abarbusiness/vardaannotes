Geography Chapter 10: Weathering & Denudation (Class Notes)

1. Weathering vs. Denudation

Definitions
  • Weathering: The static, in-situ disintegration (mechanical) and decomposition (chemical) of rocks by atmospheric elements, with no transportation.
  • Denudation: The dynamic overall wearing away and lowering of the Earth's surface relief:
    Denudation = Weathering + Mass Wasting + Erosion + Transportation.
  • Effects: Generates parent material for soil formation (pedogenesis), concentrates mineral deposits (bauxite), and modifies topography.

2. Types of Weathering: Summary Table

Category Process Mechanism & Climatic Environment
1. Physical (Mechanical)
Breaks rocks without mineral change; dominates in dry/desert areas with high diurnal range.		 Block Disintegration Daily heating (expansion) and nocturnal cooling (contraction) create tension along joints, splitting rock into large rectangular blocks.
Granular Disintegration Different minerals expand at different rates under temperature change, causing coarse-grained rock (granite) to crumble grain-by-grain into sand.
Exfoliation Outer layers heat and cool much faster than interior; outer curved concentric sheets peel off like onion skin, creating Exfoliation Domes.
2. Chemical
Decomposes and alters mineral structure; thrives in warm, humid tropical climates.		 Oxidation Atmospheric oxygen in moisture reacts with iron minerals, forming reddish-brown rust (iron oxide) that crumbles easily.
Carbonation Rainwater dissolves $CO_2$ forming weak carbonic acid ($H_2CO_3$), which converts insoluble limestone ($CaCO_3$) into soluble calcium bicarbonate [$Ca(HCO_3)_2$], creating caves and sinkholes (Karst topography).
Hydration Minerals chemically absorb water, swelling and crumbling (e.g. Anhydrite turns to Gypsum; Feldspar turns to Kaolin).
Solution Water-soluble minerals (rock salt, gypsum) dissolve directly in rainwater and wash away.
3. Biological
Combined mechanical and biochemical breakdown by living organisms.		 Plants Growing roots pry open rock joints mechanically; decaying mosses/leaves release organic (humic) acids that corrode rocks chemically.
Animals Burrowing creatures (rodents, earthworms, ants) loosen subsoil, exposing fresh rocks to air and moisture.
Humans Mining, quarrying, deforestation, blasting, and acid rain from industrial emissions accelerate disintegration.

3. Work of a River & Associated Landforms

Stage / Course Dominant Work Associated Landforms Example
Upper Course
(Youthful Stage)		 Vertical erosion (downcutting); steep gradient and high velocity.
  • V-shaped Valley: Steep-sided valley deepened by vertical downcutting and widened by weathering.
  • Waterfall: River plunging vertically over a ledge of hard rock overlying softer rock.
 Jog Falls (Karnataka), Indus Gorge, Grand Canyon.
Middle Course
(Mature Stage)		 Lateral erosion and transportation; gentle gradient, greater water volume.
  • Meander: S-shaped looping bends formed by erosion on the outer concave bank and deposition on the inner convex bank.
 Ganga & Yamuna river meanders.
Lower Course
(Old Stage)		 Deposition; sluggish velocity on flat plains.
  • Delta: Triangular alluvial tract formed at river mouth where sediment clogs the river, splitting it into distributaries.
 Ganga-Brahmaputra Delta (Sundarbans).

4. Work of Wind (Aeolian Landforms in Deserts)

Wind Actions
  • Deflation Hollows (Blowouts): Bowl-shaped depressions scooped out of the desert floor by strong wind lifting and carrying away loose sand particles. When deflation digs down to the underground water table, an Oasis forms (e.g. Qattara Depression, Egypt).
  • Sand Dunes: Depositional mounds or ridges of wind-blown sand formed when wind loses speed:
Type of Dune Shape & Structure Wind Conditions & Example
Barchan Crescent-shaped dune with two tapering horns pointing downwind; gentle convex windward slope, steep concave leeward slope. Formed by steady, unidirectional winds. Common in Thar Desert (Rajasthan).
Seif (Longitudinal) Long, narrow, sharp knife-like sand ridge running parallel to the prevailing wind direction. Formed by shifting wind directions. Common in Sahara Desert.

5. Quick Board Revision Q&A

2 Marks
Q1. State the main difference between Weathering and Erosion.
Weathering is an in-situ (static) process where rocks disintegrate on the spot without transportation. Erosion is a dynamic process involving the active detachment and transportation of rock debris by mobile agents like rivers or winds.
3 Marks
Q2. Name the three types of chemical weathering and write one line on each.
  1. Oxidation: Oxygen in moisture reacts with iron minerals to form crumbly rust.
  2. Carbonation: Carbonic acid dissolves insoluble limestone into soluble calcium bicarbonate.
  3. Hydration: Water molecules are chemically absorbed by minerals, causing them to swell and decompose (e.g. feldspar to kaolin).
3 Marks
Q3. Give geographical reasons: (a) Chemical weathering is rapid in equatorial regions, (b) Barchans have horns pointing downwind.
(a) Equatorial regions have high temperatures and heavy rainfall year-round, which accelerate chemical reaction rates.
(b) Sand grains at the thinner edges of the dune are pushed forward faster by prevailing winds than the heavier central mound, creating advancing horns.
