Unit 4: Diversity in Living Organisms

Chapter 08: Five Kingdom Classification

Rapid Class Notes — High-Yield Core Revision Summary

1. Concept of Species & Interspecific Hybrids

Gold Standard ICSE Definition

“Species means an organism of a particular kind whose members can interbreed freely among themselves under natural conditions to produce fertile young ones.”

  • Core Criterion: Natural interbreeding with production of fertile offspring.
  • Intraspecific Variations: All modern humans belong to the single species Homo sapiens despite variations in skin colour, height, or race, because they can freely interbreed and produce fertile children.
  • Interspecific Hybrids (Sterile): Crosses between two different species produce sterile offspring that cannot reproduce:
Parental Cross Hybrid Name Biological Status
Male Donkey × Female Horse Mule Intermediate ears and size; Sterile organism (not a species).
Male Horse × Female Donkey Hinny Smaller than mule; Sterile.
Male Lion × Female Tiger Liger Largest feline; Sterile.
Male Tiger × Female Lion Tigon Faint stripes/spots; Sterile.
Potato × Tomato Pomato Somatic hybrid; No viable seeds (Sterile).
Board Trap

Is a mule a species? No. Because it is an interspecific hybrid and is sterile (cannot reproduce).

3. Drawbacks of Two-Kingdom Classification (Linnaeus)

  • Fungi & Bacteria in Plantae: Placed under plants merely for having cell walls, ignoring that they completely lack chlorophyll and photosynthesis.
  • Euglena Anomaly: Photosynthesises like a plant in sunlight, but moves with flagellum and ingests food like an animal in the dark.
  • Chlamydomonas Ambiguity: Possesses chloroplasts and cell wall (plant), but actively swims with flagella and has an eyespot (animal).
  • Prokaryotes vs Eukaryotes Ignored: Did not distinguish between unorganized prokaryotes (bacteria) and organized eukaryotes.

4. Whittaker's Five Kingdom Classification (1969)

Based on four fundamental scientific criteria: (1) Cell structure (Prokaryotic vs Eukaryotic), (2) Body complexity (Unicellular vs Multicellular), (3) Mode of nutrition (Autotrophic vs Heterotrophic), and (4) Cell wall presence/absence.

1. Kingdom Monera
Cell:Prokaryotic (No nuclear membrane; naked circular DNA nucleoid)
Body:Unicellular; no membrane-bound organelles (no mitochondria/plastids)
Nutrition:Autotrophic or Heterotrophic (saprophytic/parasitic)
Examples:Bacteria (Cocci, Bacilli, Spirilla), Blue-green algae (Nostoc)
2. Kingdom Protista
Cell:Eukaryotic (Organized nucleus with nuclear membrane & organelles)
Body:Unicellular (or simple colonial)
Locomotion:Pseudopodia (Amoeba), Cilia (Paramecium), Flagella (Euglena)
Examples:Amoeba, Paramecium, Euglena, Chlamydomonas
3. Kingdom Fungi
Body:Multicellular mycelium made of thread-like hyphae (Yeast is unicellular)
Cell Wall:Composed of Chitin (insoluble fungal cellulose)
Nutrition:Heterotrophic (Absorptive Saprophytes); no chlorophyll
Examples:Bread mould (Rhizopus), Mushroom, Yeast, Penicillium
4. Kingdom Plantae
Cell & Body:Multicellular eukaryotes with green chlorophyll in chloroplasts.
Cell Wall:Composed of Cellulose; large permanent sap vacuoles.
Nutrition:Autotrophic (Photosynthetic).
Examples:Algae, Mosses, Ferns, Gymnosperms (Pines), Angiosperms (Flowering).
5. Kingdom Animalia
Cell & Body:Multicellular eukaryotes with organ and organ-system organization.
Cell Wall:Completely absent; bounded solely by flexible plasma membrane.
Nutrition & Mobility:Heterotrophic (Holozoic / Ingestive); actively mobile.
Examples:Non-chordates (Porifera to Echinodermata) and Chordates (Fishes to Mammals).

5. Kingdom Plantae — The 5 Major Divisions

Division Diagnostic Characteristics Examples
1. Thallophyta (Algae) Plant body is an undifferentiated thallus (no true roots, stem, or leaves). No vascular bundles; mostly aquatic. Spirogyra, Volvox, Ulothrix
2. Bryophyta (Mosses) “Amphibians of plant kingdom” (need water for fertilization). False root-like rhizoids; no vascular tissues. Funaria (moss), Marchantia
3. Pteridophyta (Ferns) True roots, underground rhizome, and fronds. First vascular plants (primitive conducting tissues). Spores in sori. Ferns (Dryopteris, Pteris)
4. Gymnosperms Perennial woody plants with needle leaves. Reproductive cones; produce naked seeds (not enclosed in fruit). Pinus (pine), Cycas, Fir
5. Angiosperms True flowering plants. Seeds are enclosed inside a protective fruit. Advanced xylem vessels and phloem companion cells. Monocots (Maize, Rice) & Dicots (Pea, Mango)
Rapid Fire: Monocots vs Dicots
Cotyledons:Monocots: 1 cotyledon  |  Dicots: 2 cotyledons
Venation:Monocots: Parallel venation  |  Dicots: Reticulate (netted) venation
Root System:Monocots: Fibrous roots  |  Dicots: Tap root system
Floral Parts:Monocots: Trimerous (multiples of 3)  |  Dicots: Tetramerous / Pentamerous (4 or 5)

6. Binomial Nomenclature (Carolus Linnaeus)

The Four Golden Rules
  1. Two parts: Generic name (Genus) + Specific name (Species).
  2. Capitalization: Genus starts with a Capital letter; Species starts with a small letter (e.g. Homo sapiens).
  3. Typography: Italics when printed; underlined separately when handwritten.
  4. Language: Latin (unchanging global standard).

7. Systematic Study of Invertebrate (Non-Chordate) Phyla

Phylum Diagnostic Features Key Examples
1. Porifera (Pore-bearers) Cellular grade; perforated by incurrent ostia and single large excurrent osculum; canal system; spicules/spongin skeleton. Sycon, Bathsponge, Spongilla
2. Cnidaria (Coelenterata) Tissue grade; diploblastic with mesoglea; single gastrovascular opening (mouth); tentacles with explosive stinging cnidoblasts. Hydra, Jellyfish, Sea Anemone, Corals
3. Platyhelminthes (Flatworms) Dorso-ventrally flattened; acoelomate; incomplete gut (mouth only); suckers and hooks in parasites; flame cells. Planaria, Liver fluke, Tapeworm
4. Nematoda (Roundworms) Cylindrical, unsegmented, tapering ends; pseudocoelomate; complete gut with two openings (mouth & anus). Ascaris, Hookworm, Filarial worm
5. Annelida (Segmented worms) Ring-like metameric segmentation; true coelom; closed circulation with dissolved haemoglobin; excretion by nephridia. Earthworm, Leech, Nereis
6. Arthropoda (Jointed legs) Largest phylum; jointed appendages; chitinous waterproof exoskeleton; periodic ecdysis (moulting); open circulation. Crab, Centipede, Insects, Spider
7. Mollusca (Soft-bodied) Soft unsegmented body enclosed by mantle secreting a calcareous shell; ventral muscular foot; gills (ctenidia). Snail, Octopus, Oyster, Cuttlefish
8. Echinodermata (Spiny-skinned) Exclusively marine; adult pentamerous radial symmetry; unique water vascular system with tube-feet. Starfish, Sea Urchin, Sea Cucumber
1. Phylum Porifera (Pore-Bearers)
Organization:Cellular grade; simplest multicellular animals
Body Wall:Numerous incurrent ostia & single large excurrent osculum
Skeleton:Calcareous/siliceous spicules or spongin fibres
Examples:Sycon, Bathsponge (Euspongia), Spongilla
5. Phylum Annelida (Ringed Worms)
Body:Ring-like metameric segmentation; true coelom
Circulation:Closed; haemoglobin dissolved in plasma
Excretion:Coiled microscopic nephridia in each segment
Examples:Earthworm (Pheretima), Leech, Nereis
6. Phylum Arthropoda (Jointed Appendages)
Diversity:Largest phylum (>80% of all animal species)
Exoskeleton:Chitinous waterproof coat; periodic ecdysis (moulting)
Classes:Crustacea (5 pairs legs), Myriapoda, Insecta (3 pairs), Arachnida (4 pairs)
Body Symmetry in Animals
Asymmetry:No plane divides body into equal halves (Amoeba, Sponges)
Radial:Any central longitudinal cut gives equal halves (Starfish, Hydra)
Bilateral:Only one median vertical plane gives mirror halves (Insects, Vertebrates)

8. Phylum Chordata & The Five Vertebrate Classes

Hallmarks of Chordata: (1) Notochord (replaced by vertebral column), (2) Dorsal hollow nerve cord, (3) Pharyngeal gill slits, and (4) Post-anal tail.

1. Class Pisces (Fishes)
Skin:Scaly, slimy; breathe by gills
Heart:2-chambered (venous blood only)
Body Temp:Cold-blooded (poikilothermic)
Examples:Shark, Dogfish (cartilage); Rohu, Trout (bony)
2. Class Amphibia
Skin:Moist, slimy, non-scaly skin
Respiration:Gills (larva); lungs, skin & mouth (adult)
Heart:3-chambered (2 atria, 1 ventricle); Cold-blooded
Examples:Frog (Rana), Toad (Bufo), Salamander
3. Class Reptilia
Skin:Dry, rough with horny epidermal scales
Heart:3-chambered (Crocodile is 4-chambered)
Eggs:Waterproof leathery shell on land; Cold-blooded
Examples:Lizard, Snake (Naja), Tortoise, Crocodile
4. Class Aves (Birds)
Anatomy:Body covered with feathers; forelimbs modified into wings; pneumatic hollow bones; toothless beak
Heart:4-chambered; Warm-blooded (homeothermic)
Examples:Pigeon, Peacock, Sparrow, Ostrich
5. Class Mammalia
Hallmarks:Mammary glands (suckle young with milk), body covered with hair/fur, external ear flaps (pinnae), muscular diaphragm.
Circulation:4-chambered heart; Warm-blooded; non-nucleated biconcave RBCs.
Reproduction:Mostly viviparous (give live birth).
Exceptions:Egg-laying: Echidna & Platypus; Pouched: Kangaroo; Aquatic: Whale, Dolphin; Flying: Bat.

9. High-Yield Board Exam Rapid-Fire Checklist

Q1. Why are fungi not placed under Kingdom Plantae?
Because fungi completely lack chlorophyll, cannot photosynthesise, and are absorptive heterotrophs (saprophytes) with cell walls of chitin, not cellulose.
Q2. What makes an earthworm the "Farmer's Friend"?
Its deep burrowing loosens and aerates the soil, improves drainage, brings rich subsoil to the top, and deposits pulverised fertile worm castings rich in nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium.
Q3. Differentiate between Cartilaginous and Bony Fishes.
Cartilaginous fish have cartilage skeleton, ventral mouth, exposed gill slits without operculum, and no swim bladder (e.g. Shark). Bony fish have bone skeleton, terminal mouth, operculum covering gills, and swim bladder present (e.g. Rohu).
Q4. State the reptile with a 4-chambered heart.
Crocodile / Alligator (all other reptiles have a 3-chambered heart with an incomplete ventricle).
Q5. Why is a bat classified as a mammal and not a bird?
Because it possesses hair/fur, external ear pinnae, mammary glands, a muscular diaphragm, and gives birth to live young that feed on mother's milk.
