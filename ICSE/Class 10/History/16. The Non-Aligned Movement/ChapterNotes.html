Chapter 16: The Non-Aligned Movement (NAM)

Chapter Overview

During the height of the Cold War, newly independent nations of Asia, Africa, and Latin America rejected joining either of the two rival superpower military blocs (USA or USSR). Instead, they established the Non-Aligned Movement (NAM) to preserve their national independence, promote world peace, and fight colonialism.

1. Meaning and Concept of Non-Alignment

Definition

Non-Alignment: A foreign policy doctrine whereby a nation refrains from joining military alliances (such as NATO, SEATO, or Warsaw Pact) led by superpowers, while exercising an independent judgment on international issues based on truth, justice, and national interest.

What Non-Alignment is NOT:

  • It is NOT Neutrality (passivity in times of war).
  • It is NOT Isolationism (aloofness from world affairs).
  • It is an active, dynamic policy aimed at promoting international peace and resolving global conflicts.

2. Factors Leading to the Growth of NAM

Catalysts of NAM
1. Cold War Rivalry & Bipolar World

The post-WWII world was divided into two hostile armed blocs: Western Capitalist Bloc (led by USA) and Eastern Communist Bloc (led by USSR). Joining either bloc meant risking involvement in a catastrophic third world war.

2. Preservation of Hard-Won Sovereignty

Newly decolonized Asian and African countries had suffered under European domination for centuries. They refused to surrender their newly acquired independence by becoming military puppets of superpowers.

3. Economic Development Needs

Developing nations required peace, financial capital, technology, and economic assistance from both blocs without attaching political or military strings.

4. The Bandung Conference (1955)

Held at Bandung, Indonesia in April 1955, attended by 29 Afro-Asian nations. It adopted the Ten Principles of Bandung, serving as the immediate forerunner to the founding of NAM.

3. Architects of NAM and the Belgrade Summit (1961)

Founding Fathers of NAM

The Non-Aligned Movement was built by three principal visionaries:

  • Jawaharlal Nehru Prime Minister of India — Chief philosopher of Panchsheel and Non-Alignment.
  • Josip Broz Tito President of Yugoslavia.
  • Gamal Abdel Nasser President of Egypt.

Supported strongly by: Sukarno (President of Indonesia) and Kwame Nkrumah (President of Ghana).

First NAM Summit (1961): Formally established at the landmark Belgrade Summit in Yugoslavia in September 1961, attended by 25 member nations.

4. The Panchsheel (Five Principles of Peaceful Coexistence)

Panchsheel Agreement

First articulated in the preamble to the Sino-Indian Agreement on Tibet signed on 29 April 1954 by Indian Prime Minister Jawaharlal Nehru and Chinese Premier Zhou Enlai.

The 5 Pillars of Panchsheel:

  1. Mutual respect for each other's territorial integrity and sovereignty.
  2. Mutual non-aggression.
  3. Mutual non-interference in each other's internal affairs.
  4. Equality and mutual benefit.
  5. Peaceful co-existence.

Major Objectives of NAM

  • Elimination of colonialism, imperialism, neo-colonialism, and racial discrimination (such as Apartheid in South Africa).
  • Prevention of nuclear war and promotion of universal disarmament.
  • Establishment of a New International Economic Order (NIEO) based on fair trade terms for developing nations.
  • Strengthening the United Nations as an effective instrument for global peace.
