Similarity (With Applications to Maps & Models)

ICSE Class 10 Mathematics — Chapter 15 Master Notes

1. Introduction: Similarity vs. Congruency

Similarity of Geometric Figures: Two geometric figures are said to be similar if they have the same shape, but may differ in size.

  • Same Shape Condition: Corresponding angles of the two figures are equal.
  • Proportional Sides Condition: Ratios between lengths of corresponding sides are equal.

Congruency of Figures: Two figures are congruent if they have the same shape and the same size.

Golden Rule to Remember:

Congruent figures are always similar, but similar figures are not necessarily congruent!

2. Similar Triangles & Corresponding Parts

Two triangles $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle PQR$ are said to be similar (written as $\triangle ABC \sim \triangle PQR$) if and only if:

Conditions for Similarity ($\triangle ABC \sim \triangle PQR$):

  1. Equal Corresponding Angles: $\angle A = \angle P$, $\angle B = \angle Q$, and $\angle C = \angle R$.
  2. Proportional Corresponding Sides: $$\frac{AB}{PQ} = \frac{BC}{QR} = \frac{AC}{PR}$$

Rules of Corresponding Sides and Angles:

  • In similar triangles, the sides opposite to equal angles are corresponding sides.
  • The angles opposite to proportional sides are corresponding angles and are equal.
  • Order of Vertices: Writing $\triangle ABC \sim \triangle PQR$ strictly implies vertex $A \leftrightarrow P$, $B \leftrightarrow Q$, and $C \leftrightarrow R$. Never scramble the vertex order!

3. Postulates / Criteria for Similarity of Triangles

To prove two triangles similar, it is sufficient to establish one of the following 3 postulates:

  1. 1. Angle-Angle (AA) or AAA Postulate:
    If two angles of one triangle are respectively equal to two angles of another triangle, the two triangles are similar.
    Note: Since angle sum of a triangle is $180^\circ$, two equal angles automatically imply the third angles are equal!
  2. 2. Side-Angle-Side (SAS) Postulate:
    If one angle of a triangle is equal to one angle of another triangle, and the sides including these angles are proportional, the two triangles are similar. $$\text{If } \angle A = \angle D \quad \text{and} \quad \frac{AB}{DE} = \frac{AC}{DF} \implies \triangle ABC \sim \triangle DEF$$
  3. 3. Side-Side-Side (SSS) Postulate:
    If all three pairs of corresponding sides of two triangles are in the same ratio, the triangles are similar. $$\text{If } \frac{AB}{PQ} = \frac{BC}{QR} = \frac{AC}{PR} \implies \triangle ABC \sim \triangle PQR$$

4. Basic Proportionality Theorem (BPT / Thales Theorem)

Basic Proportionality Theorem (BPT / Thales Theorem):

If a line is drawn parallel to one side of a triangle intersecting the other two sides, then it divides the two sides in the same ratio.

$$\text{If } DE \parallel BC \implies \frac{AD}{DB} = \frac{AE}{EC}$$

Corollary (Small Triangle to Large Triangle Similarity):

Since $\angle ADE = \angle ABC$ (Corresponding angles) and $\angle A$ is common, $\triangle ADE \sim \triangle ABC$ by AA Postulate.

$$\therefore \frac{AD}{AB} = \frac{AE}{AC} = \frac{DE}{BC}$$

Converse of BPT: If a line divides any two sides of a triangle in the same ratio ($\frac{AD}{DB} = \frac{AE}{EC}$), then the line is parallel to the third side ($DE \parallel BC$).

5. Altitude to Hypotenuse in Right-Angled Triangle

Theorem on Altitude to Hypotenuse:

If a perpendicular is drawn from the vertex of the right angle of a right triangle to its hypotenuse, then the triangles on each side of the perpendicular are similar to the whole triangle and to each other.

$$\triangle DBA \sim \triangle ABC, \quad \triangle DAC \sim \triangle ABC, \quad \triangle DBA \sim \triangle DAC$$

3 Fundamental Geometric Formulas for Altitude AD:

  1. Side AB Square: $$AB^2 = BD \times BC$$
  2. Side AC Square: $$AC^2 = CD \times BC$$
  3. Altitude AD Square (Geometric Mean Theorem): $$AD^2 = BD \times CD$$

6. Relation Between Areas of Two Similar Triangles

Theorem (Area Ratio of Similar Triangles):

The ratio of the areas of two similar triangles is equal to the ratio of the squares of their corresponding sides (and also corresponding altitudes, medians, or perimeters).

$$\frac{\text{Area}(\triangle ABC)}{\text{Area}(\triangle DEF)} = \left(\frac{AB}{DE}\right)^2 = \left(\frac{BC}{EF}\right)^2 = \left(\frac{AC}{DF}\right)^2 = \left(\frac{AM}{DN}\right)^2$$

Key Properties of Areas & Bases:

  1. Triangles with Common Altitude / Same Height:
    If two triangles have the same vertex and their bases are on the same straight line, the ratio of their areas equals the ratio of their bases! $$\frac{\text{Area}(\triangle ABE)}{\text{Area}(\triangle AEC)} = \frac{BE}{EC}$$
  2. Median Area Property:
    A median divides a triangle into two triangles of equal area. $$\text{Area}(\triangle ABD) = \text{Area}(\triangle ACD) = \frac{1}{2} \text{Area}(\triangle ABC)$$

7. Applications to Maps and Models (Scale Factor $k$)

Maps and Scale Models: Maps of countries, architectural blueprints, and scaled models of ships or aircraft are geometrically similar to their actual real-life counterparts.

Scale Factor ($k$): The constant ratio of any linear dimension of the model/map to the corresponding linear dimension of the actual object.

$$k = \frac{\text{Length of Model / Map}}{\text{Length of Actual Object}}$$

Transformation Rules for Scale Factor $k$:

Dimension Mathematical Relation Scale Ratio Formula
1D (Length / Height / Perimeter) $\text{Length}_{\text{model}} = k \times \text{Length}_{\text{actual}}$ $\frac{\text{Length}_{\text{model}}}{\text{Length}_{\text{actual}}} = k$
2D (Surface Area / Plot Area) $\text{Area}_{\text{model}} = k^2 \times \text{Area}_{\text{actual}}$ $\frac{\text{Area}_{\text{model}}}{\text{Area}_{\text{actual}}} = k^2$
3D (Volume / Capacity / Mass) $\text{Volume}_{\text{model}} = k^3 \times \text{Volume}_{\text{actual}}$ $\frac{\text{Volume}_{\text{model}}}{\text{Volume}_{\text{actual}}} = k^3$

Unit Conversion Tips for Scale Problems:

  • $1\text{ m} = 100\text{ cm}$
  • $1\text{ km} = 1000\text{ m} = 100,000\text{ cm} = 10^5\text{ cm}$
  • $1\text{ m}^2 = 10,000\text{ cm}^2 = 10^4\text{ cm}^2$
  • $1\text{ km}^2 = 10^{10}\text{ cm}^2$
  • $1\text{ m}^3 = 1000\text{ liters} = 10^6\text{ cm}^3$

Example 1 (BPT Calculation): In $\triangle ABC$, $DE \parallel BC$. $D$ divides $AB$ in ratio $AD : DB = 2 : 3$. If $BC = 7.5\text{ cm}$, calculate $DE$ and the ratio $\text{Area}(\triangle ADE) : \text{Area}(\text{trapezium } BCED)$.

Solution:

Since $DE \parallel BC$, $\triangle ADE \sim \triangle ABC$.

$$\frac{AD}{AB} = \frac{AD}{AD + DB} = \frac{2}{2 + 3} = \frac{2}{5}$$

By similarity ratio of corresponding sides:

$$\frac{DE}{BC} = \frac{AD}{AB} = \frac{2}{5} \implies DE = \frac{2}{5} \times 7.5 = 3\text{ cm.}$$

Ratio of areas of similar triangles:

$$\frac{\text{Area}(\triangle ADE)}{\text{Area}(\triangle ABC)} = \left(\frac{DE}{BC}\right)^2 = \left(\frac{2}{5}\right)^2 = \frac{4}{25}$$ $$\implies \text{Area}(\text{trapezium } BCED) = \text{Area}(\triangle ABC) - \text{Area}(\triangle ADE) = 25 - 4 = 21 \text{ units.}$$ $$\therefore \frac{\text{Area}(\triangle ADE)}{\text{Area}(\text{trapezium } BCED)} = \frac{4}{21}$$

Example 2 (Right Triangle Altitude): In right $\triangle ABC$, $\angle A = 90^\circ$ and altitude $AD \perp BC$. If $BD = 3.6\text{ cm}$ and $CD = 6.4\text{ cm}$, calculate altitude $AD$ and side $AB$.

Solution:

Using geometric mean theorem $AD^2 = BD \times CD$:

$$AD^2 = 3.6 \times 6.4 = 23.04 \implies AD = \sqrt{23.04} = 4.8\text{ cm.}$$

Total hypotenuse $BC = BD + CD = 3.6 + 6.4 = 10\text{ cm}$.

Using side square formula $AB^2 = BD \times BC$:

$$AB^2 = 3.6 \times 10 = 36 \implies AB = 6\text{ cm.}$$

Example 3 (Ship Model Scale Problem): A model of a ship is made to a scale of $1 : 200$. If the length of the model is $4\text{ m}$ and the area of the deck of the actual ship is $160,000\text{ m}^2$, calculate:

  • (i) The actual length of the ship in meters.
  • (ii) The area of the deck of the model in $\text{m}^2$.
  • (iii) The volume of the ship if the model volume is $200\text{ liters}$.

Solution:

Scale factor $k = \frac{1}{200}$.

(i) Actual Length:

$$\text{Length}_{\text{model}} = k \times \text{Length}_{\text{actual}} \implies 4 = \frac{1}{200} \times \text{Length}_{\text{actual}} \implies \text{Actual Length} = 800\text{ m.}$$

(ii) Model Deck Area:

$$\text{Area}_{\text{model}} = k^2 \times \text{Area}_{\text{actual}} = \left(\frac{1}{200}\right)^2 \times 160,000 = \frac{160,000}{40,000} = 4\text{ m}^2.$$

(iii) Actual Volume:

$$\text{Volume}_{\text{actual}} = \frac{\text{Volume}_{\text{model}}}{k^3} = 200 \text{ liters} \times 200^3 = 200 \times 8,000,000 = 1,600,000,000 \text{ liters} = 1.6 \times 10^6\text{ m}^3.$$

Example 4 (Map Scale & Plot Area): The scale of a map is $1 : 50,000$. On the map, a triangular plot of land has dimensions $AB = 2\text{ cm}, BC = 3.5\text{ cm}, \angle B = 90^\circ$. Calculate:

  • (i) The actual length of side $BC$ in kilometers.
  • (ii) The actual area of the plot in $\text{km}^2$.

Solution:

Scale factor $k = \frac{1}{50,000}$.

(i) Actual BC Length:

$$\text{Actual } BC = 3.5\text{ cm} \times 50,000 = 175,000\text{ cm} = \frac{175,000}{100,000}\text{ km} = 1.75\text{ km.}$$

(ii) Actual Plot Area:

$$\text{Map Area} = \frac{1}{2} \times 2\text{ cm} \times 3.5\text{ cm} = 3.5\text{ cm}^2.$$ $$\text{Actual Area} = 3.5 \times (50,000)^2 \text{ cm}^2 = 3.5 \times 2,500,000,000\text{ cm}^2 = 8,750,000,000\text{ cm}^2 = 0.875\text{ km}^2.$$
