1. Similarity as a Size Transformation & Comparison with Congruency

Size Transformation (Dilatation)

Definition: A size transformation (or enlargement / reduction) about a fixed point $O$ with scale factor $k$ transforms each point $P$ to a point $P'$ such that $O, P, P'$ are collinear and $\frac{OP'}{OP} = k$.

  • Enlargement ($k > 1$): The image is larger than the original figure.
  • Reduction ($0 < k < 1$): The image is smaller than the original figure.
  • Isometry / Congruence ($k = 1$): The image is identical in size and shape ($\cong$).
  • Invariance Properties: Size transformations preserve collinearity, angles, and orientation, but change all linear lengths by factor $k$.
Feature Congruent Figures ($\cong$) Similar Figures ($\sim$)
Shape Identical (Same Shape) Identical (Same Shape)
Size Exactly Equal ($k = 1$) Scaled ($k > 0$, Proportional)
Corresponding Angles Equal ($\angle A = \angle D, \angle B = \angle E, \angle C = \angle F$) Equal ($\angle A = \angle D, \angle B = \angle E, \angle C = \angle F$)
Corresponding Sides Strictly Equal ($AB = DE, BC = EF, AC = DF$) Proportional: $\frac{AB}{DE} = \frac{BC}{EF} = \frac{AC}{DF} = k$
Core Keyword Equality Proportionality
Fig 15.1: Similarity as a Radial Size Transformation from Center $O$ ($OA' = k \cdot OA$)
ICSE Essential Geometric Checklist
All congruent figures are similar, but similar figures are NOT necessarily congruent. Two regular polygons of the same number of sides are always similar (all circles, all squares, all equilateral triangles).

2. Conditions of Similar Triangles (AA, SAS, SSS)

The 3 Similarity Criteria (No Proofs Required for ICSE)
  1. Angle-Angle (AA or AAA) Postulate: If two angles of one triangle are respectively equal to two angles of another triangle, the triangles are similar. $$\text{If } \angle A = \angle D \text{ and } \angle B = \angle E \implies \mathbf{\Delta ABC \sim \Delta DEF}$$
  2. Side-Angle-Side (SAS) Postulate: If one angle of a triangle is equal to one angle of another triangle and the sides including these angles are proportional: $$\text{If } \angle A = \angle D \text{ and } \frac{AB}{DE} = \frac{AC}{DF} \implies \mathbf{\Delta ABC \sim \Delta DEF}$$
  3. Side-Side-Side (SSS) Postulate: If all three corresponding sides of two triangles are proportional: $$\text{If } \frac{AB}{DE} = \frac{BC}{EF} = \frac{AC}{DF} \implies \mathbf{\Delta ABC \sim \Delta DEF}$$
Ratio of Altitudes, Medians & Perimeters:

If $\Delta ABC \sim \Delta DEF$, then the ratio of corresponding sides is equal to the ratio of their altitudes, medians, angle bisectors, and perimeters:

$$\frac{AB}{DE} = \frac{BC}{EF} = \frac{AC}{DF} = \mathbf{\frac{\text{Altitude } h_1}{\text{Altitude } h_2}} = \mathbf{\frac{\text{Median } m_1}{\text{Median } m_2}} = \mathbf{\frac{\text{Perimeter}(\Delta ABC)}{\text{Perimeter}(\Delta DEF)}} = \mathbf{k}$$
Topic 2 Practice Kit: Basic Similarity Applications
Problem 2.1 (Perpendiculars on Common Hypotenuse) ICSE CLASSIC

In $\Delta ABC$, $\angle B = 90^\circ$ and $DE \perp AC$. If $AB = 6\text{ cm}, BC = 8\text{ cm}$, and $AC = 10\text{ cm}$, and $CD = 5\text{ cm}$, find the length of $DE$.

Step-by-Step Solution:
In $\Delta DEC$ and $\Delta ABC$: $\angle DEC = \angle B = 90^\circ$, and $\angle C = \angle C$ (Common).
$\implies \Delta DEC \sim \Delta ABC$ (AA Similarity). $$\frac{DE}{AB} = \frac{CD}{AC} \implies \frac{DE}{6} = \frac{5}{10} = \frac{1}{2} \implies \mathbf{DE = 3\text{ cm}}$$

3. Applications of Basic Proportionality Theorem (BPT)

Theorem: Basic Proportionality Theorem & Corollaries

Statement: In $\Delta ABC$, if a line $DE \parallel BC$, then $\frac{AD}{DB} = \frac{AE}{EC}$ and $\frac{AD}{AB} = \frac{AE}{AC} = \frac{DE}{BC}$.

Fig 15.3: BPT Parallel Line Proportionality

Core Applications:

  • Trapezium Diagonals: In trapezium $ABCD$ with $AB \parallel DC$, diagonals intersect at $O \implies \mathbf{\frac{AO}{CO} = \frac{BO}{DO}}$.
  • Right-Triangle Altitude to Hypotenuse: If $AD \perp BC$ in right $\Delta ABC$ ($\angle A = 90^\circ$): $$\mathbf{AD^2 = BD \times CD}, \qquad \mathbf{AB^2 = BD \times BC}, \qquad \mathbf{AC^2 = CD \times BC}$$
Topic 3 Practice Kit: BPT & Right-Triangle Altitudes
Problem 3.1 (Right Triangle Altitude Segments) 3 MARKS

In right $\Delta ABC$, $\angle A = 90^\circ$ and $AD \perp BC$. If $BD = 4\text{ cm}$ and $CD = 9\text{ cm}$, find the length of altitude $AD$ and side $AB$.

Step-by-Step Solution:
$$AD^2 = BD \times CD = 4 \times 9 = 36 \implies \mathbf{AD = 6\text{ cm}}$$ $$AB^2 = BD \times BC = 4 \times (4 + 9) = 4 \times 13 = 52 \implies \mathbf{AB = \sqrt{52} = 2\sqrt{13}\text{ cm} \approx 7.21\text{ cm}}$$
Fig 15.4: Right $\Delta ABC$ with Altitude $AD$

4. Areas of Similar Triangles Theorem

Theorem: Area Ratio Proportional to Square of Sides

Statement: The ratio of the areas of two similar triangles is equal to the square of the ratio of their corresponding sides.

$$\mathbf{\frac{\text{Area}(\Delta ABC)}{\text{Area}(\Delta DEF)} = \left(\frac{AB}{DE}\right)^2 = \left(\frac{BC}{EF}\right)^2 = \left(\frac{AC}{DF}\right)^2 = \left(\frac{h_1}{h_2}\right)^2 = k^2}$$
Fig 15.5: Ratio of Areas $= \left(\frac{BC}{EF}\right)^2 = \left(\frac{AM}{DN}\right)^2$
CRITICAL EXAM TRAP: Similar Triangles vs Common Vertex Triangles
  • Similar Triangles ($\Delta ADE \sim \Delta ABC$): Areas are proportional to the SQUARE of sides: $\frac{\text{Area}(\Delta ADE)}{\text{Area}(\Delta ABC)} = \left(\frac{AD}{AB}\right)^2$.
  • Triangles with Common Vertex & Collinear Bases: Areas are directly proportional to the LINEAR ratio of bases: $\frac{\text{Area}(\Delta ABD)}{\text{Area}(\Delta ADC)} = \frac{BD}{DC}$.
Topic 4 Practice Kit: Area Ratios & Trapezium Areas
Problem 4.1 (Trapezium Subtraction) ICSE BOARD FAVORITE

In $\Delta ABC$, $DE \parallel BC$. If $AD : DB = 2 : 3$, find:
(i) $\frac{\text{Area}(\Delta ADE)}{\text{Area}(\Delta ABC)}$
(ii) $\frac{\text{Area}(\Delta ADE)}{\text{Area}(\text{Trapezium } BCED)}$

Step-by-Step Solution:
$AD : AB = 2 : (2 + 3) = 2 : 5$.
(i) $\frac{\text{Area}(\Delta ADE)}{\text{Area}(\Delta ABC)} = \left(\frac{AD}{AB}\right)^2 = \left(\frac{2}{5}\right)^2 = \mathbf{\frac{4}{25}}$.
(ii) $\text{Area}(\text{Trapezium } BCED) = 25 - 4 = 21\text{ units}$. $$\frac{\text{Area}(\Delta ADE)}{\text{Area}(\text{Trapezium } BCED)} = \mathbf{\frac{4}{21}}$$

5. Direct Applications to Maps & Models (Scale Factor $k$, $k^2$, $k^3$)

Master Matrix: 1D, 2D and 3D Scale Relations

If the linear scale of a map/model is $1 : n$ (i.e. scale factor $k = \frac{1}{n}$):

Dimension Model / Map Relation Scale Ratio Formula
1D: Length, Width, Height, Perimeter $\text{Length}_{\text{model}} = k \cdot \text{Length}_{\text{actual}}$ $\mathbf{\frac{\text{Length}_{\text{model}}}{\text{Length}_{\text{actual}}} = k = \frac{1}{n}}$
2D: Surface Area, Cross-Section, Plot Area $\text{Area}_{\text{model}} = k^2 \cdot \text{Area}_{\text{actual}}$ $\mathbf{\frac{\text{Area}_{\text{model}}}{\text{Area}_{\text{actual}}} = k^2 = \frac{1}{n^2}}$
3D: Volume, Capacity, Mass $\text{Volume}_{\text{model}} = k^3 \cdot \text{Volume}_{\text{actual}}$ $\mathbf{\frac{\text{Volume}_{\text{model}}}{\text{Volume}_{\text{actual}}} = k^3 = \frac{1}{n^3}}$
Mandatory ICSE Unit Conversions:
$1\text{ km} = 10^5\text{ cm} = 1000\text{ m}$
$1\text{ km}^2 = 10^{10}\text{ cm}^2 = 10^6\text{ m}^2$
$1\text{ m}^2 = 10^4\text{ cm}^2 = 10,000\text{ cm}^2$
$1\text{ m}^3 = 10^6\text{ cm}^3 = 1000\text{ litres}$
$1\text{ litre} = 1000\text{ cm}^3$
Topic 5 Practice Kit: Maps, Scale Models & Volumes
Problem 5.1 (Map Scale & Plot Area) MAP APPLICATION / 3 MARKS

The scale of a map is $1 : 20,000$. A rectangular plot of land has dimensions $3\text{ cm} \times 2\text{ cm}$ on the map. Calculate:
(i) The actual perimeter of the plot in kilometers.
(ii) The actual area of the plot in square kilometers ($\text{km}^2$).

Step-by-Step Solution:
$k = \frac{1}{20,000}$.
(i) $\text{Perimeter on map} = 2(3 + 2) = 10\text{ cm}$. $$\text{Actual Perimeter} = 10 \times 20,000\text{ cm} = 200,000\text{ cm} = \frac{200,000}{100,000}\text{ km} = \mathbf{2.0\text{ km}}$$ (ii) $\text{Area on map} = 3 \times 2 = 6\text{ cm}^2$. $$\text{Actual Area} = 6 \times (20,000)^2\text{ cm}^2 = 6 \times 4 \times 10^8 = 2.4 \times 10^9\text{ cm}^2$$ $$\text{Actual Area in km}^2 = \frac{2.4 \times 10^9}{10^{10}} = \mathbf{0.24\text{ km}^2}$$
Problem 5.2 (Ship Model Volume & Area) ICSE BOARD 4 MARKS

A model of a ship is made to a scale of $1 : 300$.
(i) The length of the ship is $180\text{ m}$. Find the length of the model in cm.
(ii) The area of the deck of the ship is $180,000\text{ m}^2$. Find the deck area of the model in $\text{m}^2$.
(iii) The volume of the model is $6.5\text{ litres}$. Find the actual volume of the ship in $\text{m}^3$.

Step-by-Step Solution:
(i) $\text{Model Length} = \frac{180\text{ m}}{300} = 0.6\text{ m} = \mathbf{60\text{ cm}}$.
(ii) $\text{Model Deck Area} = \left(\frac{1}{300}\right)^2 \times 180,000 = \frac{180,000}{90,000} = \mathbf{2.0\text{ m}^2}$.
(iii) $\text{Actual Volume} = 6.5\text{ litres} \times (300)^3 = 6.5 \times 2.7 \times 10^7 = 1.755 \times 10^8\text{ litres}$. $$\text{Actual Volume in m}^3 = \frac{1.755 \times 10^8}{1000} = \mathbf{1.755 \times 10^5\text{ m}^3} = \mathbf{175,500\text{ m}^3}$$

6. 100/100 ICSE Examination Master Checklist

Master Exam Checklist
  • Size Transformation: Linear scale $k$, Area scale $k^2$, Volume scale $k^3$.
  • Conditions: AA (2 angles equal), SAS (2 sides proportional and included angle equal), SSS (all 3 sides proportional).
  • BPT: $DE \parallel BC \implies \frac{AD}{DB} = \frac{AE}{EC}$ and $\frac{AD}{AB} = \frac{AE}{AC} = \frac{DE}{BC}$.
  • Right Triangle Altitude: $AD^2 = BD \times CD$, $AB^2 = BD \times BC$.
  • Area Ratio: $\frac{\text{Area}_1}{\text{Area}_2} = \left(\frac{\text{Side}_1}{\text{Side}_2}\right)^2 = \left(\frac{\text{Altitude}_1}{\text{Altitude}_2}\right)^2 = \left(\frac{\text{Median}_1}{\text{Median}_2}\right)^2 = k^2$.
  • Maps & Models: $\text{Length} \propto k, \ \text{Area} \propto k^2, \ \text{Volume} \propto k^3$. (Remember $1\text{ km}^2 = 10^{10}\text{ cm}^2$ and $1\text{ m}^3 = 1000\text{ litres}$).
