Shares and Dividend

ICSE Class 10 Mathematics — Chapter 3 Master Notes

1. Commercial Terminology & Core Concepts

Share (or Stock): To establish a big company requiring large capital, the total estimated capital is divided into small equal parts (ranging from ₹1 to ₹100). Each small part is called a share, and persons purchasing shares are called shareholders.

1. Nominal Value (N.V.) / Face Value (F.V.) / Printed Value / Register Value:

  • The original price assigned to a share by the company when issued.
  • CRITICAL RULE: The Nominal Value (N.V.) of a share NEVER changes with time!

2. Market Value (M.V.) / Cash Value:

  • The current selling/buying price of a share in the open stock market at any particular time.
  • The Market Value (M.V.) changes constantly depending on company performance and market demand.

3. Dividend:

  • The profit paid by the company to its shareholders out of its annual net profits.
  • CRITICAL RULE: Dividend is ALWAYS calculated as a percentage of the Nominal Value (N.V.) of the share, NEVER on Market Value (M.V.)!

Stock Quotations Summary:

  1. Share at Par: $\text{Market Value (M.V.)} = \text{Nominal Value (N.V.)}$.
  2. Share at Premium (Above Par): $\text{Market Value (M.V.)} = \text{Nominal Value (N.V.)} + \text{Premium}$.
  3. Share at Discount (Below Par): $\text{Market Value (M.V.)} = \text{Nominal Value (N.V.)} - \text{Discount}$.

2. Master Formulae Matrix

Core Mathematical Relations for Share Calculations:

  1. Total Investment (Sum Invested): $$\text{Total Investment} = \text{Number of Shares } (n) \times \text{M.V. of 1 share}$$
  2. Number of Shares Bought ($n$): $$n = \frac{\text{Total Investment}}{\text{M.V. of 1 share}} = \frac{\text{Total Annual Dividend}}{\text{Dividend on 1 share}} = \frac{\text{Total Annual Income}}{\text{Income on 1 share}}$$
  3. Dividend / Annual Income from 1 Share: $$\text{Dividend on 1 Share} = d\% \text{ of N.V.} = \frac{d}{100} \times \text{N.V. of 1 share}$$
  4. Total Annual Income (Total Dividend): $$\text{Total Annual Income} = n \times \text{Dividend on 1 Share} = n \times \left(\frac{d}{100} \times \text{N.V.}\right)$$
  5. Rate of Return % / Profit % on Investment ($r\%$): $$\text{Return } \% = \frac{\text{Total Annual Income}}{\text{Total Investment}} \times 100\%$$

Master Shortcut Equation (1-Share Equivalence Principle):

For any investor buying shares of face value $\text{N.V.}$ at market price $\text{M.V.}$ paying $d\%$ dividend with return rate $r\%$, the annual income generated from $1$ share can be computed in two equal ways:

$$\text{Return } \% \times \text{M.V.} = \text{Dividend } \% \times \text{N.V.}$$

$$r \times \text{M.V.} = d \times \text{N.V.} \implies r = \frac{d \times \text{N.V.}}{\text{M.V.}}$$

Use this formula instantly when asked to find the rate of interest/return on investment without calculating total shares or total investment!

3. Advanced Special Problem Types & Re-investment Transactions

Master Workflow for Re-investment Problems (Selling Shares & Buying New Ones):

  1. Phase 1 (Original Shares):
    Calculate number of original shares $n_1 = \frac{\text{Investment}}{\text{M.V.}_1}$ and original annual dividend $D_1 = n_1 \times d_1\% \times \text{N.V.}_1$.
  2. Phase 2 (Sale Proceeds):
    Calculate money obtained by selling shares: $\text{Sale Proceeds} = n_1 \times \text{Selling Price per share}$.
    Note: If dividends are collected before selling, $\text{Proceeds} = (n_1 \times \text{Selling Price}) + D_1$.
  3. Phase 3 (New Shares Bought):
    Calculate number of new shares bought $n_2 = \frac{\text{Sale Proceeds}}{\text{M.V.}_2}$.
  4. Phase 4 (New Income & Net Change):
    Calculate new annual income $D_2 = n_2 \times d_2\% \times \text{N.V.}_2$.
    Change in Annual Income $= D_2 - D_1$.

Comparing Two Investment Options ("Which is a Better Investment?"):

Calculate the Return Percentage ($r\%$) for both options using $r = \frac{d \times \text{N.V.}}{\text{M.V.}}$:

  • Option 1: Return $\% = \frac{d_1 \times \text{N.V.}_1}{\text{M.V.}_1} \times 100\%$
  • Option 2: Return $\% = \frac{d_2 \times \text{N.V.}_2}{\text{M.V.}_2} \times 100\%$

Conclusion: The investment option yielding the higher return percentage ($r\%$) is the better investment!

4. Step-by-Step Solved Master Examples (Covering Every Type)

Interactive Market Value vs. Return % Curve (12% Dividend on ₹100 Share)

As Market Value (M.V.) increases above face value ₹100, the Return % on Investment decreases!

Example 1 (Basic Investment & Market Quotations): Calculate the money required to buy:

  • (i) 350, ₹20 shares at a premium of ₹7.
  • (ii) 275, ₹60 shares at a discount of ₹10.
  • (iii) 50, ₹40 shares quoted at ₹38.50.

Solution:

(i) 350 shares, N.V. = ₹20 at Premium ₹7:

$$\text{M.V. of 1 share} = 20 + 7 = \text{₹ } 27$$ $$\text{Money required} = 350 \times 27 = \text{₹ } \mathbf{9,450}$$

(ii) 275 shares, N.V. = ₹60 at Discount ₹10:

$$\text{M.V. of 1 share} = 60 - 10 = \text{₹ } 50$$ $$\text{Money required} = 275 \times 50 = \text{₹ } \mathbf{13,750}$$

(iii) 50 shares, Quoted M.V. = ₹38.50:

$$\text{Money required} = 50 \times 38.50 = \text{₹ } \mathbf{1,925}$$

Example 2 (Number of Shares & Total Annual Income): Rakhee invested ₹12,500 in shares of a company paying 6% dividend per annum. If she bought ₹50 shares for ₹62.50 each, find her annual income from the investment.

Solution:

Given: Total Investment $= \text{₹ } 12,500$, $\text{M.V.} = \text{₹ } 62.50$, $\text{N.V.} = \text{₹ } 50$, Dividend rate $d = 6\%$.

1. Number of shares bought ($n$):

$$n = \frac{\text{Total Investment}}{\text{M.V.}} = \frac{12500}{62.50} = 200 \text{ shares.}$$

2. Dividend (income) on 1 share:

$$\text{Div on 1 share} = 6\% \text{ of } 50 = \frac{6}{100} \times 50 = \text{₹ } 3$$

3. Total Annual Income:

$$\text{Total Annual Income} = n \times \text{Div on 1 share} = 200 \times 3 = \text{₹ } \mathbf{600}$$

Example 3 (Finding Return % using $r \times \text{M.V.} = d \times \text{N.V.}$): Ramesh buys ₹100 shares at ₹20 premium in a company paying 15% dividend. Find (i) M.V. of 600 shares, (ii) Annual income, (iii) Percentage income (Return %).

Solution:

$\text{N.V.} = \text{₹ } 100$, Premium $= \text{₹ } 20 \implies \text{M.V.} = 100 + 20 = \text{₹ } 120$.

(i) M.V. of 600 shares:

$$\text{M.V. of 600 shares} = 600 \times 120 = \text{₹ } \mathbf{72,000}$$

(ii) Annual Income:

$$\text{Annual Income} = 600 \times \left(\frac{15}{100} \times 100\right) = 600 \times 15 = \text{₹ } \mathbf{9,000}$$

(iii) Percentage Income (Return %):

$$\text{Return } \% = \frac{\text{Annual Income}}{\text{Total Investment}} \times 100\% = \frac{9000}{72000} \times 100\% = \mathbf{12.5\%}$$

Direct Shortcut Verification: $r \times 120 = 15 \times 100 \implies r = \frac{1500}{120} = 12.5\%$.

Example 4 (Half-Yearly Dividend Calculation): Find the dividend due at the end of a year on 250 shares of ₹50 each, if the half-yearly dividend is 4% of the value of the share.

Solution:

Half-yearly dividend $= 4\%$. Therefore, annual dividend rate $d = 4\% \times 2 = 8\%$.

Annual dividend on 1 share $= 8\% \text{ of } 50 = \frac{8}{100} \times 50 = \text{₹ } 4$.

$$\text{Total Dividend at year end} = 250 \text{ shares} \times \text{₹ } 4 = \text{₹ } \mathbf{1,000}$$

Example 5 (Comparing Investment Options): Which is a better investment: 12% ₹100 shares at 120 OR 8% ₹100 shares at 90?

Solution:

Case 1: 12% ₹100 shares at 120

$$\text{Return } \% = \frac{d \times \text{N.V.}}{\text{M.V.}} = \frac{12 \times 100}{120} = \mathbf{10\%}$$

Case 2: 8% ₹100 shares at 90

$$\text{Return } \% = \frac{d \times \text{N.V.}}{\text{M.V.}} = \frac{8 \times 100}{90} = \frac{80}{9} = \mathbf{8.89\%}$$

Conclusion: Since 10% > 8.89%, the first investment (12% ₹100 shares at 120) is better.

Example 6 (Selling Shares & Re-investing Proceeds): A man sells 60, ₹15 shares of a company paying 12% dividend at ₹21 each and invests the proceeds in ₹6 shares of another company at ₹9 each paying 8% dividend. Find his change in annual income.

Solution:

Phase 1 (Original Investment):

$$\text{Original Income } D_1 = 60 \times (12\% \text{ of } 15) = 60 \times 1.80 = \text{₹ } 108$$ $$\text{Sale Proceeds} = 60 \text{ shares} \times \text{₹ } 21 = \text{₹ } 1,260$$

Phase 2 (New Investment):

$$\text{New M.V.} = \text{₹ } 9, \quad \text{New N.V.} = \text{₹ } 6, \quad \text{New Dividend Rate} = 8\%$$ $$\text{Number of new shares bought } n_2 = \frac{\text{Proceeds}}{\text{New M.V.}} = \frac{1260}{9} = 140 \text{ shares.}$$ $$\text{New Income } D_2 = 140 \times (8\% \text{ of } 6) = 140 \times 0.48 = \text{₹ } 67.20$$

Change in Income:

$$\text{Decrease in Income} = D_1 - D_2 = 108 - 67.20 = \text{₹ } \mathbf{40.80}$$

Example 7 (Splitting Investment into Two Parts): Divide ₹40,608 into two parts such that if one part is invested in 8% ₹100 shares at 8% discount and the other part in 9% ₹100 shares at 8% premium, the annual incomes from both investments are equal.

Solution:

Let Part 1 $= \text{₹ } x$ and Part 2 $= \text{₹ } (40608 - x)$.

Part 1: $\text{N.V.} = 100, \text{M.V.} = 100 - 8 = 92$, Dividend $= 8\%$.

$$\text{Dividend}_1 = \text{Shares}_1 \times 8 = \frac{x}{92} \times 8 = \frac{2x}{23}$$

Part 2: $\text{N.V.} = 100, \text{M.V.} = 100 + 8 = 108$, Dividend $= 9\%$.

$$\text{Dividend}_2 = \text{Shares}_2 \times 9 = \frac{40608 - x}{108} \times 9 = \frac{40608 - x}{12}$$

Equating $\text{Dividend}_1 = \text{Dividend}_2$:

$$\frac{2x}{23} = \frac{40608 - x}{12} \implies 24x = 23(40608 - x) = 933984 - 23x$$ $$47x = 933984 \implies x = \text{₹ } \mathbf{19,872}$$ $$\text{Part 2} = 40608 - 19872 = \text{₹ } \mathbf{20,736}$$
